## The big idea

Energy and momentum give "before and after" shortcuts that skip the details in between. Work changes kinetic energy; with only gravity and springs, mechanical energy is conserved; friction turns some into heat. Power is the rate of energy flow, in the same watts as $P = VI$. In every collision without external forces, total momentum is conserved; kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic ones.

## Must-know formulas

| Formula | Use it when | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| $W = Fd\cos\theta$ | work by a constant force | Perpendicular forces do zero work. |
| $W = \int F\,dx$ | changing force | Area under $F$–$x$. |
| $KE = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ | kinetic energy | Doubling speed quadruples it. |
| $W_{\text{net}} = \Delta KE$ | speeds from forces and distances | Use the net work. |
| $PE = mgh$; $PE = \tfrac{1}{2}kx^2$ | gravity; springs | Only height changes matter. |
| $KE_1 + PE_1 = KE_2 + PE_2 \,(+E_{\text{lost}})$ | before and after | Add the loss if friction acts. |
| $P = W/t = Fv$ | power | 1 kWh = $3.6 \times 10^6$ J. |
| $\eta = P_{\text{out}}/P_{\text{in}}$ | efficiency | The rest becomes heat. |
| $p = mv$; $F\Delta t = \Delta p$ | momentum and impulse | Vectors: watch signs on bounces. |
| $m_1v_1 + m_2v_2 = m_1v_1' + m_2v_2'$ | collisions, recoil, explosions | Always holds without external forces. |

## How to attack a problem

1. **Speed after a drop, ramp or spring?** Use energy conservation; ignore the path.
2. **Friction over a distance?** Energy lost $= \mu_k mg\,d$.
3. **Collision?** Conserve momentum. If they stick, that's enough; if elastic, also conserve kinetic energy.
4. **Force during an impact?** $F = \Delta p/\Delta t$.

## Traps that cost points

- **Using the full force at an angle:** only $F\cos\theta$ does work.
- **Conserving kinetic energy in sticky collisions.**
- **Bounce momentum change:** a bounce changes momentum by $m(v + v')$, not $mv$.
- **Energy vs power:** joules are an amount, watts are a rate.

## Sanity checks

- **Drop from $h$:** $v = \sqrt{2gh}$ no matter the path or mass.
- **Perfectly inelastic collisions always lose kinetic energy.**
- **Real motors are about 70 to 95% efficient.**

## 60-second self-test

```recall
Q: Coaster starts at rest 20 m high. Speed at ground level?
A: $\sqrt{2 \times 9.8 \times 20} \approx 19.8$ m/s.
Q: Work to stretch a 200 N/m spring by 0.1 m?
A: $\tfrac{1}{2}(200)(0.01) = 1$ J.
Q: 1200 kg car at 20 m/s hits a stopped 1800 kg car; they stick. Speed after?
A: $24000/3000 = 8$ m/s.
Q: Why do airbags reduce injury?
A: Longer stopping time means smaller force for the same $\Delta p$.
Q: How many joules in 1 kWh?
A: $3.6 \times 10^6$ J.
```
