## The big idea

Forces cause changes in motion: $\sum \vec{F} = m\vec{a}$ in each direction. Draw a free-body diagram of one object with every force acting on it, then write Newton's second law along each axis. Friction, tension and the normal force are the everyday forces; on inclines, tilt your axes. Anything moving in a circle needs a net inward force, and gravity provides it for orbits.

## Must-know formulas

| Formula | Use it when | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| $\sum F_x = ma_x$, $\sum F_y = ma_y$ | any force problem | Draw the FBD first. |
| $W = mg$ | weight | Weight is a force (N); mass is kg. |
| $f_s \le \mu_s N$, $f_k = \mu_k N$ | friction | Static friction is only as big as needed. |
| $N = mg\cos\theta$, along slope $mg\sin\theta$ | inclines (nothing else pushing) | sin goes with "along the slope." |
| $\tan\theta_{\text{slip}} = \mu_s$ | when a block starts sliding | Independent of mass. |
| $F_c = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ | circular motion | It's a net force, not an extra force. |
| $v_{\max} = \sqrt{\mu_s g r}$ | flat-curve speed limit | Independent of mass. |
| $F = \dfrac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}$ | gravity between masses | $r$ from center to center. |
| $g = \dfrac{GM}{R^2}$, $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$ | surface gravity, orbital speed | Higher orbits are slower. |

## How to attack a problem

1. **Isolate one object** and draw every force on it: weight, then one contact force per thing touching it.
2. **Choose axes** along the acceleration (tilted for ramps, toward the center for circles).
3. **Write $\sum F = ma$** for each axis.
4. **Several connected objects:** write one equation per object; add them so the tension cancels.

## Traps that cost points

- **Including forces the object exerts on others.**
- **Assuming $N = mg$ everywhere:** not on ramps, in accelerating elevators, or with angled pushes.
- **Drawing a centrifugal force.**
- **Third-law pairs on the same object:** pairs act on different objects.

## Sanity checks

- **Accelerating up feels heavier; free fall feels weightless.**
- **The tension holding a hanging mass that accelerates down is less than its weight.**
- **Ramp acceleration is between 0 and $g$.**

## 60-second self-test

```recall
Q: 70 kg person, elevator accelerating up at 2 m/s². Scale reading?
A: $70(11.8) = 826$ N.
Q: Frictionless 30° ramp. Acceleration?
A: $g\sin 30° = 4.9$ m/s².
Q: $\mu_s = 0.6$. Angle where a block slips?
A: $\tan^{-1}(0.6) \approx 31°$.
Q: Max speed on a 50 m flat curve with $\mu_s = 0.7$?
A: $\sqrt{0.7 \times 9.8 \times 50} \approx 18.5$ m/s.
Q: Why do orbiting astronauts float?
A: They and the station are in free fall together.
```
