## The big idea

Algebra is arithmetic with placeholders. Every move you make, simplifying, expanding, solving, must keep both sides of an equation equal and follow the order of operations. Get these habits right and every later formula becomes a routine rearrangement.

## Must-know formulas

| Formula | Use it when | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| Order: parentheses, exponents, × ÷ (left to right), + − (left to right) | evaluating any expression | × and ÷ have equal rank; so do + and −. Go left to right. |
| $\dfrac{a}{b} + \dfrac{c}{d} = \dfrac{ad + bc}{bd}$ | adding fractions | Never add the bottoms: $\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{3} \ne \tfrac{2}{5}$. |
| $\dfrac{a}{b} \div \dfrac{c}{d} = \dfrac{a}{b} \times \dfrac{d}{c}$ | dividing fractions | Flip the **second** fraction only. |
| $p\%$ of $x = \dfrac{p}{100} x$ | percentages, tolerances | 5% growth is $\times 1.05$, not $\times 0.05$. |
| $a^m a^n = a^{m+n}$, $\dfrac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$, $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$ | simplifying powers | Same base only: $2^3 \cdot 3^2$ doesn't combine. |
| $a^0 = 1$, $a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n}$, $a^{1/2} = \sqrt{a}$ | negative and fractional powers | A negative exponent makes a fraction, not a negative number. |
| k $= 10^3$, M $= 10^6$, m $= 10^{-3}$, µ $= 10^{-6}$, n $= 10^{-9}$ | reading component values | m (milli) and M (mega) are a billion apart. |
| $a(b + c) = ab + ac$ | removing parentheses | Multiply **every** term inside. |
| $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$ | expanding a square | It's never just $a^2 + b^2$. |
| $x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$ | any quadratic $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ | Get zero on one side first; put negatives in parentheses. |

## How to attack a problem

1. **Simplify** each side: clear parentheses, combine like terms.
2. **Collect** the variable terms on one side and plain numbers on the other.
3. **Undo** operations in reverse order: add/subtract first, then multiply/divide, then roots/powers.
4. If there's an $x^2$, **move everything to one side** (equal to zero), then factor or use the quadratic formula.
5. **Check** by substituting your answer back into the original equation.

## Traps that cost points

- **$-3^2 = -9$**, but $(-3)^2 = 9$. The exponent binds tighter than the minus sign.
- **Doing something to only one term.** Dividing $3x + 5 = 20$ by 3 must divide the 5 and the 20 too.
- **Roots don't split over addition:** $\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$, not $7$.
- **Dropping the middle term** when expanding $(x + 5)^2$.
- **Prefix slips:** each step between k, (none), m, µ, n is a factor of 1000; count three decimal places per step.

## Sanity checks

- **Estimate first.** $\tfrac{2}{3} + \tfrac{1}{4}$ is a bit less than 1, so $\tfrac{11}{12}$ is believable.
- **Plug your answer back in**; both sides must match.
- **Units must balance**: volts ÷ ohms gives amps; watts ÷ volts gives amps.
- **Quadratics:** the two solutions add up to $-\tfrac{b}{a}$. For $x^2 - x - 6$: $3 + (-2) = 1$. ✓

## 60-second self-test

```recall
Q: What is $20 \div 4 \times 5$?
A: $25$. Equal-rank operations go left to right: $20 \div 4 = 5$, then $5 \times 5$.
Q: What is $\tfrac{3}{4} \div \tfrac{1}{2}$?
A: $\tfrac{3}{4} \times \tfrac{2}{1} = \tfrac{3}{2} = 1.5$.
Q: How many ohms is 2.2 kΩ, and how many amps is 15 mA?
A: $2200\ \Omega$ and $0.015$ A.
Q: Solve $P = I^2R$ for $I$.
A: $I = \sqrt{P/R}$.
Q: How many real solutions does $x^2 + x + 1 = 0$ have?
A: None: the discriminant $1 - 4 = -3$ is negative.
```
