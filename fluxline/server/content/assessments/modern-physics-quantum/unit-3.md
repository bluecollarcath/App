Covers Lessons 3.1–3.3: quantum tunneling and its uses; the hydrogen atom, quantum numbers and orbitals; electron spin, the Pauli exclusion principle and the periodic table. For electrons, use $T \approx e^{-2\kappa L}$ with $\kappa = \sqrt{(V_0 - E)/0.0381}$ nm⁻¹.

```quiz
? An electron meets a barrier 0.5 eV above its energy and 0.8 nm thick. What is the approximate tunneling probability? (Give it in units of $10^{-3}$.)
= 3.039 | 0.02 | × 10⁻³
> $\kappa = \sqrt{0.5/0.0381} = 3.62$ nm⁻¹, $2\kappa L = 5.80$, so $T \approx e^{-5.80} = 3.0 \times 10^{-3}$.
```

```quiz
? An electron faces a 1.0 eV barrier that is 1.5 nm thick. What is the approximate tunneling probability? (Give it in units of $10^{-7}$.)
= 2.114 | 0.02 | × 10⁻⁷
> $2\kappa L = 2(5.123)(1.5) = 15.37$, so $T \approx e^{-15.37} = 2.1 \times 10^{-7}$.
```

```quiz
? For a 1.0 eV barrier, by what factor does the tunneling probability drop when the barrier goes from 1.0 nm to 1.2 nm thick?
= 7.762 | 0.02 | ×
> The ratio is $e^{2\kappa(0.2)} = e^{2(5.123)(0.2)} = e^{2.05} \approx 7.8$.
```

```quiz
? An STM faces a 4.0 eV barrier. By about what factor does the current grow if the tip moves 0.05 nm closer?
= 2.786 | 0.02 | ×
> $\kappa = \sqrt{4.0/0.0381} = 10.25$ nm⁻¹; factor $e^{2(10.25)(0.05)} = e^{1.025} \approx 2.8$.
```

```quiz
? How thick must a 2.0 eV barrier be for the tunneling probability to drop to $10^{-6}$, in nm?
= 0.9534 | 0.01 | nm
> $2\kappa L = \ln(10^6) = 13.82$, with $\kappa = \sqrt{2.0/0.0381} = 7.245$ nm⁻¹, so $L = 13.82/14.49 = 0.953$ nm.
```

```quiz
? Which device relies on electrons tunneling through an insulating oxide to store data?
+ Flash memory
- A resistor
- A capacitor in a power supply
- A copper wire
> Flash cells write by tunneling electrons onto a floating gate through a thin oxide.
```

```quiz
? How many orbitals (not counting spin) are in hydrogen's $n = 3$ level?
= 9 | 0.001 |
> $n^2 = 9$: one 3s, three 3p and five 3d.
```

```quiz
? What are the allowed values of $m_l$ for a p orbital?
+ $-1, 0, +1$
- $0, 1$
- $1, 2, 3$
- $-2, -1, 0, 1, 2$
> A p orbital has $l = 1$, so $m_l$ runs from $-1$ to $+1$.
```


```quiz

= 63.87 | 0.003 | MHz
> $42.58 \times 1.5 = 63.9$ MHz.
```

```quiz
? Without the Pauli exclusion principle, what would happen to atoms?
+ All electrons would crowd into the lowest orbital, and chemistry as we know it wouldn't exist.
- Atoms would fly apart.
- Electrons would have no spin.
- Atoms would emit no light.
> Exclusion forces electrons into successively higher orbitals, which gives each element its outer electrons and its chemistry.
```
