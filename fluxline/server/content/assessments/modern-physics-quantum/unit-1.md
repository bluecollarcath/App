Covers Lessons 1.1–1.3: photons and the photoelectric effect, the electron-volt, de Broglie wavelengths and electron diffraction, the double-slit experiment, and atomic energy levels and spectra. Use $E\ (\text{eV}) = 1240/\lambda\ (\text{nm})$ for photons and $\lambda = 1.226/\sqrt{V}$ nm for electrons.

```quiz
? What is the energy of a 480 nm blue photon, in eV?
= 2.583 | 0.005 | eV
> $E = 1240/480 = 2.58$ eV.
```

```quiz
? A photon has an energy of 3.0 eV. What is its wavelength, in nm?
= 413.3 | 0.005 | nm
> $\lambda = 1240/3.0 = 413$ nm, violet.
```

```quiz
? Sodium has a work function of 2.28 eV. Ultraviolet light at 300 nm shines on it. What is the maximum kinetic energy of the ejected electrons, in eV?
= 1.853 | 0.01 | eV
> Photon: $1240/300 = 4.13$ eV. $KE_{\max} = 4.13 - 2.28 = 1.85$ eV.
```

```quiz
? A metal has a work function of 2.0 eV. What is the longest wavelength that ejects electrons, in nm?
= 620 | 0.005 | nm
> $\lambda = 1240/2.0 = 620$ nm.
```

```quiz
? In a photoelectric experiment, you switch to a dimmer light of the same color. What happens?
+ Fewer electrons are ejected, but their maximum energy stays the same.
- The electrons' maximum energy decreases.
- No electrons are ejected until the light builds up enough energy.
- The work function decreases.
> Dimmer means fewer photons; each photon still has the same energy, so each electron's maximum energy is unchanged.
```

```quiz
? A 10 mW violet laser emits 405 nm light. How many photons does it emit per second? (Give the answer in units of $10^{16}$.)
= 2.039 | 0.01 | × 10¹⁶
> One photon: $1240/405 = 3.062$ eV $= 4.905 \times 10^{-19}$ J. Then $0.010/4.905 \times 10^{-19} = 2.04 \times 10^{16}$ per second.
```

```quiz
? What is the de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving at $3.0 \times 10^6$ m/s, in nm?
= 0.2425 | 0.005 | nm
> $\lambda = \dfrac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{(9.109 \times 10^{-31})(3.0 \times 10^6)} = 0.242$ nm.
```

```quiz
? An electron is accelerated from rest through 25 V. What is its wavelength, in nm?
= 0.2453 | 0.005 | nm
> $\lambda = 1.226/\sqrt{25} = 0.245$ nm.
```

```quiz
? A proton ($m = 1.673 \times 10^{-27}$ kg) and an electron move at the same speed. Which has the longer de Broglie wavelength?
+ The electron, because it has much less momentum.
- The proton, because it is heavier.
- They have the same wavelength.
- Neither has a wavelength.
> $\lambda = h/(mv)$; at the same speed, the lighter particle has less momentum and a longer wavelength (about 1836 times longer).
```

```quiz
? Blue light (450 nm) passes through two slits 0.20 mm apart onto a screen 1.5 m away. What is the spacing between bright fringes, in mm?
= 3.375 | 0.005 | mm
> $\Delta y = \dfrac{\lambda L}{d} = \dfrac{(450 \times 10^{-9})(1.5)}{0.20 \times 10^{-3}} = 3.38$ mm.
```

```quiz
? A hydrogen electron drops from $n = 5$ to $n = 2$. What is the wavelength of the emitted photon, in nm?
= 434.1 | 0.005 | nm
> $\Delta E = -0.544 - (-3.40) = 2.856$ eV, so $\lambda = 1240/2.856 = 434$ nm (violet).
```

```quiz
? What is the energy of hydrogen's $n = 4$ level, in eV?
= -0.85 | 0.005 | eV
> $E_4 = -13.6/16 = -0.85$ eV.
```

```quiz
? How much energy is needed to ionize a hydrogen atom whose electron is in the $n = 3$ level, in eV?
= 1.511 | 0.005 | eV
> Lift it from $-13.6/9 = -1.51$ eV to 0: 1.51 eV.
```

```quiz
? Why does a cool gas in front of a hot, glowing source produce dark lines in the spectrum?
+ The gas atoms absorb only photons whose energies match gaps between their energy levels.
- The gas blocks all light equally.
- The gas emits extra light at those wavelengths.
- Cool gases absorb only infrared light.
> Atoms absorb exactly the photon energies that lift electrons between levels, so those wavelengths are missing.
```
