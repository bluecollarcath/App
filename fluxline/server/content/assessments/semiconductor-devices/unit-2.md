Covers Lessons 2.1–2.3: the p–n junction, the ideal diode equation, breakdown, capacitance and metal–semiconductor contacts. Use $n_i = 10^{10}$ cm⁻³, $kT/q = 0.0259$ V, $\varepsilon_s = 1.036\times10^{-12}$ F/cm.

```quiz
? What is the built-in potential of a junction with $N_A = 10^{18}$ and $N_D = 10^{15}$ cm⁻³, in V?
= 0.7753 | 0.005 | V
> $0.0259\ln(10^{33}/10^{20})$.
```

```quiz
? A one-sided $p^+$–$n$ junction has $N_D = 10^{15}$ cm⁻³ and $V_{bi} = 0.8$ V. What is its zero-bias depletion width, in µm?
= 1.018 | 0.005 | µm
> $W \approx \sqrt{2\varepsilon_sV_{bi}/qN_D} = 1.02\ \mu$m.
```

```quiz
? A junction with $C_{j0} = 2$ pF and $V_{bi} = 0.8$ V is reverse biased at 5 V. What is $C_j$, in pF?
= 0.7428 | 0.005 | pF
> $2/\sqrt{1 + 5/0.8} = 0.743$ pF.
```

```quiz
? A diode's forward voltage is 0.70 V at 1 mA. What is it at 100 mA (ideal, 300 K), in V?
= 0.8193 | 0.002 | V
> $0.7 + 0.0259\ln100 = 0.819$ V.
```
