Covers Lessons 3.1–3.3: BJT physics, the MOS capacitor, and MOSFET physics and scaling. Use $kT/q = 0.0259$ V and $n_i = 10^{10}$ cm⁻³.

```quiz
? A BJT has $\gamma = 0.99$ and $\alpha_T = 0.998$. What is $\beta$?
= 82.47 | 0.01 |
> $\alpha = 0.98802$; $\beta = \alpha/(1 - \alpha) = 82.5$.
```

```quiz
? A base 0.5 µm wide has $L_n = 5\ \mu$m. What is the base transport factor?
= 0.995 | 0.001 |
> $1 - 0.25/50$.
```

```quiz
? For $N_A = 5\times10^{16}$ cm⁻³, what is $2\phi_F$, in V?
= 0.799 | 0.005 | V
> $2(0.0259)\ln(5\times10^6)$.
```

```quiz
? What is $C_{ox}$ for a 3 nm SiO₂ gate oxide, in µF/cm²?
= 1.151 | 0.005 | µF/cm²
> $3.9 \times 8.854\times10^{-14}/3\times10^{-7}$.
```

```quiz
? $\mu_nC_{ox} = 300\ \mu$A/V², $W/L = 4$ and overdrive 0.3 V. What is the saturation current, in µA?
= 54 | 0.001 | µA
> $\tfrac12(300)(4)(0.09)$.
```

```quiz
? A transistor's off-current must be $10^5$ times below its threshold current, with $S = 60$ mV/dec. How far below threshold must $V_{GS}$ be, in V?
= 0.3 | 0.001 | V
> Five decades at 60 mV each.
```

```quiz
? Why did supply voltages stop scaling down in the 2000s?
+ Threshold voltages couldn't fall further without unacceptable subthreshold leakage
- Gate oxides could no longer be made thinner
- Transistors became too slow
- Interconnects stopped scaling
> The 60 mV/dec subthreshold limit fixes how far below $V_T$ "off" must be.
```
