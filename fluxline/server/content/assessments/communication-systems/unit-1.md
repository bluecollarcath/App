Covers Lessons 1.1–1.3: amplitude modulation, angle modulation, and superheterodyne receivers.

```quiz
? What fraction of total power is in the sidebands of single-tone AM with $\mu = 0.5$?
= 0.1111 | 0.002 |
> $\mu^2/(2 + \mu^2) = 0.25/2.25$.
```

```quiz
? An AM signal has a 1 MHz carrier and a 5 kHz message. What bandwidth does it occupy, in kHz?
= 10 | 0.001 | kHz
> Two sidebands of 5 kHz each.
```

```quiz
? An FM signal has 25 kHz peak deviation and a 5 kHz message. What is its Carson's-rule bandwidth, in kHz?
= 60 | 0.001 | kHz
> $2(25 + 5) = 60$ kHz.
```

```quiz
? An FM receiver with a 10.7 MHz IF and high-side LO is tuned to 90 MHz. What is the image frequency, in MHz?
= 111.4 | 0.001 | MHz
> $90 + 2(10.7)$.
```

```quiz
? An LNA (NF 2 dB, gain 15 dB) feeds a mixer with NF 5 dB. What is the cascade noise figure, in dB?
= 2.184 | 0.01 | dB
> $F = 1.585 + (3.162 - 1)/31.62 = 1.653$, or 2.18 dB.
```

```quiz
? Why does FM tolerate amplitude noise better than AM?
+ The message is carried in the frequency, so a limiter can strip amplitude variations
- FM uses less bandwidth
- FM signals have higher power
- FM uses a lower carrier frequency
> Amplitude noise doesn't change the zero crossings that carry the message.
```
