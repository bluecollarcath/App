Covers Lessons 5.1–5.2: average and instantaneous rates of change, limits, secant and tangent lines; accumulation as area, rectangle estimates, charge and energy, and the link between rates and totals.

```quiz
? What is the average rate of change of $f(x) = x^3$ from $x = 1$ to $x = 2$?
= 7 | 0.001 |
> $\dfrac{8 - 1}{2 - 1} = 7$.
```

```quiz
? What is the average rate of change of $f(x) = x^2$ from $x = 2$ to $x = 2.1$?
= 4.1 | 0.001 |
> $\dfrac{4.41 - 4}{0.1} = 4.1$.
```

```quiz
? What is the instantaneous rate of change of $f(x) = x^2$ at $x = 2$?
= 4 | 0.001 |
> $\dfrac{(2 + h)^2 - 4}{h} = \dfrac{4h + h^2}{h} = 4 + h \to 4$.
```

```quiz
? What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^2 - 4}{x - 2}$?
= 4 | 0.001 |
> Factor: $\dfrac{(x - 2)(x + 2)}{x - 2} = x + 2 \to 4$.
```

```quiz
? What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x^2 + 3x}{x}$?
= 3 | 0.001 |
> Factor out $x$: $\dfrac{x(x + 3)}{x} = x + 3 \to 3$.
```

```quiz
? A ball falls $d(t) = 5t^2$ meters. What is its average speed from $t = 2$ to $t = 4$ s, in m/s?
= 30 | 0.001 | m/s
> $\dfrac{80 - 20}{4 - 2} = 30$ m/s.
```

```quiz
? An object moves at a constant 4 m/s for 6 seconds. How far does it travel, in m?
= 24 | 0.001 | m
> The area of the rectangle under the speed graph: $4 \times 6 = 24$ m.
```

```quiz
? A current rises steadily from 0 to 6 A over 3 seconds. How much charge flows, in C?
= 9 | 0.001 | C
> Triangle under the current graph: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 3 \times 6 = 9$ C.
```

```quiz
? Estimate the area under $v(t) = 2t$ from 0 to 4 with 4 slices of width 1, using the value at the **start** of each slice.
= 12 | 0.001 |
> $(0 + 2 + 4 + 6) \times 1 = 12$, below the true area of 16 (a triangle, $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 8$).
```

```quiz
? Which statement describes the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus?
+ Finding a rate of change and finding an accumulated total undo each other
- Every function is a straight line when you zoom in far enough
- The area under any graph is a triangle
- Limits always equal the value you get by plugging in
> Rate (derivative) and total (integral) are inverse operations, like multiplication and division.
```
