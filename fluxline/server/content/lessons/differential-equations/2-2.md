## Adding a driving source

A second-order system with an input, $ay'' + by' + cy = f(t)$, has a complete solution made of two parts:

$$
y = y_h + y_p
$$

- the **homogeneous** (natural) solution $y_h$ from Lesson 2.1, which carries the two constants and dies away in a stable system;
- one **particular** (forced) solution $y_p$, which follows the input and remains.

Find $y_p$ first, then use the initial conditions on the **total** $y$ to set the constants.

## Undetermined coefficients

When the forcing is a polynomial, exponential, sinusoid, or sums and products of these, guess a $y_p$ of the same form with unknown coefficients:

| Forcing $f(t)$ | Guess $y_p$ |
|---|---|
| constant $K$ | $A$ |
| $e^{at}$ | $Ae^{at}$ |
| $\cos\omega t$ or $\sin\omega t$ | $A\cos\omega t + B\sin\omega t$ |
| polynomial of degree $n$ | general polynomial of degree $n$ |

Substitute and match coefficients.

> **DC input.** $y'' + 3y' + 2y = 4$: guess $y_p = A$, so $2A = 4$ and $y_p = 2$. With $y(0) = 0$, $y'(0) = 0$: $y = 2 - 4e^{-t} + 2e^{-2t}$. The step response of an overdamped second-order system.

> **Sinusoidal input.** $y'' + 3y' + 2y = 10\cos t$: guess $y_p = A\cos t + B\sin t$. Substituting:
>
> $$
> (A + 3B)\cos t + (B - 3A)\sin t = 10\cos t
> $$
>
> so $A + 3B = 10$ and $B = 3A$, giving $A = 1$, $B = 3$. The steady state is $\cos t + 3\sin t = \sqrt{10}\cos(t - 71.6°)$: the same frequency as the input, with a new amplitude and phase. Phasors (Circuit Analysis II) get this answer directly with complex algebra.

**The resonance exception.** If the guess already solves the homogeneous equation (the forcing frequency matches a natural mode), multiply the guess by $t$. For $y'' + y = \cos t$, the particular solution is $\tfrac12t\sin t$: an oscillation that grows without bound. That's undamped resonance.

## Variation of parameters

For forcing that doesn't fit the table ($\tan t$, $1/t$, measured data), **variation of parameters** constructs $y_p$ from the homogeneous solutions $y_1, y_2$ with an integral formula:

$$
y_p = -y_1\int\frac{y_2f}{aW}\,dt + y_2\int\frac{y_1f}{aW}\,dt, \qquad W = y_1y_2' - y_1'y_2
$$

In practice, engineers handle arbitrary inputs with the **Laplace transform** and **convolution** (Unit 3), which package this idea neatly.

## Key takeaways

- Complete solution = homogeneous (natural, decays) + particular (forced, remains).
- Undetermined coefficients: guess the form of the forcing; multiply by $t$ at resonance.
- Sinusoidal steady state has the input frequency with new amplitude and phase (phasors find it directly).
- Variation of parameters handles general forcing; Laplace and convolution do it systematically.
