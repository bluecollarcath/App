## Many variables at once

Real systems have several interacting state variables: the capacitor voltages and inductor currents of a circuit, the angles and speeds of coupled generators, the positions and velocities of a robot arm. Writing one first-order equation per state gives a **linear system**:

$$
\dot{\mathbf x} = A\mathbf x + B\mathbf u
$$

Any $n$th-order equation can be put in this form. For $y'' + 3y' + 2y = 0$, choose $x_1 = y$ and $x_2 = y'$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\dot x_1 \\ \dot x_2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -2 & -3\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2\end{bmatrix}
$$

## The eigenvalue method

Try $\mathbf x = \mathbf ve^{\lambda t}$ in $\dot{\mathbf x} = A\mathbf x$: you get $A\mathbf v = \lambda\mathbf v$, the eigenvalue problem (Linear Algebra, Lesson 3.2). With $n$ independent eigenvectors, the general solution is a sum of **modes**:

$$
\mathbf x(t) = c_1\mathbf v_1e^{\lambda_1t} + c_2\mathbf v_2e^{\lambda_2t} + \cdots
$$

with the constants set by $\mathbf x(0)$. The eigenvalues are the roots of the characteristic equation (here $\lambda^2 + 3\lambda + 2 = 0$, so $-1$ and $-2$), the same numbers as the poles of the transfer function.

> **Coupled tanks** (or two RC sections in cascade): $\dot{\mathbf x} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 & 1 \\ 1 & -2\end{bmatrix}\mathbf x$. Eigenvalues $-1$ (eigenvector $(1, 1)$) and $-3$ (eigenvector $(1, -1)$). The "together" mode decays slowly; the "difference" mode, where the levels equalize, decays three times faster. From $\mathbf x(0) = (2, 0) = (1, 1) + (1, -1)$:
> $$\mathbf x(t) = e^{-t}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + e^{-3t}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}$$

Complex eigenvalues $\alpha \pm j\beta$ produce real solutions with $e^{\alpha t}\cos\beta t$ and $e^{\alpha t}\sin\beta t$: oscillation.

## Phase portraits

For two states, plot trajectories in the $(x_1, x_2)$ **phase plane**. The eigenvalues fix the picture near the equilibrium at the origin:

| Eigenvalues | Portrait | Stability |
|---|---|---|
| real, both negative | **stable node**: trajectories flow straight in | stable |
| real, both positive | unstable node | unstable |
| real, opposite signs | **saddle**: in along one eigenvector, out along the other | unstable |
| complex, negative real part | **stable spiral**: damped oscillation | stable |
| complex, positive real part | unstable spiral | unstable |
| purely imaginary | **center**: closed orbits (lossless LC) | marginal |

A quick classification uses the **trace** $T$ and **determinant** $D$ of $A$: stable iff $T < 0$ and $D > 0$; a saddle iff $D < 0$; spirals when $T^2 < 4D$.

The same method extends to **nonlinear** systems: linearize around each equilibrium (the Jacobian matrix plays the role of $A$) to see whether it's a stable node, a spiral or a saddle. That's how power-system stability, oscillators and latch circuits are analyzed.

## Key takeaways

- Write any linear ODE system as $\dot{\mathbf x} = A\mathbf x + B\mathbf u$ (one state per energy store).
- Solutions are sums of modes $c_i\mathbf v_ie^{\lambda_it}$; eigenvalues = poles.
- Phase portraits: nodes, saddles, spirals, centers; stable iff trace < 0 and determinant > 0.
- Linearize nonlinear systems with the Jacobian to classify their equilibria.
