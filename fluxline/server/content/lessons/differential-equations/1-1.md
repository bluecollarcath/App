## The model

Charge a capacitor $C$ from a source $v_s(t)$ through a resistor $R$. KVL gives

$$
RC\frac{dv}{dt} + v = v_s(t)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{dv}{dt} + \frac{1}{RC}\,v = \frac{1}{RC}\,v_s(t)
$$

This is a **first-order linear ODE** in standard form:

$$
y' + p(t)\,y = q(t)
$$

## The integrating factor method

Multiply both sides by $\mu(t) = e^{\int p\,dt}$. The left side becomes an exact derivative:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\big[\mu(t)\,y\big] = \mu(t)\,q(t)
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
y(t) = \frac{1}{\mu(t)}\left[\int \mu(t)\,q(t)\,dt + K\right]
$$

## RC step response, derived

With constant $p = 1/\tau$ ($\tau = RC$) and a step input $v_s = V_s$:

$\mu = e^{t/\tau}$, so

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\left[e^{t/\tau} v\right] = \frac{V_s}{\tau}e^{t/\tau}
\;\Rightarrow\;
e^{t/\tau}v = V_s e^{t/\tau} + K
\;\Rightarrow\;
v(t) = V_s + K e^{-t/\tau}
$$

Applying $v(0) = V_0$ gives $K = V_0 - V_s$:

$$
\boxed{v(t) = V_s + (V_0 - V_s)\,e^{-t/\tau}}
$$

This is the "final + (initial − final)·e^{−t/τ}" formula used throughout circuit analysis — now you know where it comes from.

## Natural and forced response

The solution always splits into

- a **natural (homogeneous) response** $Ke^{-t/\tau}$, determined by the circuit alone, which decays, and
- a **forced (particular) response**, which has the shape of the input.

For a sinusoidal input $v_s = V_m\cos\omega t$, the forced response is a sinusoid at the same frequency with a different amplitude and phase. Finding that amplitude and phase efficiently is exactly what *phasor analysis* does.

## Key takeaways

- First-order linear ODE: $y' + p y = q$; solve with $\mu = e^{\int p\,dt}$.
- RC/RL step responses are just this equation with constant coefficients.
- Solution = decaying natural response + forced response shaped like the input.
