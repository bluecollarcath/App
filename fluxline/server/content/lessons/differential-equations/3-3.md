## Idealized inputs

Two idealized signals let us describe a linear system completely.

**The unit step** $u(t)$: 0 for $t < 0$, 1 for $t \ge 0$. It models switching something on. A delayed step $u(t - T)$ switches on at $t = T$, and pulses are differences of steps: a 2-second pulse of height $A$ starting at 1 s is $A[u(t - 1) - u(t - 3)]$.

**The unit impulse** $\delta(t)$: an infinitely tall, infinitely narrow spike with **unit area**. It models a sudden kick (a hammer blow, a lightning strike, a very short current pulse). Its defining property is **sifting**:

$$
\int_{-\infty}^\infty f(t)\,\delta(t - T)\,dt = f(T)
$$

The step and impulse are related by $\delta(t) = \dfrac{du}{dt}$, and their transforms are $\mathcal L\{u\} = 1/s$, $\mathcal L\{\delta\} = 1$.

## The transfer function and impulse response

For a linear system starting at rest, transforming the ODE gives $Y(s) = H(s)X(s)$, where the **transfer function** $H(s)$ is the ratio of output to input transforms.

> For $\tau y' + y = x$: $H(s) = \dfrac{1}{\tau s + 1}$.

Feed in an impulse ($X = 1$) and the output is $H(s)$ itself. So the **impulse response** $h(t) = \mathcal L^{-1}\{H(s)\}$ completely characterizes the system.

> For the RC lowpass, $h(t) = \dfrac1\tau e^{-t/\tau}$: a jump to $1/\tau$, then decay. The **step response** is its integral, $1 - e^{-t/\tau}$ (the transform of $H(s)/s$).

## Convolution

Any input can be viewed as a train of scaled, shifted impulses. A linear time-invariant system responds to each with a scaled, shifted copy of $h$, and adds them up. The result is **convolution**:

$$
y(t) = (h * x)(t) = \int_0^th(\tau)\,x(t - \tau)\,d\tau
$$

The **convolution theorem** says this messy integral becomes simple multiplication in the Laplace domain:

$$
\mathcal L\{h * x\} = H(s)X(s)
$$

> **Example.** $e^{-t} * e^{-2t}$: in the $s$ domain, $\dfrac{1}{(s + 1)(s + 2)} = \dfrac{1}{s + 1} - \dfrac{1}{s + 2}$, so the convolution is $e^{-t} - e^{-2t}$. Checking with the integral: $\int_0^te^{-\tau}e^{-2(t - \tau)}d\tau = e^{-2t}(e^t - 1) = e^{-t} - e^{-2t}$. ✓

## Why it matters

- **Measuring a system:** hit it with a short impulse (or a step, then differentiate) to find $h(t)$; then you can predict its response to anything. Acoustic engineers measure a concert hall's impulse response and use convolution to add its reverb to any recording.
- **Switched circuits:** a source that turns on at $t = T$ is a delayed step, handled with the $e^{-sT}$ delay property.
- **Digital filters** are discrete convolutions (Signals & Systems, DSP).

## Key takeaways

- Step $u(t) \leftrightarrow 1/s$; impulse $\delta(t) \leftrightarrow 1$; sifting picks out $f(T)$.
- The impulse response $h = \mathcal L^{-1}\{H\}$ fully characterizes an LTI system.
- Output = input convolved with $h$; in the $s$ domain, $Y = HX$.
