## Two energy-storage elements

A circuit with an inductor and a capacitor, a mass on a spring, a motor with inertia and a compliant shaft: systems with two independent ways to store energy are described by **second-order** equations. The simplest, and most important, have constant coefficients:

$$
ay'' + by' + cy = 0
$$

## The characteristic equation

Try $y = e^{st}$. Each derivative brings down a factor of $s$, so

$$
as^2 + bs + c = 0
$$

Its two roots determine everything about the natural response. Three cases, set by the discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$:

| Roots | Case | Natural response |
|---|---|---|
| real, distinct $s_1, s_2$ | **overdamped** | $c_1e^{s_1t} + c_2e^{s_2t}$ |
| real, repeated $s$ | **critically damped** | $(c_1 + c_2t)e^{st}$ |
| complex $\alpha \pm j\omega_d$ | **underdamped** | $e^{\alpha t}(c_1\cos\omega_dt + c_2\sin\omega_dt)$ |

The complex case comes from Euler's formula (Calculus, Lesson 3.3): $e^{(\alpha + j\omega_d)t} = e^{\alpha t}(\cos\omega_dt + j\sin\omega_dt)$, and real combinations of the conjugate pair give cosines and sines. The two constants are set by the **initial conditions** $y(0)$ and $y'(0)$.

> **Overdamped.** $y'' + 5y' + 6y = 0$, $y(0) = 1$, $y'(0) = 0$: roots $-2$ and $-3$. From $c_1 + c_2 = 1$ and $-2c_1 - 3c_2 = 0$: $c_1 = 3$, $c_2 = -2$, so $y = 3e^{-2t} - 2e^{-3t}$.

> **Critically damped.** $y'' + 4y' + 4y = 0$: double root $-2$; with the same initial conditions, $y = (1 + 2t)e^{-2t}$.

> **Underdamped.** $y'' + 2y' + 5y = 0$: $s = -1 \pm j2$; with the same initial conditions, $y = e^{-t}(\cos2t + \tfrac12\sin2t)$.

```plot
x: 0, 5, t
y: -0.3, 1.1, y(t)
f: 3*exp(-2*x) - 2*exp(-3*x) | overdamped
f: (1 + 2*x)*exp(-2*x) | critically damped | dash
f: exp(-x)*(cos(2*x) + 0.5*sin(2*x)) | underdamped | dot
caption: Natural responses from $y(0) = 1$, $y'(0) = 0$ for the three examples. The overdamped and critically damped cases settle without overshoot; the underdamped case overshoots and oscillates as it decays. (For a fixed natural frequency, critical damping is the fastest response without overshoot.)
```

## Stability

All solutions decay to zero if and only if **both roots have negative real parts**, which for a second-order equation with $a > 0$ means $b > 0$ and $c > 0$. A negative $b$ (negative damping, from an amplifier's positive feedback) makes oscillations grow: that's how oscillators start (Microelectronics II, Lesson 4.2).

## The series RLC circuit

KVL around a source-free series RLC gives

$$
L\frac{d^2i}{dt^2} + R\frac{di}{dt} + \frac1Ci = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad s^2 + \frac RLs + \frac{1}{LC} = 0
$$

It's underdamped when $R < 2\sqrt{L/C}$, critically damped at equality, and overdamped above. Lesson 2.3 develops this into the language of $\omega_0$, $\zeta$ and $Q$.

## Key takeaways

- Try $e^{st}$: the characteristic equation $as^2 + bs + c = 0$ gives the natural response.
- Distinct real roots: overdamped; repeated: critically damped ($te^{st}$ term); complex: underdamped oscillation.
- Stable iff both roots have negative real parts ($a, b, c > 0$).
- Series RLC: critically damped at $R = 2\sqrt{L/C}$.
