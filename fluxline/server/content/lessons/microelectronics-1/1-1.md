## The diode: a one-way valve

A **p–n junction diode** conducts readily when forward biased (anode positive relative to cathode) and blocks current when reverse biased. That nonlinearity is the basis for rectifiers, protection circuits, logic, mixers, and every transistor.

## The Shockley equation

$$
i_D = I_S\left(e^{v_D/(nV_T)} - 1\right)
$$

- $I_S$: saturation current, ~$10^{-15}$–$10^{-12}$ A for small silicon diodes (strongly temperature dependent).
- $V_T = kT/q \approx 25.9$ mV at 300 K: the thermal voltage.
- $n$: ideality factor, 1–2 (we often take $n=1$).

Two useful consequences for $v_D \gg V_T$:

$$
v_D = nV_T\ln\frac{i_D}{I_S},
\qquad
\Delta v_D = nV_T\ln\frac{I_2}{I_1}
$$

A **10× change in current changes the diode voltage by only about 60 mV** ($n=1$). That is why a conducting silicon diode sits near 0.6–0.7 V over a wide range of currents. The forward voltage also falls about **2 mV/°C**.

## Models, from accurate to simple

1. **Exponential model** — exact but requires iteration or a numerical solver.
2. **Constant-voltage-drop (CVD) model** — on: a 0.7 V battery; off: open circuit. The workhorse for hand analysis.
3. **Ideal diode** — on: short; off: open. Good when supply voltages are much larger than 0.7 V.
4. **Small-signal model** — around a bias point, the diode is a resistor $r_d = nV_T/I_D$.

## Analyzing a diode circuit

With the CVD model: **assume** a state, solve, and **check** for consistency (on ⇒ $i_D > 0$; off ⇒ $v_D < 0.7$ V).

> **Worked example.** 5 V source, 1 kΩ resistor, silicon diode in series.
>
> ```circuit
> V 0,0 0,2 "5 V" side=-1
> R 0,0 2.4,0 "1 kΩ" i=I_D
> D 2.4,0 2.4,2 v=V_D
> W 2.4,2 0,2
> ```
>
> Assume on: $I_D = (5 - 0.7)/1000 = 4.3$ mA > 0. ✓
>
> **Refine with the exponential model** ($I_S = 10^{-14}$ A): $V_D = 0.02585\ln(4.3\times10^{-3}/10^{-14}) \approx 0.693$ V. Recomputing gives $I_D \approx 4.31$ mA — the CVD model was within 0.2%.
>
> **Small-signal resistance** at this bias: $r_d = 25.85\text{ mV}/4.3\text{ mA} \approx 6.0\ \Omega$.

## Reverse breakdown

Beyond a reverse voltage $V_{BR}$, the diode conducts heavily (avalanche or Zener breakdown). Rectifier diodes must have $V_{BR}$ above the circuit's peak reverse voltage; **Zener diodes** are designed to operate in breakdown as voltage references (Lesson 1.3).

## Key takeaways

- $i_D = I_S(e^{v_D/nV_T} - 1)$; ~60 mV per decade of current.
- Use the 0.7 V CVD model for hand analysis; assume, solve, check.
- Small-signal resistance $r_d = nV_T/I_D$.
