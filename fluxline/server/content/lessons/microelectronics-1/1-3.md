## Zener diodes: breakdown on purpose

Every diode breaks down at a large enough reverse voltage. A **Zener diode** is built to break down sharply at a specified **Zener voltage** $V_Z$ (common values: 3.3, 5.1, 12 V) and to survive it. In breakdown its voltage stays nearly constant over a wide range of current, which makes it a simple voltage reference and regulator.

The model in breakdown: a battery $V_Z$ in series with a small **Zener resistance** $r_z$ (a few ohms). The diode must carry at least a minimum current $I_{ZK}$ (the "knee") to stay in breakdown, and less than its power rating allows: $P_Z = V_ZI_Z$.

## The Zener shunt regulator

```circuit
V 0,0 0,2 V_S side=-1
R 0,0 2.2,0 R_S i=I_S
D 2.2,2 2.2,0 "D_Z 5.1 V"
W 2.2,0 3.8,0
R 3.8,0 3.8,2 R_L v=V_O
W 0,2 2.2,2
W 2.2,2 3.8,2
caption: A Zener shunt regulator. $R_S$ drops the difference between $V_S$ and $V_Z$; the Zener absorbs whatever current the load doesn't take.
```

The Zener holds $V_O = V_Z$. By KCL, $I_S = I_Z + I_L$:

$$
I_S = \frac{V_S - V_Z}{R_S}, \qquad I_L = \frac{V_Z}{R_L}, \qquad I_Z = I_S - I_L
$$

> **Worked example.** $V_S = 12$ V, $R_S = 220\ \Omega$, $V_Z = 5.1$ V, $R_L = 510\ \Omega$.
>
> $I_S = (12 - 5.1)/220 = 31.4$ mA. $\quad I_L = 5.1/510 = 10$ mA. $\quad I_Z = 21.4$ mA.
>
> $P_Z = 5.1 \times 21.4\ \text{mA} = 0.109$ W.
>
> **Worst case:** if the load is disconnected, the Zener takes all 31.4 mA and dissipates 0.16 W. Choose a Zener rated well above that, and $R_S$ small enough that $I_Z$ stays above $I_{ZK}$ at maximum load and minimum $V_S$.

Zener regulators waste power and handle only small loads. Larger supplies use IC regulators (like the 7805 or LM317), which use a Zener-like reference plus a transistor and feedback.

## Clippers (limiters)

A diode and a resistor can cut off part of a waveform. With the output taken across a diode, the output can't rise above about 0.7 V. Add a battery $V_B$ in series with the diode and the clipping level becomes $V_B + 0.7$. Two diodes (or two Zeners back to back) clip both polarities. Clippers protect inputs from overvoltage: nearly every IC pin has clamp diodes to its supply rails.

```plot
x: 0, 2, t (ms)
y: -5.5, 5.5, voltage (V)
f: 5*sin(2*pi*x) | input | dot | 4
f: min(5*sin(2*pi*x), 0.7) | clipped output
hline: 0.7, 0.7 V
caption: A positive clipper: the diode conducts whenever the input exceeds 0.7 V, holding the output there.
```

## Clampers (DC restorers)

A **clamper** uses a capacitor and a diode to *shift* a waveform's DC level without changing its shape. The capacitor charges to the input's peak through the diode, then acts like a battery in series with the signal. A 5 V-peak sine through a positive clamper (ideal diode) comes out swinging from 0 to +10 V. Clampers restore the DC level of video signals and are the building block of voltage multipliers.

## Key takeaways

- A Zener holds $V_Z$ in breakdown; keep $I_Z$ between the knee current and the power limit.
- Shunt regulator: $I_S = (V_S - V_Z)/R_S$, $I_Z = I_S - I_L$; check the no-load case.
- Clippers limit amplitude; clampers shift DC level.
