## Three ways to connect one transistor

A MOSFET has three terminals. Ground one (for AC), use one as input and one as output, and you get three basic amplifiers, each with a distinct personality.

## Common source (CS): the voltage amplifier

```circuit
W 2.6,-0.4 4.4,-0.4
TEXT 3.5,-0.75 V_{DD}
T 0,2 v_{in}
C 0,2 1.4,2 C_C
W 1.4,2 2,2
W 2,2 2.5,2
R 2,2 2,3.4 R_G side=-1
GND 2,3.4
NMOS 3.5,2 ""
R 3.5,-0.4 3.5,1 R_D
GND 3.5,3
W 3.5,1 4.3,1
C 4.3,1 5.5,1 C_C
R 5.5,1 5.5,2.6 R_L
GND 5.5,2.6
TEXT 5.5,0.6 v_{out} color=anno
caption: A common-source amplifier. Coupling capacitors $C_C$ pass the signal but block DC, so they don't disturb the bias.
```

Input at the gate, output at the drain. In the small-signal model, $g_mv_{gs}$ flows through everything connected at the drain:

$$
A_v = -g_m\,(R_D \parallel r_o \parallel R_L), \qquad R_{in} = R_G, \qquad R_{out} = R_D \parallel r_o
$$

The minus sign means the output is **inverted**. High gain, very high input resistance, moderate output resistance.

> **Worked example.** $g_m = 4$ mS, $R_D = 5$ kΩ, $r_o = 50$ kΩ, $R_L = 10$ kΩ.
> $R_D \parallel r_o \parallel R_L = 3.125$ kΩ, so $A_v = -4 \times 3.125 = -12.5$.

**Source degeneration.** Leaving a resistor $R_S$ unbypassed in the source trades gain for linearity and predictability:
$$A_v \approx -\frac{g_mR_D}{1 + g_mR_S} \;\xrightarrow{\;g_mR_S \gg 1\;}\; -\frac{R_D}{R_S}$$
> With $g_m = 4$ mS, $R_D = 5$ kΩ and $R_S = 500\ \Omega$: $A_v = -20/(1 + 2) = -6.67$, now set mostly by the resistor ratio.

## Common gate (CG): the current buffer

Input at the source, output at the drain, gate grounded:

$$
A_v = +g_m(R_D \parallel R_L), \qquad R_{in} \approx \frac{1}{g_m}, \qquad R_{out} \approx R_D
$$

Same gain magnitude as CS but non-inverting, with a **low** input resistance ($1/g_m$; 250 Ω for $g_m = 4$ mS). It's used to accept current from low-impedance sources and in RF stages and cascodes.

## Common drain (CD): the source follower

Input at the gate, output at the source:

$$
A_v = \frac{g_mR_L'}{1 + g_mR_L'} \approx 1, \qquad R_{in} = R_G, \qquad R_{out} \approx \frac{1}{g_m}
$$

where $R_L'$ is the total resistance at the source. The gain is just under 1 (0.89 for $g_m = 4$ mS and $R_L' = 2$ kΩ), but the output resistance is low: it's a **voltage buffer** that lets a high-impedance stage drive a heavy load.

## Summary

| | Common source | Common gate | Common drain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voltage gain | $-g_mR_D'$ (high, inverting) | $+g_mR_D'$ (high) | $\approx 1$ |
| Input resistance | very high | low ($1/g_m$) | very high |
| Output resistance | $R_D \parallel r_o$ | $\approx R_D$ | low ($1/g_m$) |
| Typical use | main gain stage | current buffer, RF, cascode | output buffer |

## Key takeaways

- CS: $A_v = -g_m(R_D \parallel r_o \parallel R_L)$, the workhorse gain stage; degeneration gives $\approx -R_D/R_S$.
- CG: non-inverting gain, $R_{in} = 1/g_m$.
- CD (follower): gain ≈ 1, $R_{out} \approx 1/g_m$, a buffer.
