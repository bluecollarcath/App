## Three BJT amplifiers

Exactly as with the MOSFET, grounding each of the three terminals in turn gives three amplifiers. We'll use the bias point from Lesson 3.2: $I_C = 2.12$ mA, so $g_m = 82$ mS, $r_\pi = 1.22$ kΩ, $r_e = 12\ \Omega$, with $\beta = 100$, $R_C = 3$ kΩ, and $R_{Th} = R_1 \parallel R_2 = 7.5$ kΩ. A coupling capacitor connects a load $R_L$.

## Common emitter (CE): the gain stage

Input at the base, output at the collector, emitter grounded for AC (through a bypass capacitor):

$$
A_v = -g_m\,(R_C \parallel R_L \parallel r_o), \qquad R_{in} = R_{Th} \parallel r_\pi, \qquad R_{out} = R_C \parallel r_o
$$

> With $R_L = 3$ kΩ (and $r_o$ large): $A_v = -0.082 \times 1500 = -123$, and $R_{in} = 7.5\ \text{k} \parallel 1.22\ \text{k} = 1.05$ kΩ.

A CE stage gives large gain but has a rather low input resistance, because the base draws current.

### Emitter degeneration

Leave part of the emitter resistance, $R_E$, unbypassed. The gain drops but becomes predictable, and the input resistance rises by the factor $(\beta + 1)$:

$$
A_v \approx -\frac{R_C \parallel R_L}{r_e + R_E}, \qquad R_{in} = R_{Th} \parallel (\beta + 1)(r_e + R_E)
$$

> With $R_E = 200\ \Omega$: $A_v = -1500/212 = -7.07$ and $R_{in} = 7.5\ \text{k} \parallel 21.4\ \text{k} = 5.55$ kΩ. The gain is now set by a resistor ratio, almost independent of $\beta$ and temperature, at the cost of 17× less gain.

## Common base (CB)

Input at the emitter, output at the collector, base grounded for AC:

$$
A_v = +g_m(R_C \parallel R_L), \qquad R_{in} \approx r_e, \qquad R_{out} \approx R_C
$$

The same gain magnitude as CE (+123) without inversion, but an input resistance of only about 12 Ω. It's useful for matching low-impedance sources (like a 50 Ω RF input) and in cascodes, where its isolation of input from output improves high-frequency response.

## Common collector (CC): the emitter follower

Input at the base, output at the emitter:

$$
A_v = \frac{R_L}{r_e + R_L} \approx 1, \qquad R_{in} = R_{Th} \parallel (\beta + 1)(r_e + R_L), \qquad R_{out} \approx r_e + \frac{R_s \parallel R_{Th}}{\beta + 1}
$$

> Driving a 1 kΩ load: $A_v = 1000/1012 = 0.988$; the input resistance seen at the base is $101 \times 1.012$ kΩ $= 102$ kΩ; and with a 1 kΩ source, $R_{out} \approx 12 + 882/101 = 21\ \Omega$.

The follower **multiplies resistance by $\beta + 1$ looking in, and divides it by $\beta + 1$ looking back**. That's why it's the classic output buffer.

## Summary

| | CE | CE with $R_E$ | CB | CC (follower) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $A_v$ | $-g_mR_C'$ (−123) | $-R_C'/(r_e + R_E)$ (−7.1) | $+g_mR_C'$ (+123) | $\approx 1$ (0.988) |
| $R_{in}$ | low-moderate | moderate-high | very low ($r_e$) | high |
| $R_{out}$ | $R_C$ | $\approx R_C$ | $R_C$ | very low |

## Key takeaways

- CE: $A_v = -g_mR_C'$; degeneration gives $\approx -R_C'/(r_e + R_E)$ and multiplies $R_{in}$ by $\beta + 1$.
- CB: non-inverting gain with $R_{in} \approx r_e$.
- CC follower: gain ≈ 1, resistance scaled by $\beta + 1$ in and divided by it out.
