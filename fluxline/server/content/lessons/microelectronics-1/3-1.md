## The bipolar junction transistor

Before the MOSFET took over digital logic, the **BJT** (bipolar junction transistor, invented at Bell Labs in 1947) was *the* transistor, and it remains essential in analog, RF, and power circuits for its high transconductance and precise exponential behavior.

An **NPN** BJT is a thin p-type **base** sandwiched between an n-type **emitter** and **collector**. Unlike a MOSFET, the base does draw current.

```circuit
NPN 1.6,1 ""
T 0.6,1 B
T 1.6,0 C
T 1.6,2 E
caption: The NPN symbol. The arrow on the emitter shows the direction of conventional current (out for NPN, in for PNP).
```

## Active mode

With the base–emitter junction **forward-biased** (about 0.7 V) and the base–collector junction **reverse-biased**, electrons injected from the emitter diffuse across the thin base and are swept into the collector. The collector current depends exponentially on $v_{BE}$:

$$
i_C = I_S\,e^{v_{BE}/V_T}, \qquad V_T = kT/q \approx 25.9\ \text{mV}
$$

A small fraction of the carriers recombine in the base; that's the base current:

$$
i_B = \frac{i_C}{\beta}, \qquad i_E = i_C + i_B = (\beta + 1)\,i_B, \qquad \alpha = \frac{i_C}{i_E} = \frac{\beta}{\beta + 1}
$$

The **current gain** $\beta$ is typically 50–300, and it varies a lot between devices, so good circuits are designed not to depend on its exact value.

> **Worked example.** $I_S = 10^{-15}$ A, $v_{BE} = 0.7$ V, $\beta = 100$.
> $i_C = 10^{-15}e^{0.7/0.02585} = 0.576$ mA, $\quad i_B = 5.76\ \mu$A, $\quad \alpha = 0.990$.

As with a diode, a 10× change in collector current needs only about 60 mV more $v_{BE}$.

## Regions of operation

| Region | BE junction | BC junction | Behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cutoff | off | off | $i_C \approx 0$: open switch |
| **Active** | forward | reverse | $i_C = \beta i_B$: amplifier |
| Saturation | forward | forward | $v_{CE} \approx 0.2$ V: closed switch |

```plot
x: 0, 10, v_{CE} (V)
y: 0, 4.6, i_C (mA)
f: 1*(1 - exp(-x/0.08))*(1 + x/50) | i_B = 10 µA
f: 2*(1 - exp(-x/0.08))*(1 + x/50) | i_B = 20 µA
f: 3*(1 - exp(-x/0.08))*(1 + x/50) | i_B = 30 µA
caption: Output characteristics for $\beta = 100$. Past the saturation knee near 0.2 V, $i_C \approx \beta i_B$, rising slightly with $v_{CE}$ (the Early effect, $V_A = 50$ V here).
```

**The Early effect:** in the active region $i_C$ rises slightly with $v_{CE}$, modeled as an output resistance $r_o = V_A/I_C$, where $V_A$ (the Early voltage) is typically 20–200 V.

**PNP** transistors are the complement: everything reversed, with the emitter at the most positive terminal.

```

```

## Key takeaways

- Active mode: $i_C = I_Se^{v_{BE}/V_T}$, $i_B = i_C/\beta$, $i_E = (\beta+1)i_B$.
- Cutoff = off; active = amplify; saturation = on ($v_{CE} \approx 0.2$ V).
- Early effect: $r_o = V_A/I_C$.
