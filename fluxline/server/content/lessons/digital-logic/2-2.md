## Adding in binary

Binary addition works exactly like decimal addition: add each column, write the sum bit, carry the overflow to the next column. A computer's arithmetic unit is built from one small circuit, the **full adder**, repeated once per bit.

## The half adder

Adding two bits $A$ and $B$ gives a sum bit and a carry bit:

| $A$ | $B$ | Carry | Sum |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |

$$
S = A \oplus B, \qquad C = AB
$$

```circuit
T 0,-0.4 A
W 0,-0.4 0.7,-0.4
W 0.7,-0.4 1.5,-0.4
W 0.7,-0.4 0.7,1.2 1.5,1.2
T 0,0.4 B
W 0,0.4 1.1,0.4
W 1.1,0.4 1.5,0.4
W 1.1,0.4 1.1,2 1.5,2
XOR 2.5,0
AND 2.5,1.6
W 3.5,0 4.2,0
T 4.2,0 S
W 3.5,1.6 4.2,1.6
T 4.2,1.6 C
caption: A half adder: one XOR gate for the sum, one AND gate for the carry.
```

## The full adder

To add multi-bit numbers, each column must also accept the carry from the column to its right, $C_{in}$:

$$
S = A \oplus B \oplus C_{in}, \qquad C_{out} = AB + C_{in}(A \oplus B)
$$

$C_{out}$ is 1 when at least two of the three inputs are 1 (a "majority" function). A full adder is two half adders plus an OR gate.

## Ripple-carry adders

Chain $n$ full adders, connecting each $C_{out}$ to the next $C_{in}$, and you have an $n$-bit adder.

> **Example.** $0110 + 0111$ (6 + 7):
>
> | Column | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | Carry in | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
> | $A$ | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
> | $B$ | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
> | Sum | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
>
> Result $1101 = 13$ ✓.

The catch is speed. The carry must **ripple** through every stage, so the worst-case delay grows linearly with the width. If each carry takes 2 gate delays, a 64-bit ripple adder needs about 128 gate delays. That's far too slow for a modern CPU.

## Carry-lookahead

Compute the carries in parallel instead. For each bit define:

- **generate** $G_i = A_iB_i$: this column makes a carry by itself;
- **propagate** $P_i = A_i \oplus B_i$: this column passes an incoming carry through.

Then $C_{i+1} = G_i + P_iC_i$, and expanding the recursion gives every carry directly from the inputs:

$$
C_2 = G_1 + P_1G_0 + P_1P_0C_0
$$

A carry-lookahead adder computes all carries in a few gate delays regardless of width (in practice, in hierarchical blocks). Adders are a classic example of trading area for speed.

## Subtraction and overflow

With two's complement, **subtraction is addition**: $A - B = A + \overline{B} + 1$. Invert $B$ and set the first carry-in to 1, and the same adder subtracts. One XOR per bit, controlled by a SUB signal, does the inverting.

**Signed overflow** happens when the carry into the sign bit differs from the carry out of it:

$$
V = C_n \oplus C_{n-1}
$$

> In the example, 6 + 7 = 13, but a 4-bit signed number only reaches +7. Indeed $C_3 = 1$, $C_4 = 0$, so $V = 1$: $1101$ would be read as $-3$.


## Key takeaways

- Half adder: $S = A \oplus B$, $C = AB$. Full adder adds $C_{in}$.
- Ripple-carry delay grows with width; carry-lookahead computes carries in parallel.
- Subtract by adding the two's complement; overflow when $C_n \ne C_{n-1}$.
