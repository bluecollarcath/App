## Why slope matters

Slope measures **how fast one thing changes when another changes**: miles per hour, liters per minute, milliamps per volt. Calculus, at its heart, is the study of slope, so this lesson is one of the most important in the course.

## Slope: rise over run

Pick two points on a line. The **slope** is how much the line goes **up** (the rise) divided by how much it goes **across** (the run):

$$
m = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}
$$

The little numbers ("subscripts") just label the two points: $(x_1, y_1)$ is the first point and $(x_2, y_2)$ is the second. The letter $m$ is the traditional name for slope.

> **Example 1.** Find the slope of the line through $(1, 3)$ and $(4, 9)$.
>
> $$
> m = \frac{9 - 3}{4 - 1} = \frac{6}{3} = 2
> $$
>
> For every 1 step to the right, the line goes up 2.

> **Example 2.** Through $(-2, 5)$ and $(2, -3)$:
>
> $$
> m = \frac{-3 - 5}{2 - (-2)} = \frac{-8}{4} = -2
> $$
>
> A negative slope means the line goes **down** as you move right.

It doesn't matter which point you call "first," as long as you use the same order on the top and the bottom.

**The four kinds of slope:**
- **Positive:** rises to the right.
- **Negative:** falls to the right.
- **Zero:** flat (horizontal). The rise is 0.
- **Undefined:** vertical. The run is 0, and you can't divide by zero.

## The equation of a line: $y = mx + b$

Every non-vertical line can be written as

$$
y = mx + b
$$

- $m$ is the **slope**.
- $b$ is the **$y$-intercept**: where the line crosses the $y$-axis (the value of $y$ when $x = 0$).

> **Example 3.** For $y = -3x + 7$: the slope is $-3$ and the line crosses the $y$-axis at $7$.

## Finding a line's equation from two points

1. Find the slope $m$ from the two points.
2. Put one point into $y = mx + b$ and solve for $b$.

> **Example 4.** Find the line through $(1, 2)$ and $(3, 8)$.
>
> Slope: $m = \dfrac{8 - 2}{3 - 1} = \dfrac{6}{2} = 3$.
>
> Use the point $(1, 2)$: $2 = 3(1) + b$, so $b = -1$.
>
> The line is $y = 3x - 1$. **Check** with the other point: $3(3) - 1 = 8$. ✓

## Slope is a rate of change

When $x$ and $y$ are real quantities, the slope has **units**: units of $y$ per unit of $x$. That's what makes slope useful.

> **Example 5.** A water tank holds 20 liters at 2 minutes and 50 liters at 8 minutes, filling steadily.
>
> $$
> \text{rate} = \frac{50 - 20\ \text{L}}{8 - 2\ \text{min}} = \frac{30\ \text{L}}{6\ \text{min}} = 5\ \text{L/min}
> $$

> **Example 6.** You measure the current through a resistor at two voltages: 4 mA at 2 V, and 12 mA at 6 V.
>
> Slope: $\dfrac{12 - 4\ \text{mA}}{6 - 2\ \text{V}} = 2\ \text{mA per volt}$.
>
> Ohm's law ($I = V/R$) says this slope is $\dfrac{1}{R}$. So $R = \dfrac{1}{2\ \text{mA/V}} = \dfrac{1\ \text{V}}{2\ \text{mA}} = 500\ \Omega$. You can also check one point directly: $R = \dfrac{V}{I} = \dfrac{2\ \text{V}}{0.004\ \text{A}} = 500\ \Omega$.

A straight line means the rate is **constant**: the tank fills at the same speed the whole time. For curves, the rate keeps changing. Measuring the rate at a single instant on a curve is exactly what calculus does, and you'll get a first taste of it in Unit 5.

### Common mistakes

- **Mixing up the order.** Subtract in the same order on top and bottom: $\dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$, not $\dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_1 - x_2}$.
- **Putting $x$ on top.** Slope is change in $y$ (vertical) over change in $x$ (horizontal): rise over run.
- **Forgetting units.** A slope of "5" means little; "5 L/min" tells the whole story.

## Key takeaways

- Slope $m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$: rise over run.
- Positive slopes rise, negative slopes fall, horizontal lines have slope 0, vertical lines have undefined slope.
- $y = mx + b$: $m$ is the slope, $b$ the $y$-intercept. Find $m$ from two points, then $b$ from one point.
- With real quantities, slope is a **rate** with units ("per").
- A straight line has a constant rate; calculus handles rates that change.
