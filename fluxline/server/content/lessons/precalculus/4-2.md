## Beyond right triangles

In a right triangle, the angles are always less than 90°. But a wheel turns through 120°, 270°, 1000°; an AC generator spins round and round. To describe rotation, we need sine and cosine for **any** angle. The **unit circle** gives us that.

## The unit circle

Draw a circle of radius 1 centered at the origin. Start at the point $(1, 0)$ on the right and rotate **counterclockwise** by an angle $\theta$. The point you land on defines cosine and sine:

$$
(x, y) = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)
$$

```circuit

LINE 3,0.2 3,5
CIRCLE 3,2.6 2
LINE 3,2.6 4.532,1.314
LINE 4.532,1.314 4.532,2.6 dash
DOT 4.532,1.314

TEXT 3.5,2.38 "θ" size=15 color=anno

caption: The unit circle (radius 1). Rotate counterclockwise from the positive $x$-axis by $\theta$: the point's horizontal position is $\cos\theta$ and its height is $\sin\theta$.
```

For angles less than 90°, this matches the triangle definition: the radius is a hypotenuse of length 1, so $\cos\theta = \tfrac{\text{adj}}{1}$ and $\sin\theta = \tfrac{\text{opp}}{1}$. But now it also works past 90°.

## Reading values off the circle

Some values follow directly from where the point lands:

| $\theta$ | Point | $\cos\theta$ | $\sin\theta$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0° | right $(1, 0)$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| 90° | top $(0, 1)$ | $0$ | $1$ |
| 180° | left $(-1, 0)$ | $-1$ | $0$ |
| 270° | bottom $(0, -1)$ | $0$ | $-1$ |

**Signs:** cosine is negative on the **left** half of the circle; sine is negative on the **bottom** half.

**Using reference angles.** For other angles, find the matching acute angle and fix the sign. At 150°, the point is 30° short of 180°: it's the mirror image of the 30° point, flipped to the left. So

$$
\sin 150° = \sin 30° = 0.5 \qquad \cos 150° = -\cos 30° \approx -0.866
$$

(It's on the left, so cosine is negative; it's still above the axis, so sine is positive.)

**Negative angles** go clockwise: $-30°$ lands below the axis, so $\sin(-30°) = -0.5$ while $\cos(-30°) = \cos 30°$.

**Angles beyond 360°** just go around again: $390° = 360° + 30°$ lands on the same point as $30°$, so $\sin 390° = 0.5$. In general, $\sin(\theta + 360°) = \sin\theta$. Functions that repeat like this are called **periodic**.

## Sine and cosine as functions

Plot the height $\sin\theta$ as the point goes around, and you get the famous **sine wave**. Here the angle is in radians, so one full turn is $2\pi \approx 6.28$:

```plot
x: 0, 6.2832, θ (radians)
y: -1.3, 1.3
f: sin(x) | sin θ
f: cos(x) | cos θ | dash
hline: 0
vline: 1.5708, π/2
vline: 3.1416, π
vline: 4.7124, 3π/2
caption: One full turn of the unit circle. Both waves swing between $-1$ and $1$ and repeat every $2\pi$ radians (360°). Cosine is the same wave as sine, shifted a quarter turn earlier.
```

This picture is the heart of AC electricity: the voltage at a wall outlet is exactly this wave, scaled up and repeating 60 times per second. The next lesson shows how to describe its size, speed and timing.

## Key identities

An **identity** is an equation that's true for every angle. Three are worth knowing now:

**1. The Pythagorean identity.** The point $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ is on a circle of radius 1, so by Pythagoras:

$$
\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1
$$

($\sin^2\theta$ means $(\sin\theta)^2$.) If you know one, you can find the other, up to its sign.

**2. Tangent.** $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$. It's undefined where $\cos\theta = 0$, at 90° and 270°.

**3. Cosine is shifted sine.** $\cos\theta = \sin(\theta + 90°)$. Cosine reaches each peak a quarter turn before sine does. In AC circuits you'll hear this as "leads by 90°."

> **Example.** If $\sin\theta = 0.6$ and $\theta$ is between 0° and 90°, find $\cos\theta$.
>
> $\cos^2\theta = 1 - 0.6^2 = 1 - 0.36 = 0.64$, so $\cos\theta = 0.8$ (positive, because the angle is in the first quarter of the circle).

### Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the sign.** The size of $\cos 150°$ matches $\cos 30°$, but it's on the left side of the circle, so it's negative.
- **$\sin^2\theta$ vs $\sin(\theta^2)$.** $\sin^2\theta$ squares the result: $(\sin\theta)^2$.
- **Mixing degrees and radians** in the same problem.

## Key takeaways

- On the unit circle, the point at angle $\theta$ is $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$; this defines sine and cosine for any angle.
- Cosine is negative on the left half, sine on the bottom half. Use the reference angle and fix the sign.
- Sine and cosine are periodic: they repeat every 360° ($2\pi$ radians).
- $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ and $\tan\theta = \sin\theta / \cos\theta$.
- Cosine is a sine wave shifted 90° earlier.
