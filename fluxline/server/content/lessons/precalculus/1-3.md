## Equations are balances

A **variable** is a letter that stands for a number we don't know yet, like $x$. An **equation** says two things are equal, like

$$
3x + 5 = 20
$$

**Solving** it means finding the value of $x$ that makes it true. Think of an equation as a balance scale: the left side weighs the same as the right side. **Whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other**, and it stays balanced. That one rule is all of equation solving.

Your goal is to get the variable **alone** on one side. To undo an operation, use its opposite:

| To undo… | …do this to both sides |
|---|---|
| $+ 5$ | subtract 5 |
| $- 5$ | add 5 |
| $\times 3$ | divide by 3 |
| $\div 3$ | multiply by 3 |
| squaring | take the square root |

## One-step equations

> **Example 1.** $x + 7 = 12$. Subtract 7 from both sides: $x = 12 - 7 = 5$.
>
> **Example 2.** $4x = 28$. ($4x$ means $4 \times x$.) Divide both sides by 4: $x = 7$.
>
> **Example 3.** $\dfrac{x}{4} = 5$. Multiply both sides by 4: $x = 20$.

## Two-step equations

Undo things in **reverse order**: addition and subtraction first, then multiplication and division. (It's like taking off your shoes before your socks.)

> **Example 4.** Solve $3x + 5 = 20$.
>
> Subtract 5 from both sides: $3x = 15$.
>
> Divide both sides by 3: $x = 5$.
>
> **Check:** put 5 back in: $3(5) + 5 = 15 + 5 = 20$. ✓

**Always check** by substituting your answer into the original equation. It takes ten seconds and catches most mistakes.

## Variables on both sides

Gather the variable terms on one side and the plain numbers on the other.

> **Example 5.** Solve $5x - 3 = 2x + 9$.
>
> Subtract $2x$ from both sides: $3x - 3 = 9$.
>
> Add 3 to both sides: $3x = 12$.
>
> Divide by 3: $x = 4$. **Check:** $5(4) - 3 = 17$ and $2(4) + 9 = 17$. ✓

## Parentheses: the distributive property

A number in front of parentheses multiplies **everything** inside:

$$
a(b + c) = ab + ac
$$

So $2(x - 3) = 2x - 6$. This is called **distributing** (or "expanding").

> **Example 6.** Solve $2(x - 3) = 10$.
>
> Distribute: $2x - 6 = 10$. Add 6: $2x = 16$. Divide by 2: $x = 8$.
>
> (Or divide both sides by 2 first: $x - 3 = 5$, so $x = 8$. Both ways work.)

## Fractions in equations

> **Example 7.** Solve $\dfrac{x}{3} + 2 = 6$.
>
> Subtract 2: $\dfrac{x}{3} = 4$. Multiply by 3: $x = 12$.

### Common mistakes

- **Doing something to only part of a side.** In $3x + 5 = 20$, dividing by 3 must divide *every* term: $x + \tfrac{5}{3} = \tfrac{20}{3}$. That's correct but messy, which is why you subtract the 5 first.
- **Sign slips when moving terms.** "Moving" $+2x$ to the other side really means subtracting $2x$ from both sides, so it becomes $-2x$. Write the step out until it's automatic.
- **Forgetting to distribute to every term**: $2(x - 3)$ is $2x - 6$, not $2x - 3$.

## Rearranging formulas

Engineering formulas are equations with several letters. The same rules let you solve for whichever letter you need. This is one of the most-used skills in all of electronics.

**Ohm's law** relates the voltage $V$ across a resistor (in volts, V), the current $I$ through it (in amperes, A) and its resistance $R$ (in ohms, Ω):

$$
V = IR
$$

```circuit
V 0,0 0,2 "V = 12 V" side=-1
W 0,0 2.4,0
R 2.4,0 2.4,2 "R = 4 Ω" i=I
W 2.4,2 0,2
caption: A battery driving a resistor. If you know any two of $V$, $I$ and $R$, Ohm's law gives the third.
```

**Solve for $I$:** $V = IR$ has $I$ multiplied by $R$, so divide both sides by $R$:

$$
I = \frac{V}{R}
$$

For the circuit above: $I = \dfrac{12}{4} = 3$ A.

**Solve for $R$:** divide both sides of $V = IR$ by $I$: $R = \dfrac{V}{I}$.

> **Example 8.** Electrical power is $P = VI$ (in watts, W). How much current does a 60 W lamp draw from 120 V?
>
> Divide both sides by $V$: $I = \dfrac{P}{V} = \dfrac{60}{120} = 0.5$ A.

> **Example 9.** Power in a resistor is also $P = I^2 R$. Solve for $I$.
>
> Divide by $R$: $I^2 = \dfrac{P}{R}$. Undo the square with a square root: $I = \sqrt{\dfrac{P}{R}}$.
>
> For $P = 2$ W and $R = 50\ \Omega$: $I = \sqrt{0.04} = 0.2$ A.

> **Example 10.** Temperature: $F = \tfrac{9}{5}C + 32$. Solve for $C$.
>
> Subtract 32: $F - 32 = \tfrac{9}{5}C$. Multiply both sides by $\tfrac{5}{9}$: $C = \tfrac{5}{9}(F - 32)$.
>
> For $F = 68$: $C = \tfrac{5}{9}(36) = 20$ °C.

**Tip:** if a formula feels confusing, solve the same steps with simple made-up numbers first (like $12 = 3 \times 4$), then repeat with the letters.

## Key takeaways

- An equation is a balance: do the same thing to both sides.
- Undo operations in reverse order: add/subtract first, then multiply/divide.
- Distribute a number to every term inside parentheses.
- Always check your answer by substituting it back.
- Rearranging formulas uses the same moves: from $V = IR$ you get $I = V/R$ and $R = V/I$.
