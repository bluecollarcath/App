## Start here

This course assumes only that you can add, subtract, multiply and divide. Everything else is built step by step. A few tips that make a real difference:

- **Work with a pencil and paper.** Copy each worked example and do it yourself before reading the solution. Math is learned by doing, not by reading.
- **Do every practice problem.** Type your answer and press **Check**. You can enter decimals, fractions like `3/4`, or negative numbers like `-5`. If you're stuck, **Show solution** walks through it.
- **Go slowly.** Re-reading a section is normal. Nobody gets this on the first pass.
- **Use a calculator** when the numbers get messy; the goal is to understand the steps, not to do long division by hand.

This first lesson makes sure the basics are solid: negative numbers, the order of operations, fractions and percentages. Even if some of it feels familiar, read the "common mistakes" sections; they catch almost everyone.

**Adding and subtracting:**
- Adding a positive number moves **right**: $-2 + 3 = 1$.
- Subtracting a positive number moves **left**: $1 - 4 = -3$.
- Subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive: $5 - (-2) = 5 + 2 = 7$. (Taking away a debt makes you richer.)
- Adding a negative is the same as subtracting: $6 + (-8) = 6 - 8 = -2$.

**Multiplying and dividing** follow one sign rule:

| Signs | Result | Example |
|---|---|---|
| positive × positive | positive | $3 \times 4 = 12$ |
| negative × negative | positive | $(-3)(-4) = 12$ |
| positive × negative | negative | $3 \times (-4) = -12$ |
| negative ÷ positive | negative | $(-12) \div 4 = -3$ |

Two negative signs cancel; one negative sign survives. Writing two things side by side, like $(-3)(-4)$, means multiply.

## The order of operations

When an expression has several operations, everyone has to agree on which to do first, or two people would get two different answers. The agreed order is:

1. **Parentheses** (and other brackets): work inside them first.
2. **Exponents**: powers like $3^2$ (we'll cover these fully in the next lesson; for now, $3^2 = 3 \times 3 = 9$).
3. **Multiplication and division**, from **left to right**.
4. **Addition and subtraction**, from **left to right**.

> **Example 1.** $2 + 3 \times 4$
>
> Multiplication comes before addition: $3 \times 4 = 12$, then $2 + 12 = 14$. (Not $5 \times 4 = 20$.)

> **Example 2.** $(2 + 3) \times 4$
>
> Parentheses first: $2 + 3 = 5$, then $5 \times 4 = 20$. Parentheses are how you *force* a different order.

> **Example 3.** $20 \div 4 \times 5$
>
> Multiplication and division have equal rank, so go left to right: $20 \div 4 = 5$, then $5 \times 5 = 25$. (Not $20 \div 20 = 1$.)

> **Example 4.** $10 - 2 \times (1 + 3)^2$
>
> Parentheses: $1 + 3 = 4$. Exponent: $4^2 = 16$. Multiply: $2 \times 16 = 32$. Subtract: $10 - 32 = -22$.

A fraction bar works like parentheses around the top and the bottom: in $\dfrac{6 + 4}{2}$, do $6 + 4 = 10$ first, then $10 \div 2 = 5$.

### Common mistakes

- **$-3^2$ is not the same as $(-3)^2$.** In $-3^2$, the exponent applies only to the 3: $-3^2 = -(3 \times 3) = -9$. In $(-3)^2$, the parentheses include the sign: $(-3)(-3) = 9$. Calculators follow this rule too.
- **Multiplication doesn't always come before division.** They're equal; go left to right.

## Fractions

A fraction $\dfrac{a}{b}$ means "$a$ divided by $b$." The top number is the **numerator** and the bottom is the **denominator**. $\dfrac{3}{4}$ is three parts out of four equal parts, or $3 \div 4 = 0.75$.

**Simplifying.** Divide the top and bottom by the same number. Both numbers in $\dfrac{6}{8}$ divide by 2, so $\dfrac{6}{8} = \dfrac{3}{4}$. The value doesn't change, only how it's written.

**Multiplying.** Multiply the tops together and the bottoms together:

$$
\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{4}{5} = \frac{2 \times 4}{3 \times 5} = \frac{8}{15}
$$

**Dividing.** Flip the second fraction (its **reciprocal**) and multiply:

$$
\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{1} = \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2}
$$

That makes sense: there are one and a half halves in three quarters.

**Adding and subtracting** needs a **common denominator**, because you can only add pieces of the same size.

> **Example 5.** $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{3}$
>
> The smallest number both 2 and 3 divide into is 6. Rewrite each fraction with 6 on the bottom by multiplying top and bottom by the same number:
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{6}, \qquad \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{6}
> $$
>
> Now add the tops: $\dfrac{3}{6} + \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{5}{6}$.

A quick way to get a common denominator is to multiply the two denominators: $\dfrac{a}{b} + \dfrac{c}{d} = \dfrac{ad + bc}{bd}$.

### Common mistake

**Never add the bottoms.** $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{3}$ is **not** $\dfrac{2}{5}$. A quick check: $\dfrac{1}{2}$ alone is already bigger than $\dfrac{2}{5}$, so that answer can't be right.

**Where you'll see this.** Two resistors in parallel combine as $\dfrac{1}{R} = \dfrac{1}{R_1} + \dfrac{1}{R_2}$. For $6\ \Omega$ and $3\ \Omega$ (Ω, "ohms", is the unit of resistance): $\dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $R = 2\ \Omega$. That's exactly the fraction skill above.

## Percentages

"Percent" means "per hundred": $15\% = \dfrac{15}{100} = 0.15$.

- **A percentage of a number:** multiply by the decimal. $15\%$ of $80$ is $0.15 \times 80 = 12$.
- **Percent change:** $\dfrac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100\%$. A price going from 40 to 50 is a $\dfrac{50 - 40}{40} = 0.25$, or $25\%$, increase.

> **Example 6.** A resistor is marked $1000\ \Omega \pm 5\%$, meaning its true value can be up to 5% above or below 1000 Ω. Five percent of 1000 is $0.05 \times 1000 = 50$, so the real value is anywhere from $950$ to $1050\ \Omega$.

## Key takeaways

- Subtracting a negative adds; multiplying or dividing two negatives gives a positive.
- Order of operations: parentheses, exponents, then multiply/divide left to right, then add/subtract left to right.
- $-3^2 = -9$, but $(-3)^2 = 9$.
- Fractions: multiply straight across; divide by flipping the second and multiplying; add only with a common denominator, never by adding the bottoms.
- A percentage is a fraction of 100: $p\%$ of $x$ is $\dfrac{p}{100} \times x$.
