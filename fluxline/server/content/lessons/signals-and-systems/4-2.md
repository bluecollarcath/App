## The Laplace transform for sequences

The **z-transform** does for discrete time what the Laplace transform does for continuous time:

$$
X(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n]\,z^{-n}
$$

with $z$ a complex number. On the **unit circle**, $z = e^{j\omega}$, it reduces to the DTFT. Multiplying by $z^{-1}$ means a **one-sample delay**, which makes the z-transform the natural language of digital filters.

## Key pairs

| $x[n]$ | $X(z)$ | ROC |
|---|---|---|
| $\delta[n]$ | $1$ | all $z$ |
| $\delta[n - k]$ | $z^{-k}$ | $z \ne 0$ |
| $u[n]$ | $\dfrac{1}{1 - z^{-1}}$ | $|z| > 1$ |
| $a^nu[n]$ | $\dfrac{1}{1 - az^{-1}} = \dfrac{z}{z - a}$ | $|z| > |a|$ |

As with Laplace, causal sequences have ROCs *outside* a circle through the outermost pole.

## From difference equation to $H(z)$

Replace every delay $y[n - k]$ with $z^{-k}Y(z)$:

> **Example.** $y[n] = 0.5\,y[n-1] + x[n]$
> $$
> Y(z) = 0.5z^{-1}Y(z) + X(z) \;\Rightarrow\; H(z) = \frac{1}{1 - 0.5z^{-1}} = \frac{z}{z - 0.5}
> $$
> One pole at $z = 0.5$. Impulse response: $h[n] = 0.5^nu[n]$. DC gain: $H(1) = 2$.

## Stability: inside the unit circle

| Continuous time | Discrete time |
|---|---|
| stable if poles have $\operatorname{Re}\{s\} < 0$ | stable if poles have $|z| < 1$ |
| $j\omega$ axis = frequency | unit circle = frequency |

A causal discrete-time system is stable **if and only if all its poles lie inside the unit circle**. The mapping $z = e^{sT}$ connects the two pictures: the left half of the s-plane maps to the inside of the unit circle.

## Inverse z-transform by partial fractions

> $H(z) = \dfrac{1}{(1 - 0.5z^{-1})(1 - 0.4z^{-1})} = \dfrac{5}{1 - 0.5z^{-1}} - \dfrac{4}{1 - 0.4z^{-1}}$
>
> so $h[n] = \big(5(0.5)^n - 4(0.4)^n\big)u[n]$. Check: $h[0] = 1$ ✓.

## Key takeaways

- $X(z) = \sum x[n]z^{-n}$; $z^{-1}$ = one-sample delay; on the unit circle it's the DTFT.
- Difference equation → $H(z)$ by replacing each delay with $z^{-k}$.
- Causal and stable ⇔ all poles inside the unit circle.
