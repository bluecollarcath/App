## From series to transform

A Fourier series describes a periodic signal with harmonics spaced $\omega_0 = 2\pi/T$ apart. Let the period $T$ grow without bound: the harmonics crowd together into a continuous **spectrum**. That's the **continuous-time Fourier transform (CTFT)**:

$$
X(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)\,e^{-j\omega t}\,dt,
\qquad
x(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} X(\omega)\,e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega
$$

$|X(\omega)|$ says how much of each frequency the signal contains; $\angle X(\omega)$ gives the phase. (Many engineers use frequency in hertz instead: $X(f) = \int x(t)e^{-j2\pi ft}\,dt$, which drops the $2\pi$ from the inverse.)

## Essential pairs

| $x(t)$ | $X(\omega)$ |
|---|---|
| $\delta(t)$ | $1$ (all frequencies equally) |
| $1$ (DC) | $2\pi\,\delta(\omega)$ |
| $e^{-at}u(t)$, $a > 0$ | $\dfrac{1}{a + j\omega}$ |
| rectangular pulse, height 1, width $T$ | $T\,\dfrac{\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega T/2}$ (a sinc) |
| $\cos\omega_0 t$ | $\pi[\delta(\omega - \omega_0) + \delta(\omega + \omega_0)]$ |

### The pulse and its sinc

A rectangular pulse of width $T$ has a sinc-shaped spectrum with its **first zero at $f = 1/T$**. A short pulse has a wide spectrum; a long pulse, a narrow one.

```plot
x: -4, 4, f · T
y: -0.3, 1.1, X(f) / T
f: sinc(x) | X(f) / T
f: abs(sinc(x)) | magnitude | dash
caption: The spectrum of a rectangular pulse. Most energy lies within $|f| < 1/T$; the first nulls are at $f = \pm1/T$.
```

This is the **time–bandwidth tradeoff**: you can't have a signal that's short in time *and* narrow in frequency. A 1 ns digital pulse occupies roughly 1 GHz of bandwidth; a radar that wants fine range resolution needs wide bandwidth.

## Properties that do the work

| Property | Time domain | Frequency domain |
|---|---|---|
| Linearity | $ax + by$ | $aX + bY$ |
| Time shift | $x(t - t_0)$ | $e^{-j\omega t_0}X(\omega)$: same magnitude, linear phase |
| Scaling | $x(at)$ | $\frac{1}{|a|}X(\omega/a)$: squeeze in time, stretch in frequency |
| Modulation | $x(t)\cos\omega_0t$ | $\tfrac12[X(\omega - \omega_0) + X(\omega + \omega_0)]$ |
| **Convolution** | $x * h$ | $X(\omega)H(\omega)$ |
| Multiplication | $x\,y$ | $\frac{1}{2\pi}X * Y$ |
| Duality | $X(t)$ | $2\pi\,x(-\omega)$ |

The **convolution property** is the payoff: the hard operation (convolution) becomes simple multiplication. An LTI system's **frequency response** $H(\omega)$ (the transform of $h(t)$) multiplies the input spectrum. Filtering is literally multiplying spectra.

> **Example.** An RC low-pass has $h(t) = \frac{1}{RC}e^{-t/RC}u(t)$, so $H(\omega) = \dfrac{1}{1 + j\omega RC}$, the same result as the phasor voltage divider in Circuit Analysis II, now derived from the impulse response.

## Key takeaways

- $X(\omega) = \int x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt$ is the signal's spectrum.
- Pulse ↔ sinc; short in time means wide in frequency.
- Convolution in time = multiplication in frequency: $Y = HX$.
