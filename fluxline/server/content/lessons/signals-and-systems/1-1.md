## What is a signal?

A **signal** is a function carrying information: a voltage vs. time $x(t)$, a sequence of samples $x[n]$, an image $x[m,n]$. This course studies **continuous-time** signals $x(t)$, $t\in\mathbb R$, and **discrete-time** signals $x[n]$, $n \in \mathbb Z$, in parallel.

## Energy and power

Signal "size" is measured by energy or average power:

$$
E_x = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |x(t)|^2\,dt,
\qquad
P_x = \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}|x(t)|^2\,dt
$$

- **Energy signals**: $0 < E < \infty$, so $P = 0$. (Pulses, decaying transients.)
- **Power signals**: $0 < P < \infty$, so $E = \infty$. (Periodic signals, DC.)

> $x(t) = e^{-2t}u(t)$: $E = \int_0^\infty e^{-4t}\,dt = \tfrac14$. An energy signal.
>
> $x(t) = A\cos(\omega_0 t)$: $P = A^2/2$. A power signal — this is the RMS² from circuits.

## Other classifications

- **Periodic**: $x(t) = x(t + T)$ for all $t$. The smallest such $T$ is the fundamental period. A sum of periodic signals is periodic only if the ratio of periods is rational.
- **Even/odd**: $x(-t) = x(t)$ or $x(-t) = -x(t)$. Every signal decomposes as $x = x_e + x_o$ with $x_e(t) = \tfrac12[x(t) + x(-t)]$, $x_o(t) = \tfrac12[x(t) - x(-t)]$.
- **Deterministic vs. random**: random signals (noise) are studied in Probability & Random Processes.

## Elementary signals

- Unit step $u(t)$: 0 for $t<0$, 1 for $t>0$.
- Unit impulse $\delta(t)$: zero width, unit area; $\int x(t)\delta(t - t_0)\,dt = x(t_0)$ (sifting property).
- Complex exponential $e^{st}$, $s = \sigma + j\omega$: includes DC, exponentials, sinusoids, and damped sinusoids as special cases. It is the most important signal in the course.

## Transformations of the independent variable

| Operation | Effect |
|---|---|
| $x(t - t_0)$ | delay by $t_0$ (shift right if $t_0>0$) |
| $x(-t)$ | time reversal |
| $x(at)$ | compress if $|a|>1$, expand if $|a|<1$ |

For a combination like $y(t) = x(at - b)$: **shift first, then scale.** Define $v(t) = x(t - b)$, then $y(t) = v(at)$. A reliable check: find where $y$'s argument equals a known point of $x$.

> If $x(t)$ is a pulse on $[0, 2]$, then $y(t) = x(2t - 2)$ is nonzero for $0 \le 2t - 2 \le 2$, i.e., $t\in[1, 2]$.

## Practice

```quiz
? What is the energy of $x(t) = 3e^{-t}u(t)$?
= 4.5 | 0.01 |
> $\int_0^\infty 9e^{-2t}\,dt = 9/2 = 4.5$.
```

```quiz
? What is the average power of $x(t) = 4\cos(10t) $?
= 8 | 0.01 |
> $A^2/2 = 16/2 = 8$.
```

```quiz
? If $x(t)$ is nonzero only on $[0, 4]$, on what interval is $x(2t + 2)$ nonzero? Enter the interval's left endpoint.
= -1 | 0.001 |
> $0 \le 2t + 2 \le 4 \Rightarrow -1 \le t \le 1$.
```

```quiz
? Is $x(t) = \cos(2t) + \cos(\pi t)$ periodic?
- Yes, with period $2\pi$
- Yes, with period $2$
+ No, the ratio of the periods is irrational
- Only for $t > 0$
> Periods are $\pi$ and $2$; $\pi/2$ is irrational, so there is no common period.
```

## Key takeaways

- Energy signals have finite $E$; power signals have finite nonzero $P$.
- $e^{st}$ unifies DC, exponentials, and sinusoids.
- For $x(at - b)$, shift then scale — or solve for the argument's range directly.
