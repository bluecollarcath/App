## The spectrum of a sequence

For a discrete-time signal $x[n]$, the **discrete-time Fourier transform (DTFT)** is

$$
X(e^{j\omega}) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n]\,e^{-j\omega n},
\qquad
x[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} X(e^{j\omega})\,e^{j\omega n}\,d\omega
$$

Here $\omega$ is in **radians per sample**, not radians per second.

### The big difference: periodicity

$X(e^{j\omega})$ is **always periodic with period $2\pi$**, because $e^{-j(\omega + 2\pi)n} = e^{-j\omega n}$ for integer $n$. So every DTFT is fully described by $-\pi \le \omega \le \pi$:

- $\omega = 0$ is DC,
- $\omega = \pi$ is the fastest possible oscillation, $+1, -1, +1, -1, \ldots$

If the samples came from sampling at $f_s$, then $\omega = \pi$ corresponds to $f = f_s/2$, the Nyquist frequency. Frequency in Hz is $f = \omega f_s/2\pi$.

## Frequency response of digital filters

The DTFT of a filter's impulse response is its **frequency response** $H(e^{j\omega})$, the factor it applies to each frequency, just like $H(\omega)$ in continuous time.

> **Two-point average:** $h = [\tfrac12, \tfrac12]$.
> $$
> H(e^{j\omega}) = \tfrac12\left(1 + e^{-j\omega}\right) = e^{-j\omega/2}\cos(\omega/2)
> $$
> $|H| = 1$ at DC and $0$ at $\omega = \pi$: a low-pass filter. The $e^{-j\omega/2}$ factor is a half-sample delay.

> **First-order IIR:** $h[n] = a^nu[n]$, from $y[n] = a\,y[n-1] + x[n]$.
> $$
> H(e^{j\omega}) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a^ne^{-j\omega n} = \frac{1}{1 - a\,e^{-j\omega}}, \qquad |a| < 1
> $$
> With $a = 0.5$: $|H| = 2$ at DC and $1/1.5 = 0.667$ at $\omega = \pi$. Positive $a$ gives a low-pass filter; negative $a$ gives a high-pass.

```plot
x: 0, 3.1416, ω (rad/sample)
y: 0, 2.1, |H(e^{jω})|
f: abs(cos(x/2)) | two-point average
f: 1/sqrt(1.25 - cos(x)) | IIR, a = 0.5
vline: 3.1416, ω = π
caption: Both filters are low-pass: they pass DC and attenuate frequencies near $\omega = \pi$.
```

(For the IIR filter, $|1 - 0.5e^{-j\omega}|^2 = 1.25 - \cos\omega$.)

## DTFT and DFT

The DTFT is a continuous function of $\omega$, which a computer can't store. The **discrete Fourier transform (DFT)** samples the DTFT at $N$ equally spaced frequencies, $\omega_k = 2\pi k/N$, and the **FFT** computes it fast. You'll use both heavily in Digital Signal Processing.

```quiz
? Why is every DTFT periodic in $\omega$ with period $2\pi$?
+ Because $e^{-j(\omega+2\pi)n} = e^{-j\omega n}$ for every integer $n$
- Because all discrete signals are periodic
- Because of the Nyquist theorem
- Because the DTFT only exists for finite signals
> Discrete-time frequencies $2\pi$ apart are indistinguishable, which is aliasing seen from the math side.
```

## Key takeaways

- $X(e^{j\omega}) = \sum x[n]e^{-j\omega n}$; $\omega$ is in radians per sample and the DTFT repeats every $2\pi$.
- $\omega = \pi$ is the Nyquist frequency $f_s/2$.
- A filter's frequency response is the DTFT of its impulse response.
