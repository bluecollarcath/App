## The bridge between analog and digital

Every digital audio recording, phone call, camera image, and sensor reading starts by **sampling** a continuous signal. This lesson derives *why* sampling works, what goes wrong when it's done too slowly, and how the original signal is rebuilt.

## Sampling multiplies by an impulse train

Model ideal sampling at rate $f_s = 1/T_s$ as multiplying $x(t)$ by a train of impulses:

$$
x_s(t) = x(t)\sum_{n} \delta(t - nT_s) = \sum_n x(nT_s)\,\delta(t - nT_s)
$$

An impulse train in time is an impulse train in frequency (spaced $f_s$ apart), and multiplication in time is convolution in frequency. So the sampled spectrum is the original spectrum **copied at every multiple of $f_s$**:

$$
X_s(f) = f_s\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} X(f - kf_s)
$$

## The sampling theorem

> A signal bandlimited to $W$ Hz is completely determined by its samples if $f_s > 2W$.

$2W$ is the **Nyquist rate**. Below it, spectral copies overlap: **aliasing**. A tone at $f_0$ then appears at $|f_0 - kf_s|$ for whichever integer $k$ puts it in $[0, f_s/2]$.

## Reconstruction

**Ideal:** pass the samples through an ideal low-pass filter with cutoff $f_s/2$. In time, that filter's impulse response is a sinc, so

$$
x(t) = \sum_n x(nT_s)\,\operatorname{sinc}\!\left(\frac{t - nT_s}{T_s}\right)
$$

Every sample contributes a sinc pulse, and they add up to the original signal exactly between the samples.

**Practical:** real DACs use a **zero-order hold**, holding each sample flat for one period (a staircase), followed by a smoothing filter. The hold acts like a filter with response $|\operatorname{sinc}(f/f_s)|$, which droops toward high frequencies: at $f_s/2$ the gain is $2/\pi = 0.637$, which is −3.9 dB. Designers compensate with a pre-emphasis filter, or by **oversampling**, running the converter much faster than the Nyquist rate so the band of interest stays where the droop is small and the analog filters can be gentle.

> **Why CDs use 44.1 kHz:** human hearing reaches about 20 kHz, so the Nyquist rate is 40 kHz. The extra 4.1 kHz leaves a transition band for the analog anti-aliasing filter to roll off.

## Key takeaways

- Sampling copies the spectrum at every multiple of $f_s$.
- No aliasing if $f_s > 2W$; aliases appear at $|f_0 - kf_s|$.
- Ideal reconstruction is sinc interpolation; real DACs use a zero-order hold, which droops by 3.9 dB at $f_s/2$.
