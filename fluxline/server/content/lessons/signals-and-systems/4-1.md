## Beyond the Fourier transform

The Fourier transform fails for signals that grow, like $e^{t}u(t)$, and for unstable systems, which are exactly the ones a designer needs to spot. The **Laplace transform** generalizes it by allowing complex frequencies $s = \sigma + j\omega$:

$$
X(s) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)\,e^{-st}\,dt
$$

The factor $e^{-\sigma t}$ tames growing signals. On the $j\omega$ axis ($\sigma = 0$) the Laplace transform *is* the Fourier transform. (Circuit Analysis II used the one-sided version starting at $t = 0$, which handles initial conditions. Here we use the two-sided version to study systems.)

## The region of convergence

The integral converges only for some values of $s$, and that set, the **region of convergence (ROC)**, is part of the answer:

$$
e^{-at}u(t) \;\leftrightarrow\; \frac{1}{s + a},\quad \text{ROC: } \operatorname{Re}\{s\} > -a
$$
$$
-e^{-at}u(-t) \;\leftrightarrow\; \frac{1}{s + a},\quad \text{ROC: } \operatorname{Re}\{s\} < -a
$$

Same formula, different signals: the ROC tells them apart. Rules worth memorizing:

- The ROC never contains a pole.
- A **right-sided** (e.g. causal) signal has an ROC to the **right** of its rightmost pole.
- A **left-sided** signal has an ROC to the **left** of its leftmost pole.

## The system function

For an LTI system, $H(s) = \mathcal L\{h(t)\}$ is the **system function**, and $Y(s) = H(s)X(s)$. Systems described by linear differential equations have rational system functions: transform term by term with $d/dt \to s$.

> **Worked example.** $\dfrac{d^2y}{dt^2} + 3\dfrac{dy}{dt} + 2y = x(t)$
>
> $$
> (s^2 + 3s + 2)\,Y(s) = X(s) \quad\Rightarrow\quad H(s) = \frac{1}{s^2 + 3s + 2} = \frac{1}{(s + 1)(s + 2)}
> $$
>
> Poles at $-1$ and $-2$. If the system is causal, the ROC is $\operatorname{Re}\{s\} > -1$.
>
> Partial fractions: $H(s) = \dfrac{1}{s+1} - \dfrac{1}{s+2}$, so $h(t) = (e^{-t} - e^{-2t})u(t)$.
>
> DC gain: $H(0) = 1/2$. A unit step input settles at $y = 0.5$.

## Causality and stability from the ROC

| Property | Condition |
|---|---|
| Causal | ROC is a right half-plane (to the right of the rightmost pole) |
| Stable | ROC contains the $j\omega$ axis |
| **Causal and stable** | **all poles in the left half-plane** |

This is the stability test used throughout control and circuit design: for a causal system, check that every pole has a negative real part.

## Key takeaways

- $X(s) = \int x(t)e^{-st}\,dt$; the ROC is part of the transform.
- Differential equation → $H(s)$ by replacing $d/dt$ with $s$.
- Causal and stable ⇔ all poles in the left half-plane.
