## From analog to digital

Every DSP system starts at an **analog-to-digital converter**, which performs two separate operations: **sampling** (discretizing time) and **quantization** (discretizing amplitude). Each introduces its own error.

## Sampling and the Nyquist theorem

Sampling $x(t)$ every $T_s$ seconds gives $x[n] = x(nT_s)$, at rate $f_s = 1/T_s$. In the frequency domain, sampling **replicates the spectrum** at every multiple of $f_s$:

$$
X_s(f) = f_s\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} X(f - kf_s)
$$

If $x(t)$ is bandlimited to $f_{max}$, the copies don't overlap — and $x(t)$ can be perfectly reconstructed — provided

$$
\boxed{f_s > 2f_{max}}
$$

$2f_{max}$ is the **Nyquist rate**; $f_s/2$ is the **Nyquist frequency**.

## Aliasing

If the condition is violated, spectral copies overlap and high frequencies masquerade as low ones. A sinusoid at $f_0$ sampled at $f_s$ appears at

$$
f_{alias} = \left| f_0 - k f_s \right| \quad \text{for the integer } k \text{ that puts it in } [0, f_s/2]
$$

> A 7 kHz tone sampled at 10 kHz appears at $|7 - 10| = 3$ kHz. Once sampled, it is *indistinguishable* from a real 3 kHz tone — no digital processing can undo aliasing.

```plot
x: 0, 1, t (ms)
y: -1.25, 1.25, amplitude
f: cos(2*pi*7*x) | 7 kHz tone
f: cos(2*pi*3*x) | 3 kHz alias | dash
samples: cos(2*pi*7*x) | 0.1 | samples at 10 kHz
caption: Sampled at 10 kHz, the 7 kHz tone (solid) and a 3 kHz tone (dashed) pass through exactly the same points.
```

This is why every ADC is preceded by an analog **anti-aliasing filter**. CD audio samples at 44.1 kHz, leaving a transition band from 20 kHz to 22.05 kHz for that filter.

## Quantization

An $N$-bit quantizer maps each sample to one of $2^N$ levels with step size $\Delta = V_{FS}/2^N$. The error is bounded by $\pm\Delta/2$ and, for busy signals, is well modeled as uniform noise with power $\Delta^2/12$. For a full-scale sinusoid, the signal-to-quantization-noise ratio is

$$
\text{SQNR} \approx 6.02N + 1.76\ \text{dB}
$$

**Each extra bit buys about 6 dB.**

## Practice

```quiz
? A 7 kHz tone is sampled at 10 kHz. At what frequency (kHz) does it appear?
= 3 | 0.001 | kHz
> $|7 - 10| = 3$ kHz.
```

```quiz
? What is the theoretical SQNR of an ideal 16-bit ADC with a full-scale sine input, in dB?
= 98.08 | 0.01 | dB
> $6.02(16) + 1.76 = 98.08$ dB.
```

```quiz
? A 12-bit ADC has a 3.3 V full-scale range. What is its step size $\Delta$, in mV?
= 0.8057 | 0.01 | mV
> $3.3/4096 = 0.806$ mV.
```

```quiz
? A signal contains components up to 18 kHz. What is the minimum sampling rate to avoid aliasing, in kHz?
= 36 | 0.001 | kHz
> Nyquist rate $= 2f_{max} = 36$ kHz (in practice, sample somewhat higher).
```

## Key takeaways

- Sample above $2f_{max}$; filter before sampling.
- Aliased frequencies: $|f_0 - kf_s|$; aliasing is irreversible.
- SQNR ≈ 6.02N + 1.76 dB.
