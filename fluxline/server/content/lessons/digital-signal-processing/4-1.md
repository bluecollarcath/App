## Changing the sampling rate

Real systems constantly convert between sampling rates: 48 kHz audio for 44.1 kHz CDs, a 100 MHz radio ADC feeding a 1 MHz baseband processor, an image resized for a screen. **Multirate** DSP does this digitally, without going back to analog.

## Decimation (downsampling)

Keeping every $M$th sample, $y[n] = x[Mn]$, divides the sampling rate by $M$. But the new Nyquist frequency is $M$ times lower, so anything above it **aliases**. Every decimator therefore has two parts:

1. a digital **anti-aliasing lowpass filter** with cutoff $\pi/M$ (that is, $f_s/2M$),
2. then the **downsampler** ($\downarrow M$).

> **Example.** Reducing 48 kHz audio to 8 kHz for telephony: $M = 6$. Filter to below 4 kHz first, then keep every sixth sample.

## Interpolation (upsampling)

Inserting $L - 1$ zeros between samples ($\uparrow L$) raises the sampling rate by $L$, but the zero-stuffed signal's spectrum contains $L - 1$ unwanted **images** of the original spectrum. An **interpolation filter** (lowpass, cutoff $\pi/L$, gain $L$) removes them, filling in the zeros with smoothly interpolated values.

## Rational rate changes

To change by a factor $L/M$: upsample by $L$, filter once (the cutoff is the smaller of $\pi/L$ and $\pi/M$), then downsample by $M$.

> **48 kHz to 44.1 kHz.** $44100/48000 = 147/160$, so $L = 147$, $M = 160$. Done naively that means running a filter at $48 \times 147 = 7.06$ MHz, which is wasteful, since most of the samples are zeros going in and most of the outputs are thrown away.

## Polyphase filters

The fix is to avoid computing what you'll discard. Split the FIR filter into $M$ **polyphase** sub-filters, each taking every $M$th coefficient. In a decimator, each sub-filter runs at the **low** output rate on its share of the input, and their outputs are summed. The result is identical, with $M$ times less computation. Interpolators work the same way in reverse: each output phase is produced by its own short sub-filter, never multiplying by the inserted zeros.

Polyphase structures underlie every efficient sample-rate converter, the channelizers in software radios and cellular base stations (a polyphase filter bank plus an FFT splits a wide band into hundreds of channels at once), and OFDM.

## Oversampling

Multirate ideas also improve converters:

- **Oversampling ADCs** sample far above the Nyquist rate, which makes the analog anti-aliasing filter trivial (the hard filtering is done digitally, then the rate is decimated). Spreading the quantization noise over a wider band also lowers the in-band noise by $10\log_{10}(\text{OSR})$ dB: 6 dB (one bit) for every 4× oversampling.
- **Sigma-delta converters** add noise shaping that pushes quantization noise out of band, reaching 24-bit audio resolution from a 1-bit quantizer running at a few MHz.

## Key takeaways

- Decimate by $M$: lowpass to $\pi/M$, then keep every $M$th sample.
- Interpolate by $L$: insert zeros, then lowpass (cutoff $\pi/L$, gain $L$) to remove images.
- Rational $L/M$ conversion combines both; polyphase structures cut the work by the rate factor.
- Oversampling gains $10\log_{10}(\text{OSR})$ dB of quantization SNR; sigma-delta noise shaping gains far more.
