## Four equations for all of electromagnetics

Everything so far, from Gauss's law to Ampère's law, is a special case of four equations that James Clerk Maxwell assembled in the 1860s. His one addition, the **displacement current**, turned four separate laws into a theory of waves, and predicted that light is an electromagnetic wave.

| Law | Point form | Integral form |
|---|---|---|
| Gauss (electric) | $\nabla\cdot\mathbf D = \rho_v$ | $\oint_S\mathbf D\cdot d\mathbf s = Q_{enc}$ |
| Gauss (magnetic) | $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B = 0$ | $\oint_S\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf s = 0$ |
| Faraday | $\nabla\times\mathbf E = -\dfrac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial t}$ | $\oint_C\mathbf E\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell = -\dfrac{d}{dt}\displaystyle\int_S\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf s$ |
| Ampère–Maxwell | $\nabla\times\mathbf H = \mathbf J + \dfrac{\partial\mathbf D}{\partial t}$ | $\oint_C\mathbf H\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell = I_{enc} + \dfrac{d}{dt}\displaystyle\int_S\mathbf D\cdot d\mathbf s$ |

with the material relations $\mathbf D = \varepsilon\mathbf E$, $\mathbf B = \mu\mathbf H$, $\mathbf J = \sigma\mathbf E$.

## Faraday's law: changing B makes circulating E

A time-varying magnetic flux induces an EMF around any loop that links it. That's the principle of transformers, generators, inductors and wireless chargers. The minus sign is **Lenz's law**: the induced current opposes the change in flux.

> **Example.** A 0.01 m² loop sits in a uniform field $B = 0.5\sin(377t)$ T perpendicular to it. The EMF is $-0.01 \times 0.5 \times 377\cos(377t)$, a 1.885 V peak sine at 60 Hz.

Faraday's law also says that once fields vary in time, $\nabla\times\mathbf E \ne 0$: the voltage between two points now depends on the path. That's why the ground "voltage" on a fast circuit board depends on how the probe's ground lead is routed.

## Displacement current: Maxwell's fix

Ampère's original law, $\nabla\times\mathbf H = \mathbf J$, is inconsistent with charge conservation. The divergence of a curl is zero, so it would require $\nabla\cdot\mathbf J = 0$ always, but charge piling up on a capacitor plate has $\nabla\cdot\mathbf J = -\partial\rho_v/\partial t \ne 0$.

Maxwell added the term $\partial\mathbf D/\partial t$, the **displacement current density**. Consider a capacitor being charged: conduction current flows in the wires, but none crosses the gap. In the gap, the changing $\mathbf D$ carries an equal displacement current, so Ampère's law gives the same $\mathbf H$ whichever surface you choose.

$$
i_d = \varepsilon A\frac{dE}{dt} = C\frac{dv}{dt} = i_c
$$

More importantly, the new term lets a changing $\mathbf E$ create $\mathbf H$, just as Faraday's law lets a changing $\mathbf H$ create $\mathbf E$. Each regenerates the other: a self-sustaining **wave** that needs no charges or wires at all. That's the next lesson.

## Continuity

Taking the divergence of the Ampère–Maxwell law and using Gauss's law gives conservation of charge:

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf J = -\frac{\partial\rho_v}{\partial t}
$$

Current flowing out of a region must deplete the charge inside. Kirchhoff's current law is the special case where no charge accumulates.

## Time-harmonic (phasor) form

For sinusoidal fields at angular frequency $\omega$, replace $\partial/\partial t$ by $j\omega$:

$$
\nabla\times\mathbf E = -j\omega\mu\mathbf H, \qquad
\nabla\times\mathbf H = (\sigma + j\omega\varepsilon)\,\mathbf E
$$

The ratio of conduction to displacement current, $\sigma/(\omega\varepsilon)$ (the **loss tangent**), classifies a material at a given frequency:

- $\sigma/\omega\varepsilon \gg 1$: **good conductor**. Copper at 1 GHz: $10^9$.
- $\sigma/\omega\varepsilon \ll 1$: **good dielectric** (low-loss insulator).

> Seawater ($\sigma = 4$ S/m, $\varepsilon_r = 81$) at 1 MHz: $\sigma/\omega\varepsilon = 888$, a good conductor, which is why radio barely penetrates the ocean and submarines use very low frequencies.

## Key takeaways

- Four laws: two divergences (sources) and two curls (circulation), plus $\mathbf D = \varepsilon\mathbf E$, $\mathbf B = \mu\mathbf H$, $\mathbf J = \sigma\mathbf E$.
- Faraday: changing flux induces EMF; voltage becomes path-dependent.
- Displacement current $\partial\mathbf D/\partial t$ keeps Ampère's law consistent with charge conservation and makes waves possible.
- Phasors: $\partial/\partial t \to j\omega$; $\sigma/\omega\varepsilon$ sorts conductors from dielectrics.
