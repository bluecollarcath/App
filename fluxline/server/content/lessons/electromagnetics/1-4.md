## Steps instead of sines

Digital signals aren't sinusoids; they're edges. When a step is launched onto a transmission line, it travels as a wave, reflects at each mismatch, and bounces back and forth until the line settles at its DC value. Those bounces are what cause **ringing**, false clock edges and failed links on fast circuit boards. Analyzing them is called **signal integrity**.

## The first wave

Close a switch at $t = 0$ connecting a source $V_s$ with internal resistance $R_s$ to a line of impedance $Z_0$. Before any reflection returns, the line just looks like a resistor $Z_0$, so the first wave launched is set by a voltage divider:

$$
V_1^+ = V_s\,\frac{Z_0}{R_s + Z_0}
$$

It reaches the load after the one-way delay $T = \ell/u_p$, where it reflects with $\Gamma_L$. The reflected wave returns to the source at $2T$ and reflects again with

$$
\Gamma_s = \frac{R_s - Z_0}{R_s + Z_0}
$$

and so on. The voltage at any point is the **sum** of every wave that has passed it so far.

## The bounce (lattice) diagram

Draw distance across and time downward; each wave is a diagonal line labeled with its amplitude.

> **Example.** $V_s = 10$ V, $R_s = 25\ \Omega$, $Z_0 = 50\ \Omega$, $R_L = 150\ \Omega$.
>
> $V_1^+ = 10 \times 50/75 = 6.67$ V; $\Gamma_L = 100/200 = 0.5$; $\Gamma_s = -25/75 = -1/3$.

```circuit
LINE 0,-0.4 0,4.6
LINE 4,-0.4 4,4.6
TEXT 0,-0.75 "z = 0 (Γ_s = −1/3)"
TEXT 4,-0.75 "z = ℓ (Γ_L = 0.5)"
ARROW 0,0 4,1 "+6.67 V" color=anno
ARROW 4,1 0,2 "+3.33 V" side=-1 color=anno
ARROW 0,2 4,3 "−1.11 V" color=anno
ARROW 4,3 0,4 "−0.56 V" side=-1 color=anno
TEXT -0.2,0 0 anchor=end color=muted size=12
TEXT -0.2,2 2T anchor=end color=muted size=12
TEXT -0.2,4 4T anchor=end color=muted size=12
TEXT 4.2,1 T anchor=start color=muted size=12
TEXT 4.2,3 3T anchor=start color=muted size=12
caption: Bounce diagram for the example. Each reflection multiplies the wave by the $\Gamma$ at that end.
```

Adding the waves at the load:

| Time | Load voltage |
|---|---|
| $0 < t < T$ | 0 |
| $T$ | $6.67 + 3.33 = 10.00$ V |
| $3T$ | $10.00 - 1.11 - 0.56 = 8.33$ V |
| $5T$ | $8.33 + 0.19 + 0.09 = 8.61$ V |
| $t \to \infty$ | $10 \times 150/175 = 8.57$ V |

The load overshoots to 10 V, then rings around the DC value it would have with no line at all.

## Terminating to kill reflections

Reflections need a mismatch at **both** ends to keep bouncing. Fix either end and the ringing stops.

- **Parallel (end) termination:** $R_L = Z_0$ at the receiver. Clean, but it draws DC current all the time.
- **Series (source) termination:** make $R_s = Z_0$ (add a resistor to the driver's own low output resistance). The first wave is only **half** the swing, but at an open, high-impedance receiver it doubles to the full value at $T$, and the returning reflection is absorbed at the source. No DC power, and the standard choice for point-to-point CMOS links.
- **Differential links** (USB, PCIe, Ethernet) use a 100 Ω differential termination at the receiver.

The same physics gives the **time-domain reflectometer** (TDR): launch a fast step and watch the reflections return. The delay of each reflection gives the distance to a fault, and its sign and size tell you what it is (a short, an open, a bad connector).

## Practice

```quiz
? A 5 V step source with $R_s = 50\ \Omega$ drives a 50 Ω line. What is the amplitude of the first wave, in V?
= 2.5 | 0.001 | V
> $5 \times 50/(50 + 50) = 2.5$ V.
```

```quiz
? That line (series-terminated) ends in an open circuit. What voltage appears at the open end at $t = T$, in V?
= 5 | 0.001 | V
> $\Gamma_L = +1$, so the wave doubles: $2.5 + 2.5 = 5$ V. The reflection is then absorbed at the matched source.
```

```quiz
? $V_s = 12$ V, $R_s = 10\ \Omega$, $R_L = 50\ \Omega$ on a 75 Ω line. What is the final, steady-state load voltage, in V?
= 10 | 0.001 | V
> At DC the line is just a wire: $12 \times 50/60 = 10$ V.
```

```quiz
? A TDR sees a reflection return 4 ns after launch, on a cable with $u_p = 1.5\times10^8$ m/s. How far away is the fault, in m?
= 0.3 | 0.001 | m
> Round trip: $d = u_pt/2 = 1.5\times10^8 \times 4\times10^{-9}/2 = 0.3$ m.
```

```quiz
? A TDR reflection is negative (the voltage steps down). The fault is most likely:
+ a lower impedance, such as a short or excess capacitance
- a higher impedance, such as an open
- a perfect match
- a resistive load equal to $Z_0$
> $\Gamma < 0$ when the impedance drops below $Z_0$.
```

## Key takeaways

- First wave: $V_1^+ = V_sZ_0/(R_s + Z_0)$; each end reflects with its own $\Gamma$.
- Bounce diagrams track the waves; the voltage at a point is the sum of those that have arrived.
- Ringing needs mismatches at both ends; series termination ($R_s = Z_0$) or end termination ($R_L = Z_0$) stops it.
- TDR locates faults from reflection delay and sign.
