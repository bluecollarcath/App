## What happens at the end of the line

A wave traveling down a line with characteristic impedance $Z_0$ carries voltage and current in the fixed ratio $V^+/I^+ = Z_0$. At the load, Ohm's law demands $V/I = Z_L$. If $Z_L \ne Z_0$, the only way to satisfy both is for part of the wave to **reflect**. The **reflection coefficient** at the load is

$$
\Gamma_L = \frac{V_0^-}{V_0^+} = \frac{Z_L - Z_0}{Z_L + Z_0}
$$

| Load | $\Gamma_L$ | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| matched, $Z_L = Z_0$ | 0 | all power absorbed, no reflection |
| short, $Z_L = 0$ | $-1$ | total reflection, inverted voltage |
| open, $Z_L = \infty$ | $+1$ | total reflection, same polarity |
| resistive $R_L > Z_0$ | real, between 0 and 1 | partial reflection |
| reactive (pure L or C) | $|\Gamma| = 1$ | total reflection with a phase shift |

The fraction of incident power reflected is $|\Gamma_L|^2$. The **return loss** expresses the reflection in decibels: $\text{RL} = -20\log_{10}|\Gamma_L|$ (bigger is better).

## Standing waves and VSWR

The forward and reflected waves add. A distance $d$ back from the load, the voltage magnitude is

$$
|V(d)| = |V_0^+|\,\big|1 + \Gamma_Le^{-j2\beta d}\big|
$$

As $d$ changes, the reflected wave slides in and out of phase with the forward wave, creating a **standing wave** pattern with maxima $|V_0^+|(1 + |\Gamma|)$ and minima $|V_0^+|(1 - |\Gamma|)$, repeating every **half wavelength**. Their ratio is the **voltage standing-wave ratio**:

$$
\text{VSWR} = \frac{1 + |\Gamma|}{1 - |\Gamma|}
$$

VSWR runs from 1 (perfect match) to $\infty$ (total reflection). A spec of "VSWR < 2" means $|\Gamma| < 1/3$: no more than 11% of the power reflected.

> **Example.** A 100 Ω load on a 50 Ω line: $\Gamma_L = 50/150 = 1/3$, VSWR $= 2$, return loss $= 9.5$ dB, and 11.1% of the incident power is reflected.

## Input impedance

Looking into a lossless line of length $\ell$ terminated in $Z_L$:

$$
Z_{in} = Z_0\,\frac{Z_L + jZ_0\tan\beta\ell}{Z_0 + jZ_L\tan\beta\ell}
$$

The line **transforms** the load impedance, and the transformation depends on the electrical length $\beta\ell = 2\pi\ell/\lambda$. Special cases worth memorizing:

- **Matched load:** $Z_{in} = Z_0$ for any length.
- **Half-wave line** ($\ell = \lambda/2$): $Z_{in} = Z_L$. The line is "invisible."
- **Shorted stub:** $Z_{in} = jZ_0\tan\beta\ell$, a pure reactance: inductive for $\ell < \lambda/4$.
- **Open stub:** $Z_{in} = -jZ_0\cot\beta\ell$: capacitive for $\ell < \lambda/4$.
- **Quarter-wave line** ($\ell = \lambda/4$): $Z_{in} = Z_0^2/Z_L$. It **inverts** the impedance: a short looks open, and vice versa.

> **Example.** $Z_L = 25 + j25\ \Omega$ on a 50 Ω line: $\Gamma_L = -0.2 + j0.4$, $|\Gamma_L| = 0.447$, VSWR $= 2.62$. An eighth of a wavelength back ($\tan\beta\ell = 1$):
> $$
> Z_{in} = 50\,\frac{25 + j25 + j50}{50 + j(25 + j25)} = 50\,\frac{25 + j75}{25 + j25} = 100 + j50\ \Omega
> $$

## The quarter-wave transformer

The inversion property gives a simple matching network. To match a real load $R_L$ to a line $Z_0$, insert a quarter-wave section with

$$
Z_1 = \sqrt{Z_0R_L}
$$

Then $Z_{in} = Z_1^2/R_L = Z_0$. To match 100 Ω to 50 Ω, use a $\sqrt{5000} = 70.7\ \Omega$ section. The match is perfect only at the design frequency (where the section is exactly $\lambda/4$), so it's a narrowband technique; cascading several sections widens the bandwidth.

## Key takeaways

- $\Gamma_L = (Z_L - Z_0)/(Z_L + Z_0)$; $|\Gamma|^2$ of the power reflects.
- Standing waves repeat every $\lambda/2$; $\text{VSWR} = (1 + |\Gamma|)/(1 - |\Gamma|)$.
- $Z_{in} = Z_0(Z_L + jZ_0\tan\beta\ell)/(Z_0 + jZ_L\tan\beta\ell)$; $\lambda/2$ repeats, $\lambda/4$ inverts.
- Quarter-wave transformer: $Z_1 = \sqrt{Z_0R_L}$.
