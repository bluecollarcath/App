## When is a wire not just a wire?

In circuit analysis, a wire connects two points at the same potential instantly. That assumption fails when the wire's length is a significant fraction of a wavelength, or when signal edges are faster than the time it takes a signal to travel the wire. Rule of thumb: use transmission-line theory when

$$
\ell > \frac{\lambda}{10} \quad\text{or}\quad t_{rise} < 2\,t_{delay}
$$

At 5 GHz on a PCB ($\lambda \approx 3$ cm), that's a 3 mm trace. Every high-speed digital link, RF circuit, and power line is a transmission line.

## The distributed model

Model a short length $\Delta z$ of line by per-unit-length parameters: series resistance $R'$ (Ω/m) and inductance $L'$ (H/m), shunt conductance $G'$ (S/m) and capacitance $C'$ (F/m). Applying KVL and KCL and letting $\Delta z \to 0$ gives the **telegrapher's equations**:

$$
-\frac{\partial v}{\partial z} = R'i + L'\frac{\partial i}{\partial t},
\qquad
-\frac{\partial i}{\partial z} = G'v + C'\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}
$$

## Wave solutions

In phasor form these combine into wave equations with solutions

$$
\mathbf{V}(z) = V_0^+e^{-\gamma z} + V_0^-e^{+\gamma z}
$$

— a forward wave and a reflected wave — with **propagation constant**

$$
\gamma = \alpha + j\beta = \sqrt{(R' + j\omega L')(G' + j\omega C')}
$$

The ratio of voltage to current *for a single traveling wave* is the **characteristic impedance**:

$$
Z_0 = \sqrt{\frac{R' + j\omega L'}{G' + j\omega C'}}
$$

## The lossless line

With $R' = G' = 0$ (a good approximation for short, low-loss lines):

$$
Z_0 = \sqrt{\frac{L'}{C'}}, \qquad
\beta = \omega\sqrt{L'C'}, \qquad
u_p = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L'C'}} = \frac{c}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_r}}
$$

$Z_0$ is real — a pure resistance — even though the line contains no resistors. A 50 Ω coaxial cable presents 50 Ω to a fast edge regardless of its length (until the reflection returns).

> **Worked example.** A line has $L' = 250$ nH/m and $C' = 100$ pF/m.
>
> $Z_0 = \sqrt{250\times10^{-9}/100\times10^{-12}} = \sqrt{2500} = 50\ \Omega$.
>
> $u_p = 1/\sqrt{2.5\times10^{-17}} = 2\times10^8$ m/s (two-thirds of $c$; $\varepsilon_r = 2.25$, typical of polyethylene coax).
>
> At 1 GHz, $\lambda = u_p/f = 20$ cm.

## Practice

```quiz
? A lossless line has $L' = 300$ nH/m and $C' = 120$ pF/m. What is $Z_0$, in Ω?
= 50 | 0.01 | Ω
> $\sqrt{300\times10^{-9}/120\times10^{-12}} = \sqrt{2500} = 50\ \Omega$.
```

```quiz
? A PCB dielectric has $\varepsilon_r = 4$. What is the propagation velocity, as a fraction of $c$?
= 0.5 | 0.01 |
> $1/\sqrt{4} = 0.5$.
```

```quiz
? Signals travel at $2\times10^8$ m/s on a cable. What is the one-way delay of a 3 m cable, in ns?
= 15 | 0.01 | ns
> $3/(2\times10^8) = 15$ ns.
```

```quiz
? Doubling a lossless line's $C'$ (with $L'$ fixed) changes $Z_0$ by a factor of:
- 2
- 1/2
+ $1/\sqrt2$
- $\sqrt2$
> $Z_0 \propto 1/\sqrt{C'}$.
```

## Key takeaways

- Treat interconnect as a transmission line when it's longer than ~λ/10.
- Lossless: $Z_0 = \sqrt{L'/C'}$, $u_p = 1/\sqrt{L'C'}$.
- Voltage on a line is a superposition of forward and reflected waves.
