## Why switch?

A linear regulator drops excess voltage across a transistor and burns it as heat: converting 12 V to 5 V at 2 A wastes 14 W, an efficiency of 42%. A **switching converter** uses transistors as switches (fully on or fully off, so they dissipate almost nothing) plus inductors and capacitors (which store but don't dissipate energy). Efficiencies of 90–98% are routine.

## The buck converter

A switch connects the input to an LC filter for a fraction $D$ (the **duty cycle**) of each switching period $T_s$; a diode or second switch provides a path for the inductor current the rest of the time. The voltage at the switch node is a square wave between $V_{in}$ and 0, and the LC filter extracts its average.

```circuit

caption: The buck converter. The switch and diode chop $V_{in}$ into a square wave; the $LC$ filter keeps only its average.
```

## Volt-second balance

In periodic steady state, the inductor current returns to the same value each cycle, so the **average inductor voltage is zero**:

$$
\langle v_L \rangle = \frac{1}{T_s}\int_0^{T_s} v_L\,dt = 0
$$

For the buck converter (assuming small ripple, so $v_{out} \approx V_{out}$):

- switch on, for $DT_s$: $v_L = V_{in} - V_{out}$
- switch off, for $(1-D)T_s$: $v_L = -V_{out}$

$$
(V_{in} - V_{out})D + (-V_{out})(1 - D) = 0
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\boxed{V_{out} = D\,V_{in}}
$$

The dual principle, **capacitor charge balance** ($\langle i_C \rangle = 0$), gives current relationships. Together these two rules analyze every converter topology.

## Inductor ripple

During the on-time the inductor current rises with slope $(V_{in} - V_{out})/L$:

$$
\Delta i_L = \frac{(V_{in} - V_{out})\,D\,T_s}{L}
$$

> **Worked example.** 12 V → 5 V at $f_s = 200$ kHz with $L = 10$ µH.
>
> $D = 5/12 = 0.417$. $\Delta i_L = (7)(0.417)(5\ \mu\text{s})/(10\ \mu\text{H}) \approx 1.46$ A peak-to-peak.
>
> Designers usually target 20–40% ripple of the full-load current; for a 4 A load this is about right.

## Other topologies

Applying the same rule to other arrangements of the same parts gives:

| Converter | $V_{out}/V_{in}$ |
|---|---|
| Buck | $D$ |
| Boost | $\dfrac{1}{1 - D}$ |
| Buck-boost (inverting) | $-\dfrac{D}{1 - D}$ |

## Key takeaways

- Switching converters use lossless elements for high efficiency.
- Volt-second and charge balance analyze any converter.
- Buck: $D$; boost: $1/(1-D)$.
