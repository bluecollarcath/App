## The question

A source with Thévenin equivalent $V_{th}$, $R_{th}$ drives a load $R_L$. Which load receives the **most power**? This matters for antennas, sensors, RF amplifiers, solar panels, and audio systems, anywhere signal or energy is scarce.

```circuit
V 0,0 0,2 V_{th} side=-1
R 0,0 2.4,0 R_{th} i=i
R 2.4,0 2.4,2 R_L v=v_L
W 2.4,2 0,2
caption: Any linear source, reduced to its Thévenin equivalent, driving a load.
```

## Derivation

The load current is $i = V_{th}/(R_{th} + R_L)$, so the load power is

$$
p_L = i^2 R_L = \frac{V_{th}^2\,R_L}{(R_{th} + R_L)^2}
$$

Too small an $R_L$ gives lots of current but little voltage; too large gives voltage but little current. Set the derivative to zero:

$$
\frac{dp_L}{dR_L} = V_{th}^2\,\frac{(R_{th}+R_L)^2 - 2R_L(R_{th}+R_L)}{(R_{th}+R_L)^4} = 0
\;\Rightarrow\; R_{th} + R_L = 2R_L
\;\Rightarrow\; \boxed{R_L = R_{th}}
$$

and the maximum power is

$$
\boxed{p_{L,\max} = \frac{V_{th}^2}{4R_{th}}}
$$

## The efficiency trade-off

The source's internal resistance dissipates power too. Efficiency, the fraction of the delivered power that reaches the load, is

$$
\eta = \frac{p_L}{p_L + p_{R_{th}}} = \frac{R_L}{R_{th} + R_L}
$$

At maximum power transfer, $\eta = 50\%$: half the power is wasted inside the source.

```plot
x: 0, 6, R_L / R_{th}
y: 0, 1.05
f: 4*x/(1 + x)^2 | p_L / p_{L,max}
f: x/(1 + x) | efficiency η | dash
vline: 1, R_L = R_{th}
mark: 1, 1, maximum power, η = 50%
caption: Load power peaks at $R_L = R_{th}$, but efficiency keeps rising as $R_L$ grows.
```

So the right design goal depends on what's scarce:

- **Signals are scarce** (antenna to receiver, sensor to ADC input, RF amplifier stages): match for maximum power. RF systems standardize on 50 Ω for this reason.
- **Energy is cheap at the source but losses are costly** (power grids, battery-powered devices): make $R_L \gg R_{th}$ for high efficiency. A utility operating at maximum power transfer would waste half its generation and overheat its equipment.

In AC circuits (Circuit Analysis II) the condition becomes $\mathbf Z_L = \mathbf Z_{th}^*$, the **complex conjugate**: match the resistance and cancel the reactance.

## Worked example

> A 30 V source in series with 10 Ω feeds a node with 15 Ω to ground; from that node, 4 Ω leads to the load terminals. Choose $R_L$ for maximum power.
>
> $V_{th} = 30\cdot\frac{15}{10+15} = 18$ V, $\quad R_{th} = (10\parallel15) + 4 = 6 + 4 = 10\ \Omega$.
>
> So $R_L = 10\ \Omega$ and $p_{L,\max} = \dfrac{18^2}{4 \cdot 10} = 8.1$ W.


## Key takeaways

- Max power at $R_L = R_{th}$: $p_{max} = V_{th}^2/4R_{th}$.
- Efficiency is only 50% there; $\eta = R_L/(R_{th}+R_L)$.
- Match for signals; avoid matching for bulk power. AC version: $\mathbf Z_L = \mathbf Z_{th}^*$.
