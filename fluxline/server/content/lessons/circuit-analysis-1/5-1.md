## Storing energy instead of burning it

Resistors convert electrical energy to heat. **Capacitors** store energy in an electric field, and **inductors** store it in a magnetic field. Because they can store and release energy, they make circuits depend on *time* and *frequency*: they're the reason filters, oscillators, power converters, and memory work.

## The capacitor

Two conductors separated by an insulator (dielectric). A parallel-plate capacitor has

$$
C = \frac{\varepsilon A}{d} \qquad [\text{F}]
$$

Charge on the plates is proportional to voltage, $q = Cv$, so differentiating:

$$
\boxed{i = C\frac{dv}{dt}}
\qquad\qquad
v(t) = \frac{1}{C}\int_{t_0}^{t} i(\tau)\,d\tau + v(t_0)
$$

Consequences:

- **DC steady state:** if $v$ is constant, $i = 0$. A capacitor is an **open circuit** at DC.
- **Continuity:** an instantaneous jump in $v$ would need infinite current. **Capacitor voltage cannot change instantaneously.**
- **Stored energy:** $w_C = \tfrac12 C v^2$.

> **Worked example.** The voltage across a 1 µF capacitor ramps from 0 to 10 V in 2 ms, then holds.
> During the ramp, $dv/dt = 10/0.002 = 5000$ V/s, so $i = 10^{-6}\times5000 = 5$ mA. After $t = 2$ ms, $i = 0$. Final energy $= \tfrac12(10^{-6})(10^2) = 50\ \mu$J.
>
> ```plot
> x: 0, 4, t (ms)
> y: 0, 11
> f: min(5*x, 10) | v(t) (V)
> f: 5*(u(x) - u(x - 2)) | i(t) (mA) | dash
> caption: The current is proportional to the slope of the voltage, not to the voltage itself.
> ```

## The inductor

A coil of wire. Current produces magnetic flux; the flux linkage is proportional to current, $\lambda = Li$, and Faraday's law gives

$$
\boxed{v = L\frac{di}{dt}}
\qquad\qquad
i(t) = \frac{1}{L}\int_{t_0}^{t} v(\tau)\,d\tau + i(t_0)
$$

The inductor is the **dual** of the capacitor:

- **DC steady state:** constant current means $v = 0$. An inductor is a **short circuit** at DC.
- **Continuity:** **inductor current cannot change instantaneously.** Forcing it to (opening a switch) creates a huge voltage spike.
- **Stored energy:** $w_L = \tfrac12 L i^2$.

| | Capacitor | Inductor |
|---|---|---|
| Law | $i = C\,dv/dt$ | $v = L\,di/dt$ |
| At DC | open circuit | short circuit |
| Can't jump | voltage | current |
| Energy | $\tfrac12 Cv^2$ | $\tfrac12 Li^2$ |
| Series | $\frac{1}{C_{eq}} = \sum\frac{1}{C_k}$ | $L_{eq} = \sum L_k$ |
| Parallel | $C_{eq} = \sum C_k$ | $\frac{1}{L_{eq}} = \sum\frac{1}{L_k}$ |

Notice capacitors combine like *conductances*, and inductors combine like *resistances*.

## Real components

- **Capacitors** have voltage ratings (exceed them and the dielectric breaks down), tolerance (often ±10–20%), and parasitic series resistance (ESR) and inductance (ESL). Ceramic capacitors lose much of their capacitance under DC bias; electrolytics are polarized and can fail violently if reversed.
- **Inductors** have winding resistance (DCR) and a saturation current, above which the core stops storing more energy and inductance collapses.

## Practice

```quiz
? A 2 µF capacitor and a 3 µF capacitor are connected in series. What is the equivalent capacitance, in µF?
= 1.2 | 0.01 | µF
> $\frac{1}{C} = \frac12 + \frac13 = \frac56$, so $C = 1.2\ \mu$F.
```

```quiz
? A 6 mH and a 3 mH inductor are connected in parallel. What is the equivalent inductance, in mH?
= 2 | 0.01 | mH
> Inductors in parallel combine like resistors in parallel: $\frac{6\cdot3}{6+3} = 2$ mH.
```

```quiz
? How much energy does a 50 mH inductor store when carrying 2 A, in J?
= 0.1 | 0.01 | J
> $\tfrac12 L i^2 = 0.5 \times 0.05 \times 4 = 0.1$ J.
```

```quiz
? A constant 3 mA flows into a 10 µF capacitor that starts at 0 V. What is its voltage after 5 ms?
= 1.5 | 0.01 | V
> $v = \frac{1}{C}\int i\,dt = \frac{3\times10^{-3}\times5\times10^{-3}}{10^{-5}} = 1.5$ V.
```

```quiz
? In a DC circuit that has reached steady state, how should you treat capacitors and inductors?
+ Capacitors as open circuits, inductors as short circuits
- Capacitors as short circuits, inductors as open circuits
- Both as open circuits
- Both as short circuits
> With nothing changing, $i_C = C\,dv/dt = 0$ and $v_L = L\,di/dt = 0$.
```

## Key takeaways

- $i = C\,dv/dt$ and $v = L\,di/dt$; capacitor voltage and inductor current are continuous.
- At DC: C is open, L is short.
- Energy: $\tfrac12Cv^2$ and $\tfrac12Li^2$.
- Capacitors combine like conductances; inductors like resistances.
