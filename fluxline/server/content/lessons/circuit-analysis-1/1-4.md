## Sources that depend on the circuit

The sources so far were **independent**: a battery's voltage doesn't care what else is in the circuit. A **dependent** (controlled) source has a value set by a voltage or current *elsewhere* in the circuit. They are drawn as diamonds, and they are how we model transistors, op-amps, and amplifiers in general.

| Type | Symbol output | Controlled by | Gain units |
|---|---|---|---|
| VCVS: voltage-controlled voltage source | $v = \mu\,v_x$ | a voltage | V/V (none) |
| CCVS: current-controlled voltage source | $v = r\,i_x$ | a current | V/A = Ω |
| VCCS: voltage-controlled current source | $i = g\,v_x$ | a voltage | A/V = S |
| CCCS: current-controlled current source | $i = \beta\,i_x$ | a current | A/A (none) |

A BJT's collector current is approximately $\beta$ times its base current: a CCCS. A MOSFET's small-signal drain current is $g_m v_{gs}$: a VCCS. An op-amp's output is $A(v_+ - v_-)$: a VCVS.

## Analyzing circuits with dependent sources

Two rules cover everything:

1. **Write KCL/KVL as usual**, treating the dependent source like an independent source of unknown value.
2. **Add one equation per dependent source** that expresses its controlling variable ($v_x$ or $i_x$) in terms of your unknowns.

> **Worked example (CCVS).** A 20 V source drives a single loop containing a 2 Ω resistor, a CCVS of value $5i$ (its $+$ terminal meets the loop current first), and a 3 Ω resistor. The control current $i$ is the loop current.
>
> ```circuit
> V 0,0 0,2 "20 V" side=-1
> R 0,0 2.4,0 "2 Ω" i=i
> VD 2.4,0 2.4,2 5i
> R 2.4,2 0,2 "3 Ω"
> ```
>
> KVL clockwise: $-20 + 2i + 5i + 3i = 0 \Rightarrow i = 2$ A.
>
> The dependent source absorbs $p = (5i)(i) = 20$ W. Check: the 20 V source delivers 40 W; the resistors absorb $8 + 12 = 20$ W; total absorbed $= 40$ W. ✓

Notice that this CCVS behaves exactly like a 5 Ω resistor, because its voltage is proportional to its own current. Dependent sources can act like resistors, negative resistors, or amplifiers depending on how they are connected.

> **Worked example (VCCS).** A 2 A source injects current into node 1. A 10 Ω resistor runs from node 1 to ground, and a VCCS of value $0.1\,v_1$ draws current from node 1 to ground.
>
> KCL at node 1: $\dfrac{v_1}{10} + 0.1\,v_1 = 2 \Rightarrow 0.2\,v_1 = 2 \Rightarrow v_1 = 10$ V.

**Warning:** when you find a Thévenin resistance, you may deactivate **independent** sources, but **never** dependent ones. Their value is tied to the circuit, so they stay in place. Use the test-source method from Lesson 3.1.

## Wye–delta transformations

Some resistor networks are neither series nor parallel. The classic example is the **bridge**:

```circuit
W 2,-0.7 2,0
T 2,-0.7 a
R 2,0 0.8,1.2 "10 Ω" side=-1
R 2,0 3.2,1.2 "20 Ω"
R 0.8,1.2 3.2,1.2 "30 Ω"
R 0.8,1.2 2,2.4 "15 Ω" side=-1
R 3.2,1.2 2,2.4 "10 Ω"
W 2,2.4 2,3.1
T 2,3.1 b
TEXT 0.55,1.2 c anchor=end color=muted
TEXT 3.45,1.2 d anchor=start color=muted
caption: A bridge network. No two resistors are in series or in parallel.
```

The fix is to convert three resistors connected in a **delta (Δ)**, a triangle between three nodes, into an equivalent **wye (Y)**, a star with a new center node, or the reverse. Name the Δ resistors by the nodes they connect ($R_{ab}, R_{bc}, R_{ca}$) and the Y resistors by the node they touch ($R_a, R_b, R_c$).

**Δ → Y:** each Y resistor is the product of the two Δ resistors touching its node, divided by the sum of all three:

$$
R_a = \frac{R_{ab}R_{ca}}{R_{ab} + R_{bc} + R_{ca}},\quad
R_b = \frac{R_{ab}R_{bc}}{\Sigma},\quad
R_c = \frac{R_{bc}R_{ca}}{\Sigma}
$$

**Y → Δ:** each Δ resistor is the sum of the pairwise products, divided by the Y resistor *opposite* it:

$$
R_{ab} = \frac{R_aR_b + R_bR_c + R_cR_a}{R_c},\quad
R_{bc} = \frac{\Pi}{R_a},\quad
R_{ca} = \frac{\Pi}{R_b}
$$

For a **balanced** network (all equal), $R_Y = R_\Delta / 3$.

> **Worked example.** Find $R_{ab}$ of the bridge above.
>
> Convert the top triangle $a$–$c$–$d$ ($R_{ac} = 10$, $R_{ad} = 20$, $R_{cd} = 30$, sum 60 Ω) into a Y:
>
> $R_a = \frac{10\cdot20}{60} = 3.33\ \Omega$, $\quad R_c = \frac{10\cdot30}{60} = 5\ \Omega$, $\quad R_d = \frac{20\cdot30}{60} = 10\ \Omega$
>
> Now the circuit is series/parallel: $R_a + \big[(R_c + 15)\parallel(R_d + 10)\big] = 3.33 + (20 \parallel 20) = 3.33 + 10 = 13.33\ \Omega$.

```quiz
? A CCCS has gain $\beta = 50$ and its control current is $i_b = 40$ µA. What current does it produce, in mA?
= 2 | 0.01 | mA
> $\beta i_b = 50 \times 40\ \mu\text{A} = 2$ mA. This is the core of the BJT model.
```

```quiz
? A balanced Δ network has 60 Ω in each branch. What is each resistor of the equivalent Y?
= 20 | 0.001 | Ω
> $R_Y = R_\Delta/3 = 20\ \Omega$.
```

```quiz
? A Y network has $R_a = 10\ \Omega$, $R_b = 20\ \Omega$, $R_c = 30\ \Omega$. What is the equivalent $R_{ab}$ of the Δ, in Ω?
= 36.67 | 0.01 | Ω
> $\Pi = 10\cdot20 + 20\cdot30 + 30\cdot10 = 1100$; $R_{ab} = \Pi / R_c = 1100/30 = 36.67\ \Omega$.
```

```quiz
? In the VCCS example, suppose the dependent source is reversed so that it *injects* $0.05\,v_1$ into node 1. The 2 A source and the 10 Ω resistor are unchanged. What is $v_1$?
= 40 | 0.001 | V
> KCL at node 1: $\dfrac{v_1}{10} - 0.05\,v_1 = 2 \Rightarrow 0.05\,v_1 = 2 \Rightarrow v_1 = 40$ V. A source that injects current in proportion to its own node voltage behaves like a **negative** resistance, which is why the voltage rose.
```

```quiz
? When finding the Thévenin resistance of a circuit containing a dependent source, you should:
- replace the dependent source with a short circuit
- replace the dependent source with an open circuit
+ leave the dependent source in place and use a test source (or $v_{oc}/i_{sc}$)
- remove the dependent source and its controlling element
> A dependent source's value depends on the circuit, so it is never "turned off."
```

## Key takeaways

- Four dependent sources: VCVS, CCVS, VCCS, CCCS. They model active devices.
- Analyze as usual, then add an equation for each controlling variable.
- Never deactivate dependent sources.
- Δ → Y: product of adjacent / sum. Y → Δ: sum of products / opposite. Balanced: $R_Y = R_\Delta/3$.
