## Linearity

A circuit made of resistors, linear dependent sources, and independent sources is **linear**: every voltage and current is a weighted sum of the independent source values. Two consequences make analysis faster: **superposition** and **source transformation**.

## Superposition

> In a linear circuit, the response to several independent sources equals the sum of the responses to each source acting alone.

To make a source "act alone," **deactivate** all the other independent sources:

- voltage source → **short circuit** (0 V),
- current source → **open circuit** (0 A).

Dependent sources are **never** deactivated.

> **Worked example.** Find the current $i$ in the 6 Ω resistor.
>
> ```circuit
> V 0,0 0,2 "12 V" side=-1
> R 0,0 2.4,0 "4 Ω"
> R 2.4,0 2.4,2 "6 Ω" i=i
> W 2.4,0 4.6,0
> I 4.6,2 4.6,0 "3 A" side=-1
> W 0,2 2.4,2
> W 2.4,2 4.6,2
> TEXT 2.4,-0.4 A
> ```
>
> **12 V alone** (current source opened): a divider, $v_A' = 12\cdot\frac{6}{4+6} = 7.2$ V, so $i' = 1.2$ A.
>
> **3 A alone** (voltage source shorted): the 4 Ω and 6 Ω are in parallel, and the 6 Ω gets $i'' = 3\cdot\frac{4}{4+6} = 1.2$ A.
>
> **Total:** $i = i' + i'' = 2.4$ A, so $v_A = 14.4$ V. (Check with one nodal equation: $\frac{v_A - 12}{4} + \frac{v_A}{6} = 3 \Rightarrow v_A = 14.4$ V. ✓)

### Power does not superpose

Power is proportional to $i^2$, which isn't linear. In the example the 6 Ω resistor absorbs $6(2.4)^2 = 34.56$ W, but adding the "individual" powers gives $6(1.2)^2 + 6(1.2)^2 = 17.28$ W. Always superpose **currents and voltages**, then compute power.

### When superposition shines

Superposition is rarely the fastest way to solve a DC circuit, but it is essential when sources have **different frequencies** (Circuit Analysis II), and it explains ideas like DC bias plus a small AC signal in amplifiers.

## Source transformation

A voltage source $v_s$ in series with $R$ and a current source $i_s = v_s/R$ in parallel with the same $R$ are indistinguishable from outside:

```circuit
V 0,0 0,2 v_s side=-1
R 0,0 2,0 R
T 2,0 ""
W 0,2 2,2
T 2,2 ""
TEXT 2.9,1 ⇔ size=24
W 4.2,0 6.4,0
W 4.2,2 6.4,2
I 4.2,2 4.2,0 "i_s = v_s/R" side=-1
R 6.4,0 6.4,2 R
W 6.4,0 7.2,0
T 7.2,0 ""
W 6.4,2 7.2,2
T 7.2,2 ""
caption: Thévenin form (left) and Norton form (right) of the same source.
```

This is just Thévenin ↔ Norton applied to a single branch. Repeated transformations collapse ladders without writing equations.

> **Same example by transformation.** The 12 V source with 4 Ω becomes 3 A in parallel with 4 Ω. Now two 3 A sources feed the same node, totaling 6 A, into $4 \parallel 6 = 2.4\ \Omega$. So $v_A = 6 \times 2.4 = 14.4$ V. ✓

## Key takeaways

- Linear circuits: total response = sum of responses to each independent source.
- Deactivate voltage sources as shorts, current sources as opens; keep dependent sources.
- Superpose voltages and currents, never power.
- $v_s$ in series with $R$ ⇔ $v_s/R$ in parallel with $R$.
