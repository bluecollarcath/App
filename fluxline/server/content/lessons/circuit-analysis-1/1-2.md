## Resistance and Ohm's law

A **resistor** is an element whose voltage is proportional to its current. Using the passive sign convention,

$$
v = iR \qquad \text{[}\Omega\text{]}
$$

The physical resistance of a uniform conductor of length $\ell$, cross-section $A$, and resistivity $\rho$ is

$$
R = \frac{\rho \ell}{A}
$$

Copper has $\rho \approx 1.72\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}$. The reciprocal of resistance is **conductance**, $G = 1/R$, measured in siemens (S), so $i = Gv$.

Because $v$ and $i$ always have the same sign for a positive resistor, a resistor **always absorbs** power:

$$
p = vi = i^2 R = \frac{v^2}{R} \ge 0
$$

Two limiting cases appear constantly:

- **Short circuit**: $R = 0$, so $v = 0$ for any current.
- **Open circuit**: $R \to \infty$, so $i = 0$ for any voltage.

## Circuit topology

Before applying Kirchhoff's laws you need vocabulary:

- **Node** — a point where two or more elements connect. All wire joined without elements between is *one* node.
- **Branch** — a single element (or source).
- **Loop** — any closed path that passes through each node at most once.
- **Mesh** — a loop that contains no other loop inside it (for planar circuits).

For a connected circuit with $b$ branches and $n$ nodes, there are $n-1$ independent KCL equations and $b - n + 1$ independent KVL equations (the number of meshes).

## Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL)

> The algebraic sum of currents entering any node (or any closed surface) is zero.

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{N} i_k = 0
$$

KCL is conservation of charge: a node cannot store charge, so whatever flows in must flow out. Pick a convention (e.g., entering $= +$) and stick to it at each node.

KCL also applies to a **closed surface** (a "supernode") enclosing part of a circuit. This is extremely useful — e.g., the current in the single wire connecting two subcircuits must be zero if there is no return path.

## Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL)

> The algebraic sum of voltages around any closed loop is zero.

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{M} v_k = 0
$$

KVL is conservation of energy: a charge carried around a closed path returns to the same potential. Walk around the loop in a chosen direction; add a voltage if you enter its $+$ terminal first, subtract it if you enter its $-$ terminal first (or use the reverse rule — just be consistent).

## Putting it together

```circuit
V 0,0 0,2 "30 V" side=-1
R 0,0 3.4,0 "2 Ω" i=i
R 3.4,0 3.4,2 "4 Ω" v=v_{4Ω}
R 3.4,2 0,2 "9 Ω"
LOOP 1.3,1 KVL
caption: A single loop: the same current $i$ flows through every element.
```

> **Worked example.** A 30 V source drives three series resistors: 2 Ω, 4 Ω, and 9 Ω. Find the current and the voltage across the 4 Ω resistor.
>
> Let $i$ flow clockwise. KVL around the loop: $-30 + 2i + 4i + 9i = 0 \Rightarrow i = 2$ A.
> Then $v_{4\Omega} = 4 \times 2 = 8$ V.
> Power check: source delivers $30 \times 2 = 60$ W; resistors absorb $2^2(2+4+9) = 60$ W. ✓

```circuit
W 0,1.2 1.4,1.2 i="3 A" side=-1
W 1.4,0 1.4,1.2 i="5 A" side=-1
W 1.4,1.2 2.8,1.2 i="1 A"
W 1.4,1.2 1.4,2.4 i=i_x
caption: Currents at a node: 3 A and 5 A enter; 1 A and $i_x$ leave.
```

> **Worked example (KCL).** At a node, currents of 3 A and 5 A enter and currents of 1 A and $i_x$ leave. Then $3 + 5 - 1 - i_x = 0$, so $i_x = 7$ A.

## Common mistakes

1. Treating two dots connected by a plain wire as separate nodes.
2. Mixing sign conventions halfway around a loop.
3. Assuming the current through a voltage source is known (it is not — the *rest of the circuit* determines it). Likewise, the voltage across a current source is unknown.

## Practice

```quiz
? A 2.2 kΩ resistor has 11 V across it. What current flows through it, in mA?
= 5 | 0.01 | mA
> $i = v/R = 11/2200 = 5$ mA.
```

```quiz
? What power (in W) does a 47 Ω resistor dissipate when carrying 0.2 A?
= 1.88 | 0.01 | W
> $p = i^2R = 0.04 \times 47 = 1.88$ W. A standard ¼-W resistor would burn up.
```

```quiz
? A circuit has 7 branches and 4 nodes. How many independent KVL (mesh) equations are there?
= 4 | 0 |
> $b - n + 1 = 7 - 4 + 1 = 4$.
```

```quiz
? A 24 V source is in series with a 3 Ω and a 5 Ω resistor. What is the voltage across the 5 Ω resistor?
= 15 | 0.01 | V
> $i = 24/8 = 3$ A, so $v = 5 \times 3 = 15$ V.
```

```quiz
? Which conservation law underlies KCL?
+ Conservation of charge
- Conservation of energy
- Conservation of momentum
- Conservation of power
> KCL says charge cannot accumulate at a node — conservation of charge. KVL comes from conservation of energy.
```

## Key takeaways

- Ohm's law: $v = iR$ (with passive sign convention). Resistors only absorb power.
- KCL: currents into a node sum to zero. KVL: voltages around a loop sum to zero.
- $n-1$ KCL equations and $b-n+1$ KVL equations are independent.
