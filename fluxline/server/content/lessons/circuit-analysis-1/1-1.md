## Why start here

Every circuit you will ever analyze — a smartphone power stage, a radio receiver, a 500 kV transmission line — is described by just four quantities: **charge**, **current**, **voltage**, and **power**. Getting the definitions *and especially the sign conventions* exactly right now will save you from the most common errors students make for the rest of the degree.

## Charge

Electric charge $q$ is measured in **coulombs (C)**. The charge of one electron is

$$
e = -1.602 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{C}
$$

so one coulomb corresponds to about $6.24 \times 10^{18}$ electrons. Charge is *conserved*: it cannot be created or destroyed, only moved. That single fact is the physical basis of Kirchhoff's Current Law in the next lesson.

## Current

**Current** is the rate at which charge flows past a point:

$$
i(t) = \frac{dq}{dt} \qquad \text{[A = C/s]}
$$

Integrating gives the charge transferred between $t_0$ and $t$:

$$
q(t) = \int_{t_0}^{t} i(\tau)\, d\tau + q(t_0)
$$

By convention, current direction is the direction **positive** charge would move. In a metal wire the carriers are electrons moving the *opposite* way — but you never need to think about that in circuit analysis. An arrow labeled $i$ on a schematic is a *reference direction*; if the computed value is negative, the actual flow is opposite the arrow. That is not an error.

> **Worked example.** The charge entering a terminal is $q(t) = 5t\sin(4\pi t)$ mC. Find the current at $t = 0.5$ s.
>
> $i = \dfrac{dq}{dt} = 5\sin(4\pi t) + 20\pi t\cos(4\pi t)$ mA. At $t = 0.5$: $\sin(2\pi)=0$, $\cos(2\pi)=1$, so $i = 10\pi \approx 31.4$ mA.

## Voltage

**Voltage** (potential difference) is the energy required to move a unit charge between two points:

$$
v = \frac{dw}{dq} \qquad \text{[V = J/C]}
$$

Voltage is always *between two points*. The symbol $v_{ab}$ means the potential of $a$ relative to $b$, and $v_{ab} = -v_{ba}$. On a schematic, the $+$ and $-$ marks are reference polarities, exactly like current arrows.

## Power and the passive sign convention

Power is the rate of energy transfer:

$$
p = \frac{dw}{dt} = \frac{dw}{dq}\cdot\frac{dq}{dt} = v\,i \qquad \text{[W]}
$$

Whether that power is *absorbed* or *delivered* depends on how you drew the reference directions. Engineers use the **passive sign convention (PSC)**:

> If the reference current **enters the $+$ terminal** of an element, then $p = vi$ is the power **absorbed** by that element.

If the current arrow enters the $-$ terminal instead, use $p = -vi$. With the PSC:

| Sign of $p$ | Meaning | Typical elements |
|---|---|---|
| $p > 0$ | element absorbs power | resistors, charging batteries |
| $p < 0$ | element delivers power | sources, discharging capacitors |

### Conservation of power (Tellegen's theorem)

In any circuit, the algebraic sum of power over all elements is zero at every instant:

$$
\sum_{k} p_k = 0
$$

This is the single best sanity check you have. After every analysis, add up the powers; if they do not sum to zero, there is a sign error somewhere.

## Energy

Energy over an interval is the integral of power:

$$
w = \int_{t_0}^{t} p(\tau)\, d\tau
$$

Utilities bill in kilowatt-hours: $1\ \text{kWh} = 3.6\ \text{MJ}$.

> **Worked example.** A 1.5 kW space heater runs 6 hours a day for 30 days at \$0.16/kWh. Energy: $1.5 \times 6 \times 30 = 270$ kWh. Cost: $270 \times 0.16 = 43.20$ dollars.

## Practice

```quiz
? A current of $i(t) = 4e^{-2t}$ A flows into a terminal for $t \ge 0$. How much total charge (in coulombs) enters the terminal from $t = 0$ to $t \to \infty$?
= 2 | 0.01 | C
> $q = \int_0^\infty 4e^{-2t}\,dt = 4 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} = 2$ C.
```

```quiz
? An element has $v = 12$ V across it with $+$ at the top, and the reference current $i = -3$ A is drawn entering the top terminal. Under the passive sign convention, what power does the element absorb?
= -36 | 0.01 | W
> Current enters the $+$ terminal, so $p = vi = 12(-3) = -36$ W. The element **delivers** 36 W — it is acting as a source.
```

```quiz
? A circuit has three elements. Element A absorbs 40 W, element B absorbs −15 W. What power does element C absorb?
- 55 W
- 25 W
+ −25 W
- −55 W
> Power must sum to zero: $40 + (-15) + p_C = 0 \Rightarrow p_C = -25$ W, so C delivers 25 W.
```

```quiz
? A phone battery stores 15 Wh. How many joules is that?
= 54000 | 0.01 | J
> $15\ \text{Wh} \times 3600\ \text{s/h} = 54{,}000$ J.
```

## Key takeaways

- $i = dq/dt$, $v = dw/dq$, $p = vi$.
- Reference directions are arbitrary; a negative answer just means the true direction is opposite.
- Passive sign convention: current into $+$ → $p = vi$ is absorbed.
- Total power in a circuit always sums to zero — use it to check your work.
