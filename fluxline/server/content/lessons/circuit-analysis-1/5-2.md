## Why first-order circuits matter

A circuit with **one** energy-storage element (one capacitor or one inductor, after combining) is described by a first-order differential equation. These circuits model timing, debouncing, filtering, sample-and-hold, relay coils, and gate charging in every power converter.

## The source-free RC circuit

A capacitor charged to $V_0$ discharges through $R$ for $t \ge 0$. KCL at the top node:

$$
C\frac{dv}{dt} + \frac{v}{R} = 0
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
v(t) = V_0\,e^{-t/\tau}, \qquad \tau = RC
$$

The **time constant** $\tau$ is the time for the response to fall to $e^{-1} \approx 36.8\%$ of its initial value.

| $t$ | $\tau$ | $2\tau$ | $3\tau$ | $4\tau$ | $5\tau$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $v/V_0$ | 0.368 | 0.135 | 0.050 | 0.018 | 0.0067 |

After $5\tau$ the transient is effectively over (< 1%).

## The source-free RL circuit

An inductor carrying $I_0$ discharges through $R$. KVL:

$$
L\frac{di}{dt} + Ri = 0
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
i(t) = I_0\,e^{-t/\tau}, \qquad \tau = \frac{L}{R}
$$

Note the inversion: a *larger* $R$ makes an RC circuit *slower* but an RL circuit *faster*.

## The general first-order solution

For **any** first-order circuit driven by DC sources, every voltage and current has the form

$$
\boxed{x(t) = x(\infty) + \big[x(0^+) - x(\infty)\big]\,e^{-(t-t_0)/\tau}, \qquad t \ge t_0}
$$

So you need only three numbers:

1. **Initial value $x(0^+)$.** Use continuity: a capacitor's *voltage* and an inductor's *current* cannot change instantaneously. Find $v_C(0^-)$ or $i_L(0^-)$ from the circuit before switching (in DC steady state, C is an open circuit and L is a short circuit), then $v_C(0^+) = v_C(0^-)$, $i_L(0^+) = i_L(0^-)$.
2. **Final value $x(\infty)$.** Solve the circuit after switching with C open / L short.
3. **Time constant.** $\tau = R_{th}C$ or $\tau = L/R_{th}$, where $R_{th}$ is the Thévenin resistance seen by the storage element *after* switching.

> **Worked example.** A 10 V step is applied at $t=0$ through $R = 10$ kΩ to an uncharged $C = 100\ \mu\text{F}$.
>
> $v_C(0^+) = 0$, $v_C(\infty) = 10$ V, $\tau = 10^4 \times 10^{-4} = 1$ s.
>
> $v_C(t) = 10\left(1 - e^{-t}\right)$ V. At $t = 2$ s, $v_C = 10(1 - 0.1353) = 8.65$ V.
>
> The current is $i(t) = C\,dv/dt = 1\,e^{-t}$ mA — it jumps to 1 mA at $t = 0^+$ (the capacitor initially acts as a short).

## Inductive kick

Suddenly opening a switch in series with an inductor forces $di/dt \to -\infty$, so $v = L\,di/dt$ becomes enormous. This is how ignition coils create sparks — and why every relay coil driven by a transistor needs a **flyback diode** to give the current a path.

## Practice

```quiz
? An RC circuit has $R = 4.7$ kΩ and $C = 2.2\ \mu$F. What is the time constant, in ms?
= 10.34 | 0.01 | ms
> $\tau = 4700 \times 2.2\times10^{-6} = 10.34$ ms.
```

```quiz
? A capacitor charged to 20 V discharges with $\tau = 5$ ms. What is its voltage at $t = 10$ ms?
= 2.707 | 0.02 | V
> $20 e^{-2} = 2.71$ V.
```

```quiz
? An inductor of 50 mH carries 2 A when it is switched across a 25 Ω resistor. What is the time constant, in ms?
= 2 | 0.01 | ms
> $\tau = L/R = 0.05/25 = 2$ ms.
```

```quiz
? Which quantity is continuous (cannot jump) at a switching instant?
+ Capacitor voltage and inductor current
- Capacitor current and inductor voltage
- Capacitor voltage and inductor voltage
- All voltages and currents in the circuit
> Energy $\tfrac12Cv^2$ and $\tfrac12Li^2$ cannot change instantly, so $v_C$ and $i_L$ are continuous.
```

```quiz
? A capacitor starts at 2 V and charges toward 12 V with $\tau = 1$ ms. What is its voltage at $t = 1$ ms?
= 8.32 | 0.01 | V
> $12 + (2 - 12)e^{-1} = 12 - 3.679 = 8.32$ V.
```

## Key takeaways

- $\tau = RC$ or $L/R_{th}$; after $5\tau$ the transient is over.
- $x(t) = x(\infty) + [x(0^+) - x(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau}$ — find three numbers and you are done.
- $v_C$ and $i_L$ are continuous; everything else may jump.
