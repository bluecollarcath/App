## From atoms to bands

An isolated silicon atom has discrete electron energy levels. Bring $\sim 5\times10^{22}$ atoms/cm³ together in a crystal, and those levels split and broaden into nearly continuous **energy bands**. Two bands matter:

- the **valence band** — filled with bonding electrons at 0 K, top edge $E_v$;
- the **conduction band** — empty at 0 K, bottom edge $E_c$.

They are separated by the **bandgap** $E_g = E_c - E_v$, a range of forbidden energies.

## Metals, insulators, semiconductors

| Material | Bandgap | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Metal | bands overlap | always conducts |
| Insulator (SiO₂) | ~9 eV | essentially never conducts |
| Semiconductor | ~0.5–3.5 eV | conductivity controllable by doping, light, temperature, and fields |

Common semiconductors at 300 K:

| | Si | Ge | GaAs | SiC (4H) | GaN |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $E_g$ (eV) | 1.12 | 0.66 | 1.42 | 3.26 | 3.4 |

Wide-bandgap SiC and GaN tolerate higher fields and temperatures — that's why they are taking over power electronics.

## Electrons and holes

At room temperature, thermal energy breaks some bonds, lifting electrons into the conduction band. Each leaves behind a **hole** in the valence band, which behaves as a mobile positive charge. Both contribute to current. In pure (intrinsic) silicon at 300 K, the carrier concentration is only

$$
n_i \approx 1\times10^{10}\ \text{cm}^{-3}
$$

— about one free carrier per $5\times10^{12}$ atoms. **Doping** with ~$10^{15}$–$10^{20}$ cm⁻³ impurity atoms changes this by many orders of magnitude (next lesson).

## Bandgap and light

A photon can create an electron–hole pair only if its energy exceeds $E_g$, and recombination across the gap emits a photon of energy $\approx E_g$:

$$
\lambda\,[\mu\text{m}] = \frac{1.24}{E_g\,[\text{eV}]}
$$

Silicon absorbs visible light (good for solar cells and camera sensors) but, being an **indirect** gap material, emits light very inefficiently. LEDs and lasers use **direct**-gap materials like GaAs and GaN.

## Key takeaways

- Bands form from overlapping atomic levels; $E_g$ separates valence and conduction bands.
- Electrons and holes both carry current.
- $\lambda = 1.24/E_g$ links bandgap to light.
