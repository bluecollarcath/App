## From charge to current

With the MOS capacitor inverted (Lesson 3.2), the channel holds a sheet of electrons whose charge per unit area is set by how far the gate is above threshold:

$$
Q_{inv} = -C_{ox}\,(V_{GS} - V_T)
$$

Add a source and drain, and apply $V_{DS}$. The channel charge now varies along the channel (less gate–channel voltage near the drain), and the current is the charge times its drift velocity $\mu_nE$. Integrating along the channel length $L$ gives the long-channel **square law** used throughout Microelectronics:

$$
I_D = \mu_nC_{ox}\frac{W}{L}\left[(V_{GS} - V_T)V_{DS} - \frac{V_{DS}^2}{2}\right] \quad\text{(triode)}
$$

$$
I_D = \frac{1}{2}\mu_nC_{ox}\frac{W}{L}(V_{GS} - V_T)^2 \quad\text{(saturation, } V_{DS} \ge V_{GS} - V_T)
$$

Saturation begins when the channel **pinches off** at the drain end; beyond that, extra drain voltage drops across a short depleted region near the drain, and the current barely changes (apart from channel-length modulation).

> **Example.** $t_{ox} = 5$ nm ($C_{ox} = 6.9\times10^{-7}$ F/cm²), $\mu_n = 400$ cm²/V·s in the channel (surface scattering cuts it well below bulk): $\mu_nC_{ox} = 276\ \mu$A/V². With $W/L = 10$ and $V_{GS} - V_T = 0.5$ V, $I_D = \tfrac12(276\ \mu)(10)(0.25) = 0.35$ mA.

## Subthreshold conduction

Below $V_T$ the current doesn't stop. It falls **exponentially**, carried by diffusion over a barrier like a diode:

$$
I_D \propto e^{(V_{GS} - V_T)/nV_T}, \qquad S = 2.3\,n\,\frac{kT}{q} = 2.3\frac{kT}{q}\left(1 + \frac{C_d}{C_{ox}}\right)
$$

The **subthreshold swing** $S$ is the gate voltage needed to change the current tenfold. It can never be below $2.3kT/q = 60$ mV/decade at room temperature, a fundamental limit. With $C_d/C_{ox} = 0.3$, $S = 77$ mV/dec, so a transistor with $V_T = 0.3$ V leaks only $10^{-0.3/0.077} \approx 10^{-3.9}$ of its threshold current when off. That leakage, multiplied by billions of transistors, is a major part of a modern chip's power, and the 60 mV/dec limit is why supply voltages stopped falling near 0.7–1 V.

## Scaling

For decades, **Dennard scaling** shrank every dimension and voltage by a factor $\kappa$ (about 1.4 per generation): transistors became $\kappa^2$ denser, $\kappa$ times faster, and used $\kappa^2$ less power each, so power density stayed constant. Around 2005 it broke down: $V_T$ couldn't keep falling (because of subthreshold leakage), so supply voltages stalled, and power density began to rise. Clock frequencies have been flat near 3–5 GHz since.

## Short-channel effects

As $L$ shrinks toward the depletion widths of the source and drain junctions, the gate loses control of the channel:

- **Velocity saturation:** with fields above $10^4$ V/cm, carriers travel at about $v_{sat} = 10^7$ cm/s. The current then grows only **linearly** with overdrive: $I_D \approx WC_{ox}(V_{GS} - V_T)v_{sat}$. For $W = 1\ \mu$m, $L = 50$ nm and 0.5 V overdrive, the square law predicts 0.69 mA, but the velocity-saturated limit is 0.35 mA.
- **$V_T$ roll-off:** the source and drain depletion regions take over part of the channel's charge, lowering $V_T$ in short devices.
- **Drain-induced barrier lowering (DIBL):** the drain voltage reaches through and lowers the source barrier, raising off-current with $V_{DS}$.
- **Gate leakage:** oxides below about 2 nm leak by tunneling, so SiO₂ was replaced by **high-k** dielectrics such as HfO₂ (thicker, for the same $C_{ox}$), with metal gates.

## FinFETs and gate-all-around

The fix for poor gate control is to wrap the gate around the channel. In a **FinFET** (Intel's 22 nm node, 2011) the channel is a thin vertical fin with the gate on three sides; the fin is so thin that the gate controls all of it, restoring a steep subthreshold swing. **Gate-all-around** (nanosheet) transistors, in production at about the 3 nm generation, surround stacked horizontal sheets on all four sides. "Node names" no longer correspond to any physical length; they're marketing labels for density.

## Key takeaways

- $Q_{inv} = C_{ox}(V_{GS} - V_T)$ leads to the square law; saturation starts at pinch-off.
- Subthreshold current falls exponentially with swing $S \ge 60$ mV/dec at room temperature.
- Dennard scaling ended when $V_T$ (and so $V_{DD}$) stopped scaling; leakage and power density now dominate.
- Short channels bring velocity saturation, $V_T$ roll-off, DIBL and gate leakage; high-k/metal gates, FinFETs and GAA restore control.
