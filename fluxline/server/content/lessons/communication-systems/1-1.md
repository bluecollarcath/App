## Why modulate?

Audio (20 Hz–20 kHz) can't be radiated efficiently — antennas must be a sizeable fraction of a wavelength, and a 10 kHz wavelength is 30 km. And if every station transmitted at baseband, they'd all overlap. **Modulation** shifts a message $m(t)$ up to a carrier frequency $f_c$, solving both problems and enabling frequency-division multiplexing.

## Double-sideband suppressed carrier (DSB-SC)

Multiply the message by a carrier:

$$
s(t) = A_c\,m(t)\cos(2\pi f_c t)
$$

By the modulation property of the Fourier transform,

$$
S(f) = \frac{A_c}{2}\big[M(f - f_c) + M(f + f_c)\big]
$$

The spectrum is copied to $\pm f_c$. If the message has bandwidth $W$, the transmitted bandwidth is $2W$ (upper and lower sidebands). Demodulation requires a **coherent** receiver — a local oscillator synchronized in frequency and phase.

## Conventional AM

Add a carrier so a cheap receiver can demodulate:

$$
s(t) = A_c\big[1 + \mu\,m_n(t)\big]\cos(2\pi f_c t)
$$

where $m_n(t)$ is the message normalized to $|m_n| \le 1$, and $\mu$ is the **modulation index**. If $\mu \le 1$, the envelope $A_c[1 + \mu m_n(t)]$ never goes negative, and a simple **envelope detector** — a diode, a resistor, and a capacitor — recovers the message. If $\mu > 1$, the envelope is distorted (overmodulation).

## The price: power efficiency

The carrier carries no information but consumes most of the power. For single-tone modulation:

$$
\eta = \frac{P_{sidebands}}{P_{total}} = \frac{\mu^2}{2 + \mu^2}
$$

Even at $\mu = 1$, only one third of the power carries the message. AM broadcasting accepted that inefficiency in exchange for receivers costing a few dollars.

## Practice

```quiz
? An AM station transmits audio limited to 5 kHz. What bandwidth does its signal occupy, in kHz?
= 10 | 0.001 | kHz
> AM bandwidth is $2W = 10$ kHz.
```

```quiz
? What is the power efficiency of single-tone AM with $\mu = 0.5$? (as a percentage)
= 11.11 | 0.01 | %
> $0.25/2.25 = 11.1\%$.
```

```quiz
? An AM transmitter with $\mu = 1$ (single tone) radiates 1.5 kW total. How much power is in the carrier, in kW?
= 1 | 0.01 | kW
> Carrier fraction is $2/(2 + \mu^2) = 2/3$, so 1 kW; the sidebands carry 0.5 kW.
```

```quiz
? What happens when an AM signal is overmodulated ($\mu > 1$)?
- The bandwidth is halved
+ An envelope detector produces a distorted output
- The carrier disappears
- Nothing; it improves efficiency
> The envelope goes through zero and "folds", so it no longer matches the message.
```

## Key takeaways

- Modulation shifts spectra to $\pm f_c$; DSB occupies $2W$.
- AM adds a carrier so an envelope detector works when $\mu \le 1$.
- AM efficiency is at most 33% for a tone.
