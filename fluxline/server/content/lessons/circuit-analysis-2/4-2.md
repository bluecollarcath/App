## The transfer function

For a circuit with zero initial conditions, the ratio of the output transform to the input transform is the **transfer function**:

$$
H(s) = \frac{Y(s)}{X(s)}
$$

It depends only on the circuit, not on the input. Once you know $H(s)$:

- the **impulse response** is $h(t) = \mathcal L^{-1}\{H(s)\}$,
- the response to any input is $Y(s) = H(s)X(s)$,
- the **frequency response** is $H(j\omega)$, which is the subject of the next unit.

> **RC low-pass.** Input across series R and C; output across C. A voltage divider of impedances:
> $$H(s) = \frac{1/(sC)}{R + 1/(sC)} = \frac{1}{1 + sRC}$$

> **Series RLC band-pass.** Output across R:
> $$H(s) = \frac{R}{R + sL + 1/(sC)} = \frac{(R/L)\,s}{s^2 + (R/L)\,s + 1/(LC)}$$

## Poles and zeros

Factor the numerator and denominator:

$$
H(s) = K\frac{(s - z_1)(s - z_2)\cdots}{(s - p_1)(s - p_2)\cdots}
$$

The **zeros** $z_k$ are frequencies the circuit blocks. The **poles** $p_k$ are the circuit's natural frequencies: each pole contributes a term $e^{p_kt}$ to the natural response. Their locations in the complex s-plane tell you almost everything:

| Pole location | Natural response term |
|---|---|
| negative real axis, $s = -a$ | decaying exponential $e^{-at}$ |
| complex pair in left half-plane, $-\alpha \pm j\omega_d$ | decaying oscillation $e^{-\alpha t}\cos(\omega_d t + \phi)$ |
| on the $j\omega$ axis | sustained oscillation (marginally stable) |
| right half-plane | growing response (**unstable**) |

> A circuit is **stable** if and only if every pole has a negative real part.

## Worked example

> $$H(s) = \frac{10(s + 2)}{(s + 1)(s + 5)}$$
>
> ```circuit

> ARROW 0,1.5 0,-1.6
> TEXT 1.55,0 σ anchor=start
> TEXT 0.2,-1.55 jω anchor=start
> TEXT -1,0 × size=26 color=anno
> TEXT -5,0 × size=26 color=anno
> TEXT -2,0 ○ size=24 color=anno
> TEXT -1,0.45 "−1" color=muted size=13
> TEXT -2,0.55 "−2" color=muted size=13
> TEXT -5,0.45 "−5" color=muted size=13
> caption: Pole-zero plot: poles (×) at −1 and −5, a zero at −2. Both poles are in the left half-plane, so the circuit is stable.
> ```
>
> **DC gain** ($s = 0$): $H(0) = \frac{10\cdot2}{1\cdot5} = 4$. A 1 V step therefore settles at 4 V.
>
> **Impulse response** by partial fractions:
> $$\frac{10(s+2)}{(s+1)(s+5)} = \frac{A}{s+1} + \frac{B}{s+5},\quad A = \frac{10(1)}{4} = 2.5,\quad B = \frac{10(-3)}{-4} = 7.5$$
> $$h(t) = 2.5e^{-t} + 7.5e^{-5t}$$
>
> The pole at $-1$ is the **dominant** pole: its term decays slowest and sets how long the response takes to settle (about $4/1 = 4$ s).

## Key takeaways

- $H(s) = Y/X$ with zero initial conditions; $h(t) = \mathcal L^{-1}\{H\}$.
- Poles are natural frequencies; their real parts set stability and decay rate.
- DC gain is $H(0)$; the dominant (slowest) pole sets settling time.
