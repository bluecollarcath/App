## Two storage elements, one equation

With one capacitor *and* one inductor, a circuit is described by a **second-order** differential equation. These circuits can **oscillate** — the foundation of resonant filters, tuned radios, wireless power transfer, and the ringing you will see on every oscilloscope.

## The series RLC

With no source, KVL around $R$, $L$, $C$ in series (differentiate once to eliminate the integral):

$$
\frac{d^2 i}{dt^2} + \frac{R}{L}\frac{di}{dt} + \frac{1}{LC}\,i = 0
$$

Write it in standard form:

$$
\frac{d^2 i}{dt^2} + 2\alpha\frac{di}{dt} + \omega_0^2\,i = 0,
\qquad
\alpha = \frac{R}{2L},\quad \omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}
$$

- $\alpha$ is the **neper frequency** (damping coefficient), in Np/s.
- $\omega_0$ is the **resonant (undamped natural) frequency**, in rad/s.

For a **parallel** RLC, $\omega_0$ is the same but $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2RC}$.

```circuit

R 0,0 2.4,0 R i=i
L 2.4,0 2.4,2 L
W 2.4,2 0,2
caption: A source-free series RLC circuit. Energy stored in $L$ and $C$ sloshes back and forth while $R$ dissipates it.
```

## Characteristic roots

Try $i = Ae^{st}$: $s^2 + 2\alpha s + \omega_0^2 = 0$, so

$$
s_{1,2} = -\alpha \pm \sqrt{\alpha^2 - \omega_0^2}
$$

| Case | Condition | Roots | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overdamped | $\alpha > \omega_0$ | real, distinct | $A_1e^{s_1t} + A_2e^{s_2t}$ |
| Critically damped | $\alpha = \omega_0$ | real, repeated | $(A_1 + A_2t)e^{-\alpha t}$ |
| Underdamped | $\alpha < \omega_0$ | complex | $e^{-\alpha t}(B_1\cos\omega_d t + B_2\sin\omega_d t)$ |

where the **damped frequency** is $\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \alpha^2}$.

```plot
x: 0, 16, ω_0 t
y: -0.8, 1.1, v_C / V_0
f: exp(-0.2*x)*(cos(0.9798*x) + 0.2041*sin(0.9798*x)) | underdamped (ζ = 0.2)
f: (1 + x)*exp(-x) | critically damped (ζ = 1)
f: (3.732*exp(-0.2679*x) - 0.2679*exp(-3.732*x))/3.4641 | overdamped (ζ = 2) | dash
caption: Natural response of the capacitor voltage, starting from $V_0$ with zero current, for three amounts of damping.
```

Critical damping gives the fastest return to zero without overshoot. The **damping ratio** $\zeta = \alpha/\omega_0$ and **quality factor** $Q = 1/(2\zeta)$ summarize the behavior; for a series RLC, $Q = \omega_0 L / R$.

## Worked example

> Series RLC: $R = 40\ \Omega$, $L = 0.1$ H, $C = 10\ \mu$F.
>
> $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{(0.1)(10^{-5})} = 1000$ rad/s. $\alpha = 40/(2\cdot0.1) = 200$ Np/s.
>
> $\alpha < \omega_0$ → **underdamped**. $\omega_d = \sqrt{10^6 - 4\times10^4} \approx 979.8$ rad/s.
>
> The current rings at about 156 Hz with an envelope $e^{-200t}$ that decays to ~1% in $5/200 = 25$ ms.
>
> For critical damping we would need $R = 2L\omega_0 = 200\ \Omega$.

## Finding the constants

You need two initial conditions: $i(0^+)$ (inductor current, continuous) and $di/dt(0^+)$, which comes from KVL at $t = 0^+$ using $v_L(0^+) = L\,di/dt(0^+)$ and the continuous capacitor voltage.

## Key takeaways

- $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC}$ for both topologies; $\alpha = R/2L$ (series) or $1/2RC$ (parallel).
- Compare $\alpha$ to $\omega_0$ to determine the damping case.
- Underdamped circuits ring at $\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \alpha^2}$.
