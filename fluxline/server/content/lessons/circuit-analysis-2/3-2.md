## Coupling through a shared magnetic field

When the magnetic flux of one coil passes through another, a changing current in the first induces a voltage in the second. This **mutual inductance** $M$ is how transformers, wireless chargers, current sensors, and isolated power supplies work.

For two coupled coils:

$$
v_1 = L_1\frac{di_1}{dt} \pm M\frac{di_2}{dt}, \qquad
v_2 = L_2\frac{di_2}{dt} \pm M\frac{di_1}{dt}
$$

The coupling is measured by the **coefficient of coupling** $k$:

$$
M = k\sqrt{L_1L_2}, \qquad 0 \le k \le 1
$$

Loosely coupled coils (wireless charging) may have $k \approx 0.2$–$0.7$; a transformer on an iron core has $k > 0.99$.

## The dot convention

The sign of the mutual term depends on how the coils are wound, which a schematic can't show. Instead, a **dot** marks one end of each coil:

> If a current **enters** the dotted terminal of one coil, the voltage it induces in the other coil is **positive at that coil's dotted terminal**.

In practice: if both currents enter (or both leave) their dotted terminals, the mutual term has the **same sign** as the self term ($+M$). Otherwise it's $-M$.

In phasor form: $\mathbf V_1 = j\omega L_1\mathbf I_1 \pm j\omega M\mathbf I_2$ and $\mathbf V_2 = j\omega L_2\mathbf I_2 \pm j\omega M\mathbf I_1$.

### Series connection

Two coupled coils in series act like one inductor:

$$
L_{\text{aiding}} = L_1 + L_2 + 2M, \qquad L_{\text{opposing}} = L_1 + L_2 - 2M
$$

Measuring both values is a lab method for finding $M$: $M = (L_{\text{aiding}} - L_{\text{opposing}})/4$.

## The ideal transformer

Make the coupling perfect ($k = 1$), the inductances huge, and the windings lossless. With $N_1$ primary turns and $N_2$ secondary turns, and turns ratio $n = N_2/N_1$:

$$
\boxed{\frac{\mathbf V_2}{\mathbf V_1} = \frac{N_2}{N_1} = n, \qquad \frac{\mathbf I_2}{\mathbf I_1} = \frac{N_1}{N_2} = \frac1n}
$$

(with $\mathbf I_2$ leaving the dotted secondary terminal). Power in equals power out: $\mathbf V_1\mathbf I_1^* = \mathbf V_2\mathbf I_2^*$.

### Reflected impedance

A load $\mathbf Z_L$ on the secondary looks, from the primary, like

$$
\boxed{\mathbf Z_{in} = \frac{\mathbf Z_L}{n^2}}
$$

Transformers therefore **match impedances**: they let a source see whatever load resistance gives maximum power transfer.

> **Worked example.** An audio amplifier with 800 Ω output resistance drives an 8 Ω speaker. What turns ratio gives maximum power transfer?
>
> We need $8/n^2 = 800$, so $n = \sqrt{8/800} = 0.1$. A **10:1** step-down transformer ($N_1 = 10N_2$).

## Practice

```quiz
? Two coils have $L_1 = 4$ mH, $L_2 = 9$ mH, and $k = 0.5$. What is $M$, in mH?
= 3 | 0.001 | mH
> $M = k\sqrt{L_1L_2} = 0.5\sqrt{36} = 3$ mH.
```

```quiz
? Those coils are connected in series aiding. What is the total inductance, in mH?
= 19 | 0.001 | mH
> $L_1 + L_2 + 2M = 4 + 9 + 6 = 19$ mH. (Opposing would give 7 mH.)
```

```quiz
? An ideal transformer steps 120 V down to 12 V. A 3 Ω load is connected to the secondary. What resistance does the source see, in Ω?
= 300 | 0.001 | Ω
> $n = 12/120 = 0.1$, so $Z_{in} = 3/0.01 = 300\ \Omega$.
```

```quiz
? In that transformer, the secondary current is 4 A. What is the primary current, in A?
= 0.4 | 0.001 | A
> Current scales by the inverse ratio: $I_1 = nI_2 = 0.1 \times 4 = 0.4$ A. Check power: $120 \times 0.4 = 12 \times 4 = 48$ W.
```

```quiz
? Current $i_1$ enters the dotted end of coil 1 and $i_2$ leaves the dotted end of coil 2. The mutual-inductance term in $v_1$ (with $v_1$ positive at the dot) is:
- $+M\,di_2/dt$
+ $-M\,di_2/dt$
- $+M\,di_1/dt$
- zero
> One current enters a dot and the other leaves one, so the mutual term has the opposite sign to the self term.
```

## Key takeaways

- $M = k\sqrt{L_1L_2}$; dots set the sign of the mutual voltage.
- Series coupled coils: $L_1 + L_2 \pm 2M$.
- Ideal transformer: $V_2/V_1 = n$, $I_2/I_1 = 1/n$, $\mathbf Z_{in} = \mathbf Z_L/n^2$.
