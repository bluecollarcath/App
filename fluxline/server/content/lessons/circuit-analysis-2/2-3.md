## Instantaneous power

For $v = V_m\cos(\omega t + \theta_v)$ and $i = I_m\cos(\omega t + \theta_i)$:

$$
p(t) = \tfrac12 V_m I_m\cos(\theta_v - \theta_i) + \tfrac12 V_m I_m\cos(2\omega t + \theta_v + \theta_i)
$$

A constant term plus a term oscillating at **twice** the source frequency. The oscillating term averages to zero.

## RMS values

The **effective** or **RMS** value of a periodic signal is the DC value that delivers the same average power to a resistor:

$$
X_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T x^2(t)\,dt}
\qquad\text{for a sinusoid:}\quad X_{rms} = \frac{X_m}{\sqrt2}
$$

Household "120 V" is RMS: the peak is $120\sqrt2 \approx 170$ V. Power engineers always use RMS phasors.

## Average (real) power

$$
P = V_{rms}I_{rms}\cos(\theta_v - \theta_i) \qquad \text{[W]}
$$

Resistors absorb all of it ($\theta = 0$). Ideal inductors and capacitors absorb **zero** average power ($\theta = \pm90^\circ$) — they borrow energy and return it every half cycle.

## Complex power

Define

$$
\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{V}_{rms}\mathbf{I}_{rms}^{*} = P + jQ \qquad \text{[VA]}
$$

- $P$ = real power (W) — does useful work.
- $Q = V_{rms}I_{rms}\sin(\theta_v - \theta_i)$ = **reactive power** (VAR). Positive for inductive loads, negative for capacitive.
- $|\mathbf{S}|$ = **apparent power** (VA) — what the wires and transformers must be sized for.

Equivalent forms: $\mathbf{S} = I_{rms}^2\mathbf{Z} = V_{rms}^2/\mathbf{Z}^*$. Complex power is **conserved**: the sum of $\mathbf{S}$ over all elements is zero.

## Power factor

$$
\text{pf} = \frac{P}{|\mathbf{S}|} = \cos(\theta_v - \theta_i)
$$

It is **lagging** when current lags voltage (inductive loads — motors) and **leading** when current leads (capacitive). A low power factor means more current, and therefore more $I^2R$ loss, for the same useful power. Utilities charge industrial customers penalties for low pf.

## Worked example

> A load $\mathbf{Z} = 30 + j40\ \Omega$ is connected to 100 V RMS.
>
> $I_{rms} = 100/50 = 2$ A RMS, lagging by 53.13°.
>
> $P = I^2R = 4(30) = 120$ W, $\quad Q = I^2X = 4(40) = 160$ VAR, $\quad |\mathbf S| = 200$ VA.
>
> $\text{pf} = 120/200 = 0.6$ lagging.


```quiz
? A load draws 10 A RMS at 240 V RMS with a power factor of 0.8 lagging. What is the real power, in W?
= 1920 | 0.01 | W
> $P = 240 \times 10 \times 0.8 = 1920$ W.
```

```quiz
? For the same load (240 V, 10 A, pf 0.8 lagging), what is the reactive power, in VAR?
= 1440 | 0.01 | VAR
> $\sin\theta = 0.6$; $Q = 2400 \times 0.6 = 1440$ VAR (positive: inductive).
```

```quiz
? An ideal capacitor connected to an AC source absorbs:
- positive real power and zero reactive power
+ zero real power and negative reactive power
- zero real power and positive reactive power
- positive real and reactive power
> $\theta_v - \theta_i = -90^\circ$: $P = 0$, $Q < 0$. Capacitors *supply* VARs.
```

## Key takeaways

- Use RMS: $X_{rms} = X_m/\sqrt2$ for sinusoids.
- $\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{V}\mathbf{I}^* = P + jQ$; $|\mathbf S|$ sizes the equipment.
- pf = $\cos\theta$; lagging = inductive.
