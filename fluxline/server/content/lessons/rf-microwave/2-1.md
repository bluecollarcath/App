## Designing with S-parameters

At microwave frequencies, a transistor is characterized not by $g_m$ and capacitances but by its measured **S-parameters** at the chosen bias (Lesson 1.1). Amplifier design then becomes: choose source and load reflection coefficients $\Gamma_S$ and $\Gamma_L$ (realized with matching networks, Lesson 1.2) that make the amplifier stable and give the gain, noise or output power you want.

## Stability first

A transistor with internal feedback ($S_{12} \ne 0$) can show an input or output reflection coefficient with magnitude **greater than 1**, a negative resistance, for some source or load impedances. Then it oscillates. A device is **unconditionally stable** (stable for every passive source and load) if

$$
K = \frac{1 - |S_{11}|^2 - |S_{22}|^2 + |\Delta|^2}{2|S_{12}S_{21}|} > 1 \quad\text{and}\quad |\Delta| < 1, \qquad \Delta = S_{11}S_{22} - S_{12}S_{21}
$$

This is the **Rollett stability factor**. If $K < 1$, the device is only **conditionally stable**: plotting **stability circles** on the Smith chart shows which $\Gamma_S$ and $\Gamma_L$ regions to avoid. Designers often add a small series or shunt resistor, or feedback, to make $K > 1$ at all frequencies (including far outside the band, where oscillations also happen).

## Gain

The **transducer gain** (power delivered to the load over power available from the source) for a unilateral device ($S_{12} \approx 0$) separates into three factors:

$$
G_{TU} = \underbrace{\frac{1 - |\Gamma_S|^2}{|1 - S_{11}\Gamma_S|^2}}_{G_S}\ \underbrace{|S_{21}|^2}_{G_0}\ \underbrace{\frac{1 - |\Gamma_L|^2}{|1 - S_{22}\Gamma_L|^2}}_{G_L}
$$

$G_0 = |S_{21}|^2$ is the gain in a plain 50 Ω system; the matching networks add $G_S$ and $G_L$. Conjugate matching ($\Gamma_S = S_{11}^*$, $\Gamma_L = S_{22}^*$) maximizes both:

$$
G_{TU,max} = \frac{1}{1 - |S_{11}|^2}\,|S_{21}|^2\,\frac{1}{1 - |S_{22}|^2}
$$

> **Example.** A transistor at 2 GHz has $S_{11} = 0.5\angle{-60°}$, $S_{21} = 4\angle90°$, $S_{12} = 0.05\angle30°$, $S_{22} = 0.4\angle{-45°}$.
>
> Stability: $|\Delta| = 0.37$, $K = 1.82$. Unconditionally stable.
>
> Gain: $G_0 = 16$ (12.0 dB); matching adds $1/(1 - 0.25) = 1.25$ dB at the input and $1/(1 - 0.16) = 0.76$ dB at the output, for $G_{TU,max} = 14.0$ dB.

**Constant-gain circles** on the Smith chart show all $\Gamma_S$ (or $\Gamma_L$) giving a particular gain below the maximum. Designers deliberately mismatch for flat gain over a band, or to trade gain for lower noise (next lesson) or more output power.

## Key takeaways

- Design by choosing $\Gamma_S$ and $\Gamma_L$ with matching networks around the transistor's S-parameters.
- Unconditional stability: $K > 1$ and $|\Delta| < 1$; otherwise use stability circles or add resistive loading.
- $G_{TU} = G_SG_0G_L$; conjugate matching gives the maximum; gain circles map the trade-offs.
