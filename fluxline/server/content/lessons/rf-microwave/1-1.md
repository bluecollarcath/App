## Why not Z or Y parameters?

At low frequencies, two-port networks are characterized with open- and short-circuit measurements (Z, Y, h parameters). At microwave frequencies, good opens and shorts are impossible to make, and many active devices oscillate under those terminations. Instead we terminate every port in a matched load (usually 50 Ω) and measure **traveling waves**. These are the **scattering parameters**.

## Definition

At each port $n$, define incident and reflected power waves $a_n$ and $b_n$ (with $|a_n|^2$ = incident power). For a two-port:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} S_{11} & S_{12} \\ S_{21} & S_{22} \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{bmatrix}
$$

- $S_{11} = b_1/a_1$ with port 2 matched ($a_2 = 0$): input reflection coefficient.
- $S_{21} = b_2/a_1$: forward transmission (gain or insertion loss).
- $S_{12}$: reverse transmission (isolation).
- $S_{22}$: output reflection coefficient.

A **vector network analyzer** measures all four directly.

## Reflection coefficient and friends

For a load $Z_L$ on a reference impedance $Z_0$:

$$
\Gamma = \frac{Z_L - Z_0}{Z_L + Z_0}
$$

Common figures of merit:

$$
\text{RL} = -20\log_{10}|\Gamma|\ \text{dB},
\qquad
\text{VSWR} = \frac{1 + |\Gamma|}{1 - |\Gamma|},
\qquad
\text{IL} = -20\log_{10}|S_{21}|\ \text{dB}
$$

> **Example.** A 75 Ω antenna on a 50 Ω system: $\Gamma = 25/125 = 0.2$, RL = 14.0 dB, VSWR = 1.5. Only $|\Gamma|^2 = 4\%$ of the power is reflected.

## Network properties

- **Reciprocal** (passive, no ferrites): $S_{12} = S_{21}$, i.e., $\mathbf S = \mathbf S^T$.
- **Lossless**: $\mathbf S$ is unitary, $\mathbf S^H\mathbf S = \mathbf I$. For a two-port this gives $|S_{11}|^2 + |S_{21}|^2 = 1$.
- **Matched**: $S_{nn} = 0$.


## Key takeaways

- S-parameters relate incident and reflected waves under matched terminations.
- $\Gamma = (Z_L - Z_0)/(Z_L + Z_0)$; RL, VSWR, and IL derive from it.
- Reciprocal ⇒ symmetric S; lossless ⇒ unitary S.
