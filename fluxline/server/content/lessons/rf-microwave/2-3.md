## Moving signals in frequency

Every radio translates signals between frequencies: down from the antenna to an IF or baseband in the receiver, up to the carrier in the transmitter (Communication Systems, Lesson 1.3). That takes two building blocks: a **mixer** to multiply, and an **oscillator** to provide a clean local-oscillator (LO) signal.

## Mixers

An ideal mixer multiplies the RF signal by the LO, producing the sum and difference frequencies. Real mixers use a nonlinear or switching device driven hard by the LO:

- **Diode mixers** (single, balanced or double-balanced rings): passive, very linear, broadband; typically 6–8 dB **conversion loss**.
- **Active mixers**, such as the **Gilbert cell** (a differential pair whose tail current is steered by the LO): conversion **gain**, less LO power needed; standard in ICs.
- **Passive FET (switching) mixers**: CMOS switches commutating the signal; excellent linearity, common in modern radio chips.

Key specifications:

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Conversion gain/loss | IF power ÷ RF power |
| Noise figure | as for amplifiers (Lesson 2.2) |
| Isolation | LO leakage to the RF and IF ports |
| Linearity | IIP3, P1dB (Lesson 3.1) |
| Spurious responses | unwanted products $\lvert mf_{LO} \pm nf_{RF}\rvert$ that fall in the IF band |

**Balanced** and **double-balanced** mixers cancel the LO and/or RF at the output, improving isolation and suppressing even-order spurs. **Image-reject** mixers use two mixers with LOs 90° apart and a 90° hybrid (Lesson 1.3) to cancel the image band, relaxing the RF filter.

## Oscillators

An oscillator is an amplifier with positive feedback through a resonator (Microelectronics II, Lesson 4.2). At microwave frequencies it's easier to think of a **negative resistance**: a transistor with suitable feedback presents $\text{Re}\{Z_{in}\} < 0$ at its port, and connected to a resonator with positive loss resistance, it oscillates where the reactances cancel and the negative resistance exceeds the loss.

A **VCO** (voltage-controlled oscillator) tunes its frequency with a varactor (Semiconductor Devices, Lesson 2.3). Resonator choices: LC tanks on chip (Q ≈ 10–20), transmission-line or dielectric resonators (Q in the thousands), crystals for references.

## Phase noise

A real oscillator's output isn't a perfect line in the spectrum: random phase fluctuations spread it into skirts. **Phase noise** $\mathcal L(\Delta f)$ is the noise power in 1 Hz at offset $\Delta f$ from the carrier, relative to the carrier power, in **dBc/Hz** (for example −110 dBc/Hz at 100 kHz offset). **Leeson's model** shows it falls with higher resonator Q and higher signal power.

Phase noise matters because, in a receiver, the LO's noise skirt mixes a strong **adjacent-channel** signal right into the IF band (**reciprocal mixing**), burying the weak wanted signal. In a transmitter it spreads energy into neighboring channels, and in digital modulation it rotates constellation points, adding errors.

## Frequency synthesis

Radios need many precise, stable frequencies from one crystal. A **phase-locked loop** (PLL) locks a VCO to a multiple of a crystal reference: a phase detector compares the reference with the VCO frequency divided by $N$, and a loop filter steers the VCO:

$$
f_{out} = N f_{ref}
$$

> With a 1 MHz reference and $N = 2400$, the output is 2.4 GHz; changing $N$ steps it in 1 MHz increments. **Fractional-N** synthesizers let $N$ take non-integer average values for much finer steps.

Inside the loop bandwidth, the output tracks the reference's phase noise multiplied by $N$, a penalty of $20\log_{10}N$ dB (67.6 dB for $N = 2400$). Outside it, the VCO's own phase noise dominates. Choosing the loop bandwidth balances the two.

## Key takeaways

- Mixers multiply RF by LO: diode (lossy, linear), Gilbert cell (gain), passive FET (linear); balanced types improve isolation.
- Oscillators: negative resistance plus a resonator; VCOs tune with varactors.
- Phase noise (dBc/Hz) causes reciprocal mixing and spectral spreading; higher Q and power reduce it.
- PLL synthesizers give $f_{out} = Nf_{ref}$; in-loop phase noise rises by $20\log_{10}N$.
