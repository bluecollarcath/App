## The FE Electrical and Computer exam

The **Fundamentals of Engineering** exam is the first step toward a PE license (Lesson 2.1), and many employers value it on its own. The Electrical and Computer version is computer-based: **110 questions** in **5 hours 20 minutes** of testing time, about **2.9 minutes per question**. Topics span the whole undergraduate curriculum: mathematics, circuits, electronics, signals, power, electromagnetics, control, communications, digital systems, computer systems, and ethics.

The only reference allowed is the **NCEES FE Reference Handbook**, provided as a searchable PDF on screen. You may use an NCEES-approved calculator (models from the Casio fx-115/991, HP 33s/35s and TI-30X/36X families).

## Strategy

- **Know the handbook.** Download it free from NCEES and practice with it. The search box is your most valuable tool, but only if you know which words to search for. Use the handbook's notation in your own practice.
- **Two passes.** Answer the quick questions first; mark longer ones and come back. Never leave a question blank: there's no penalty for guessing.
- **Estimate before calculating**, then check that your answer is in the right range and has the right units. Many wrong options come from common mistakes (peak vs. RMS, radians vs. degrees, line vs. phase quantities).
- **Practice timed.** Speed matters as much as knowledge.

The problems below are typical of the circuits and electronics portion. Try each before reading the solution.

## Worked problems

> **1. Thévenin equivalent.** A 12 V source with 4 kΩ in series feeds a 12 kΩ resistor to ground. Find the voltage across a 6 kΩ load connected across the 12 kΩ.
>
> $V_{th} = 12 \times 12/16 = 9$ V; $R_{th} = 4 \parallel 12 = 3$ kΩ. Load voltage $= 9 \times 6/9 = 6$ V.

> **2. RC transient.** A 5 V step charges a 10 µF capacitor through 10 kΩ from 0 V. What is the voltage after 0.2 s?
>
> $\tau = 0.1$ s, so $v = 5(1 - e^{-2}) = 4.32$ V.

> **3. Resonance.** A series RLC has $L = 10$ mH, $C = 1\ \mu$F and $R = 10\ \Omega$. Find $f_0$ and $Q$.
>
> $f_0 = 1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC}) = 1.59$ kHz; $Q = (1/R)\sqrt{L/C} = 10$.

> **4. Power-factor correction.** A 10 kW load runs at 0.8 pf lagging. What capacitive kVAR brings it to unity?
>
> $Q = P\tan(\cos^{-1}0.8) = 10 \times 0.75 = 7.5$ kVAR.

> **5. Op-amp.** A non-inverting amplifier has $R_f = 47$ kΩ and $R_1 = 4.7$ kΩ. Gain $= 1 + 47/4.7 = 11$.

> **6. Diode circuit.** 5 V drives a silicon diode (0.7 V) and 2.2 kΩ in series: $I = 4.3/2.2 = 1.95$ mA.

> **7. BJT.** With $\beta = 100$ and $I_B = 10\ \mu$A, $I_C = 1$ mA, and $g_m = I_C/V_T = 40$ mS.

> **8. MOSFET.** In saturation with $k_n = 2$ mA/V² and $V_{ov} = 0.5$ V: $I_D = \tfrac12k_nV_{ov}^2 = 0.25$ mA.

## Common traps

- **RMS vs. peak:** household 120 V is RMS; its peak is 170 V. Power formulas use RMS.
- **Line vs. phase:** in a wye system, $V_{LL} = \sqrt3V_{ph}$; three-phase power is $\sqrt3V_{LL}I_L\cos\theta$.
- **Calculator mode:** radians for $e^{j\omega t}$ and $\omega t$; degrees for phasor angles.
- **Units and prefixes:** kΩ and mA multiply to volts; µF and kΩ give milliseconds.

## Practice (timed: aim for 3 minutes each)

```quiz
? A 24 V source with 6 kΩ in series feeds 3 kΩ to ground. What is the Thévenin resistance seen across the 3 kΩ, in kΩ?
= 2 | 0.001 | kΩ
> $6 \parallel 3$.
```

```quiz
? An RL circuit has $R = 50\ \Omega$ and $L = 20$ mH. What is its time constant, in ms?
= 0.4 | 0.001 | ms
> $L/R$.
```

```quiz
? A series RLC has $L = 1$ mH and $C = 10$ nF. What is its resonant frequency, in kHz?
= 50.33 | 0.005 | kHz
> $1/(2\pi\sqrt{10^{-11}})$.
```

```quiz
? A load draws 20 kW at 0.6 pf lagging. How much capacitive kVAR corrects it to unity?
= 26.67 | 0.005 | kVAR
> $20\tan(\cos^{-1}0.6) = 20 \times 4/3$.
```

```quiz
? An inverting amplifier has $R_f = 100$ kΩ and $R_1 = 20$ kΩ. What is its gain?
= -5 | 0.001 |
> $-R_f/R_1$.
```

```quiz
? A household outlet supplies 230 V RMS. What is its peak voltage, in V?
= 325.3 | 0.002 | V
> $230\sqrt2$.
```

```quiz
? How many minutes per question does the FE exam's timing allow, on average?
= 2.91 | 0.01 | min
> 320 minutes / 110 questions.
```

## Key takeaways

- 110 questions in 5 h 20 min with only the NCEES Reference Handbook: know where things are in it.
- Two passes, never leave blanks, estimate and check units.
- Watch RMS vs. peak, line vs. phase, radians vs. degrees, and prefixes.
