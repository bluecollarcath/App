## When there are more equations than unknowns

Fit a straight line through 50 measured points, calibrate a sensor from 20 readings, estimate a battery's resistance from noisy voltage–current data. Each gives an **overdetermined** system $A\mathbf x = \mathbf b$ with more equations than unknowns and, because of noise, **no exact solution**. The best you can do is make the error as small as possible.

## The least-squares solution

Choose $\hat{\mathbf x}$ to minimize the total squared error $\|\mathbf b - A\mathbf x\|^2$. Geometrically, $A\hat{\mathbf x}$ is the **projection** of $\mathbf b$ onto the column space of $A$ (Lesson 2.3), so the residual is perpendicular to every column, giving the **normal equations**:

$$
A^TA\,\hat{\mathbf x} = A^T\mathbf b
$$

$A^TA$ is square and symmetric, and invertible when $A$'s columns are independent.

## Fitting a line

To fit $y = c_0 + c_1t$ to data $(t_i, y_i)$, each point gives one equation $c_0 + c_1t_i = y_i$:

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & t_1 \\ 1 & t_2 \\ \vdots & \vdots \\ 1 & t_m \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf x = \begin{bmatrix} c_0 \\ c_1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf b = \begin{bmatrix} y_1 \\ \vdots \\ y_m \end{bmatrix}
$$

> **Example.** Points $(0, 1)$, $(1, 3)$, $(2, 4)$, $(3, 6)$.
> $$A^TA = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 6 \\ 6 & 14 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad A^T\mathbf b = \begin{bmatrix} 14 \\ 29 \end{bmatrix}$$
> Solving: $c_0 = 1.1$, $c_1 = 1.6$. The best line is $y = 1.1 + 1.6t$, with residuals $(-0.1, 0.3, -0.3, 0.1)$ and a sum of squared errors of 0.2. The residuals sum to zero, as they always do for a fit that includes a constant term.

```plot
x: -0.3, 3.3, t
y: 0, 7, y
f: 1.1 + 1.6*x | least-squares line y = 1.1 + 1.6t
mark: 0, 1
mark: 1, 3
mark: 2, 4
mark: 3, 6
caption: The least-squares line through four noisy points minimizes the sum of the squared vertical distances.
```

The same method fits any model that's **linear in its unknown coefficients**: polynomials ($c_0 + c_1t + c_2t^2$), sums of sinusoids, or exponentials after taking logarithms. A typical engineering use: measure $V$ at several currents and fit $V = V_{oc} - IR_{int}$ to find a battery's open-circuit voltage and internal resistance.

## Computing it well

Forming $A^TA$ squares the condition number (Lesson 1.3), losing accuracy for ill-conditioned problems. Robust software instead uses the **QR factorization** ($A = QR$, then solve $R\hat{\mathbf x} = Q^T\mathbf b$) or the SVD (Lesson 3.4). In Python, `numpy.linalg.lstsq` does this; in MATLAB, `A\b`.

Least squares is the foundation of estimation and machine learning: linear regression, system identification, the Kalman filter, and the training of neural networks all minimize squared error.

## Key takeaways

- Overdetermined systems: minimize $\|\mathbf b - A\mathbf x\|^2$ by solving $A^TA\hat{\mathbf x} = A^T\mathbf b$.
- $A\hat{\mathbf x}$ is the projection of $\mathbf b$ onto the column space; the residual is orthogonal to it.
- Any model linear in its coefficients can be fitted this way.
- Use QR or SVD in practice; least squares underlies estimation and machine learning.
