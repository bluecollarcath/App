## A different description: state

A transfer function relates one input to one output. **State-space models** describe a system by its internal **state**: the smallest set of variables (positions, velocities, capacitor voltages, inductor currents) that, together with the inputs, determines all future behavior. They handle multiple inputs and outputs naturally, they're what modern control design and simulation use, and they're how computers represent dynamics.

$$
\dot{\mathbf x} = \mathbf A\mathbf x + \mathbf B\mathbf u, \qquad \mathbf y = \mathbf C\mathbf x + \mathbf D\mathbf u
$$

For $n$ states, $m$ inputs and $p$ outputs, $\mathbf A$ is $n\times n$, $\mathbf B$ is $n\times m$, $\mathbf C$ is $p\times n$, and $\mathbf D$ is $p\times m$ (usually zero).

## From a differential equation to state space

Pick the output and its derivatives as states.

> **Example.** $\ddot y + 3\dot y + 2y = u$. Let $x_1 = y$ and $x_2 = \dot y$. Then $\dot x_1 = x_2$ and $\dot x_2 = -2x_1 - 3x_2 + u$:
> $$\mathbf A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -2 & -3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf B = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf C = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf D = 0$$

For a circuit, the natural states are **capacitor voltages and inductor currents**, the energy-storage variables.

## From state space to a transfer function

Take Laplace transforms with zero initial conditions:

$$
G(s) = \mathbf C(s\mathbf I - \mathbf A)^{-1}\mathbf B + \mathbf D
$$

The denominator is $\det(s\mathbf I - \mathbf A)$, so **the poles are the eigenvalues of $\mathbf A$**.

> For the example: $\det(s\mathbf I - \mathbf A) = s^2 + 3s + 2$, so $G(s) = \dfrac{1}{s^2 + 3s + 2}$ with poles at $-1$ and $-2$, the eigenvalues of $\mathbf A$. DC gain: $G(0) = -\mathbf C\mathbf A^{-1}\mathbf B = 0.5$.

A given transfer function has many state-space realizations (states can be chosen in many ways), but they all share the same eigenvalues.

## Key takeaways

- $\dot{\mathbf x} = \mathbf A\mathbf x + \mathbf B\mathbf u$, $\mathbf y = \mathbf C\mathbf x + \mathbf D\mathbf u$; states are the energy variables.
- ODE → state space by choosing $y, \dot y, \ldots$ as states.
- $G(s) = \mathbf C(s\mathbf I - \mathbf A)^{-1}\mathbf B + \mathbf D$; poles = eigenvalues of $\mathbf A$.
