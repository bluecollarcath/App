## What control engineering does

A **control system** makes a physical quantity — motor speed, drone attitude, converter output voltage, chemical temperature — follow a desired reference despite disturbances and uncertainty. The central tool is **feedback**: measure the output, compare it to the reference, and act on the error.

## Transfer functions

For a linear time-invariant system with zero initial conditions, the **transfer function** is the ratio of output to input Laplace transforms:

$$
G(s) = \frac{Y(s)}{U(s)} = \frac{b_m s^m + \cdots + b_0}{s^n + a_{n-1}s^{n-1} + \cdots + a_0}
$$

Roots of the numerator are **zeros**; roots of the denominator are **poles**. Poles determine the natural response and stability: all poles in the left half-plane ⇒ stable.

> **A DC motor.** With armature resistance $R$, torque constant $K$, inertia $J$, and negligible inductance and friction, voltage to speed is a first-order system:
> $$\frac{\Omega(s)}{V(s)} = \frac{K/(RJ)}{s + K^2/(RJ)}$$

The feedback formula is the most important equation in the course. $L(s) = G(s)H(s)$ is the **loop gain**.

## Why feedback works

If $|GH| \gg 1$, then $T = \frac{G}{1+GH} \approx \frac{1}{H}$: the closed-loop behavior depends almost entirely on the **sensor** $H$, not on the uncertain plant $G$. Feedback also

- reduces the effect of disturbances by a factor of $1 + GH$,
- reduces sensitivity to parameter variation: $S^T_G = \frac{1}{1 + GH}$,
- can stabilize unstable plants — or destabilize stable ones if designed poorly.

> **Worked example.** $G(s) = \dfrac{10}{s + 2}$ with unity feedback ($H = 1$):
> $$T(s) = \frac{10/(s+2)}{1 + 10/(s+2)} = \frac{10}{s + 12}$$
> The pole moved from $-2$ to $-12$: the closed loop responds **6× faster**. DC gain is $T(0) = 10/12 \approx 0.83$, so there is a 17% steady-state error to a step — we'll eliminate it with integral control.

## Key takeaways

- Transfer function = output/input in the Laplace domain; poles set the dynamics.
- Negative feedback: $T = G/(1 + GH)$.
- High loop gain makes performance depend on the sensor, not the plant.
