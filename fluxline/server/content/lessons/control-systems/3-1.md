## Where do the poles go as gain increases?

Turn up the gain of a feedback loop and the closed-loop poles move. The **root locus** is the path they trace in the s-plane as a gain $K$ sweeps from 0 to $\infty$. It shows at a glance which gains give a fast, well-damped response, and at what gain the system goes unstable.

For unity feedback around $KG(s)$, the closed-loop poles are the roots of

$$
1 + KG(s) = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad KG(s) = -1
$$

A point $s$ is on the locus when it meets two conditions:

- **Angle condition:** $\angle G(s) = 180° \pm k\,360°$. This decides *whether* $s$ is on the locus.
- **Magnitude condition:** $K = 1/|G(s)|$. This gives the gain that puts a pole *there*.

## Sketching rules

With $n$ open-loop poles and $m$ open-loop zeros:

1. **Start and end.** The locus has $n$ branches. They start ($K = 0$) at the open-loop poles and end ($K \to \infty$) at the open-loop zeros; the $n - m$ branches with no zero to end at go off to infinity.
2. **Real axis.** A point on the real axis is on the locus if the total number of real poles and zeros **to its right** is odd.
3. **Asymptotes.** The branches heading to infinity follow straight lines at angles
$$\theta_k = \frac{(2k + 1)\,180°}{n - m}, \qquad k = 0, 1, \ldots$$
all leaving one point on the real axis, the **centroid**
$$\sigma_a = \frac{\sum \text{poles} - \sum \text{zeros}}{n - m}$$
4. **Breakaway points.** Where two branches meet on the real axis and split off into the complex plane, $K$ is at a local maximum along the axis: solve $dK/ds = 0$ with $K = -1/G(s)$.
5. **Imaginary-axis crossings.** Use the Routh array (Lesson 2.2): the gain that makes a row of zeros is the gain at the crossing, and the auxiliary polynomial gives the frequency.

## A complete example

Sketch the locus for

$$
G(s) = \frac{1}{s(s + 2)(s + 4)}
$$

- **Poles:** $0, -2, -4$; no zeros. Three branches, all going to infinity.
- **Real axis:** the segments $[-2, 0]$ (one pole to the right) and $(-\infty, -4]$ (three to the right).
- **Asymptotes:** $n - m = 3$, so the angles are $60°, 180°, 300°$, from $\sigma_a = (0 - 2 - 4)/3 = -2$.
- **Breakaway:** $K = -(s^3 + 6s^2 + 8s)$, so $dK/ds = -(3s^2 + 12s + 8) = 0$ gives $s = -0.845$ or $-3.155$. Only $-0.845$ lies on a locus segment; the gain there is $K = 3.08$.
- **Crossing:** the characteristic equation is $s^3 + 6s^2 + 8s + K = 0$. The Routh $s^1$ row is $(48 - K)/6$, which vanishes at $K = 48$. The auxiliary polynomial $6s^2 + 48 = 0$ gives $s = \pm j2.83$.

## Designing with the locus

The damping ratio of a complex pole is fixed by its angle: poles on a ray at angle $\theta = \cos^{-1}\zeta$ from the negative real axis all have the same $\zeta$. To get $\zeta = 0.5$ (16% overshoot), find where the locus crosses the $60°$ ray and read off the gain.

> **Example.** For the system above, the locus crosses the $\zeta = 0.5$ ray at $s = -0.667 \pm j1.155$. The magnitude condition gives
>
> $$
> K = |s|\,|s + 2|\,|s + 4| = 8.30
> $$
>
> The third pole sits at $-4.67$ (the poles must sum to $-6$). It is seven times farther left than the complex pair, so it dies out quickly, and the pair **dominates**: expect about 16% overshoot and a settling time of about $4/0.667 = 6$ s.

If no gain gives the response you want, the locus must be **reshaped**, by adding poles and zeros with a compensator. That's the next lesson.

## Key takeaways

- The root locus shows the closed-loop poles as $K$ goes from 0 to $\infty$; points satisfy $\angle G = 180°$.
- Branches start at poles and end at zeros or along asymptotes at $(2k + 1)180°/(n - m)$ from the centroid.
- Breakaway from $dK/ds = 0$; imaginary-axis crossings from Routh.
- Pick $K$ where the locus crosses the constant-$\zeta$ ray, then check that the other poles are far enough left to be negligible.
