## Stability without finding the roots

A closed-loop system is stable if every root of its **characteristic equation** (the denominator of $T(s)$) has a negative real part. For high-order polynomials, especially ones containing an unknown gain $K$, solving for the roots is impractical. The **Routh–Hurwitz criterion** answers the question directly from the coefficients.

### Quick necessary check

For all roots to be in the left half-plane, **every coefficient must be present and have the same sign**. If any coefficient is zero or negative, the system is unstable (or at best marginal). The converse isn't true for third order and above, which is why we need the full test.

## Building the Routh array

For $a_ns^n + a_{n-1}s^{n-1} + \cdots + a_0$:

1. Row $s^n$: $a_n, a_{n-2}, a_{n-4}, \ldots$
2. Row $s^{n-1}$: $a_{n-1}, a_{n-3}, \ldots$
3. Each later entry comes from the two rows above it:
$$b_1 = \frac{a_{n-1}a_{n-2} - a_na_{n-3}}{a_{n-1}}$$
(the first column of the two rows above, cross-multiplied with the next column, divided by the pivot just above).

> **The criterion:** the number of roots in the right half-plane equals the number of **sign changes in the first column**. Stable ⇔ no sign changes.

## Worked example: finding the stable range of $K$

> $$s^3 + 2s^2 + 3s + K = 0$$
>
> | | | |
> |---|---|---|
> | $s^3$ | 1 | 3 |
> | $s^2$ | 2 | $K$ |
> | $s^1$ | $\dfrac{2 \cdot 3 - 1 \cdot K}{2} = \dfrac{6 - K}{2}$ | 0 |
> | $s^0$ | $K$ | |
>
> For no sign changes we need $\frac{6 - K}{2} > 0$ and $K > 0$:
> $$0 < K < 6$$
>
> At $K = 6$ the $s^1$ row is zero: the system is **marginally stable** and oscillates. The auxiliary equation from the row above, $2s^2 + 6 = 0$, gives the oscillation frequency: $\omega = \sqrt3 = 1.73$ rad/s.

For a third-order polynomial $s^3 + a_2s^2 + a_1s + a_0$ with positive coefficients, the array always reduces to one simple test: **stable if $a_2a_1 > a_0$**.

## Special cases

- **A zero in the first column** (but not a whole zero row): replace it with a small $\varepsilon > 0$ and continue.
- **A whole row of zeros:** the polynomial has roots symmetric about the origin, often a pair on the $j\omega$ axis. Form the auxiliary polynomial from the row above, differentiate it, and use its coefficients as the replacement row.

## Practice

```quiz
? For $s^3 + 3s^2 + 2s + K = 0$, what is the largest $K$ for stability?
= 6 | 0.001 |
> $a_2a_1 > a_0$: $3 \times 2 > K$, so $K < 6$.
```

```quiz
? For $s^3 + 4s^2 + 5s + K = 0$, what is the largest $K$ for stability?
= 20 | 0.001 |
> $4 \times 5 = 20 > K$.
```

```quiz
? The polynomial $s^4 + 2s^3 - s^2 + 4s + 1$:
- is stable
+ is unstable, since a coefficient is negative
- is marginally stable
- cannot be tested with Routh–Hurwitz
> A sign change among the coefficients already rules out stability.
```

```quiz
? A Routh array's first column is $1, 4, -2, 3$. How many roots are in the right half-plane?
= 2 | 0 |
> Two sign changes: $4 \to -2$ and $-2 \to 3$.
```

## Key takeaways

- All coefficients must be positive (necessary, not sufficient).
- Build the Routh array; sign changes in the first column = right-half-plane roots.
- Use it to find the stable range of a gain $K$; third order: stable iff $a_2a_1 > a_0$.
