## How fast, how smooth?

Customers specify control systems in terms of their **step response**: how quickly the output reaches a new setpoint, how much it overshoots, and how long it takes to settle. Most closed-loop systems behave like first- or second-order systems, at least approximately, so their simple formulas are the language of control specs.

## First order

$$
G(s) = \frac{K}{\tau s + 1} \quad\Rightarrow\quad y(t) = K\big(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\big)
$$

The time constant $\tau$ sets everything: 63% of the final value at $t = \tau$, rise time (10–90%) of $2.2\tau$, and 2% settling time of about $4\tau$.

## Second order

$$
G(s) = \frac{\omega_n^2}{s^2 + 2\zeta\omega_ns + \omega_n^2}
$$

The **natural frequency** $\omega_n$ sets speed; the **damping ratio** $\zeta$ sets shape. The poles are at $s = -\zeta\omega_n \pm j\omega_n\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}$.

### The underdamped specifications ($0 < \zeta < 1$)

| Spec | Formula |
|---|---|
| Peak time | $t_p = \dfrac{\pi}{\omega_n\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}}$ |
| Percent overshoot | $\%OS = 100\,e^{-\zeta\pi/\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}}$ (depends only on $\zeta$) |
| 2% settling time | $t_s \approx \dfrac{4}{\zeta\omega_n}$ |
| Rise time (10–90%) | $t_r \approx \dfrac{1.8}{\omega_n}$ (for $\zeta \approx 0.5$) |

> **Worked example.** $T(s) = \dfrac{25}{s^2 + 6s + 25}$: $\omega_n = 5$ rad/s, $2\zeta\omega_n = 6$, so $\zeta = 0.6$.
>
> $\%OS = 100e^{-0.6\pi/0.8} = 9.5\%$, $\quad t_p = \pi/4 = 0.785$ s, $\quad t_s = 4/3 = 1.33$ s.

A common design target is $\zeta \approx 0.5$–$0.7$: about 5–16% overshoot, a good balance of speed and smoothness. Specs translate into **pole locations**: overshoot fixes the angle of the poles from the negative real axis ($\zeta = \cos\theta$), and settling time fixes their real part ($\zeta\omega_n = 4/t_s$).

## Dominant poles

A higher-order system often behaves like a second-order one when one pair of poles is much closer to the $j\omega$ axis than the rest (rule of thumb: 5× closer). Those **dominant poles** set the response, because the others' terms decay much faster.

## Key takeaways

- First order: $\tau$; settles in about $4\tau$.
- Second order: $\%OS$ depends on $\zeta$; $t_p = \pi/\omega_d$; $t_s \approx 4/(\zeta\omega_n)$.
- Specs map to pole locations; dominant poles set the response.
