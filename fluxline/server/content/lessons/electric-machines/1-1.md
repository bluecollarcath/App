## The analogy

Motors, generators, transformers, and inductors all guide magnetic flux through iron. Analyzing them uses a **magnetic circuit**, directly analogous to an electric circuit:

| Electric | Magnetic |
|---|---|
| EMF $V$ (V) | MMF $\mathcal F = NI$ (A·turns) |
| Current $I$ (A) | Flux $\Phi$ (Wb) |
| Resistance $R = \ell/(\sigma A)$ | Reluctance $\mathcal R = \ell/(\mu A)$ |
| $V = IR$ | $\mathcal F = \Phi\mathcal R$ |

where $\mu = \mu_r\mu_0$ and $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}$ H/m. Reluctances in series add, just like resistors.

## Air gaps dominate

Iron has $\mu_r$ in the thousands; air has $\mu_r = 1$. So a 1 mm gap can have more reluctance than 40 cm of iron. Machines need air gaps (the rotor must turn), and transformers try to avoid them.

> **Worked example.** An iron core has mean length 0.4 m, cross-section 10 cm², $\mu_r = 2000$, and a 200-turn coil carrying 1 A.
>
> $\mathcal R_{core} = \dfrac{0.4}{2000(4\pi\times10^{-7})(10^{-3})} \approx 1.59\times10^5$ A·t/Wb
>
> $\Phi = 200/1.59\times10^5 = 1.26$ mWb, so $B = \Phi/A = 1.26$ T.
>
> Now cut a 1 mm air gap: $\mathcal R_{gap} = \dfrac{10^{-3}}{(4\pi\times10^{-7})(10^{-3})} \approx 7.96\times10^5$ A·t/Wb — **five times** the entire core.
>
> $\Phi = 200/(9.55\times10^5) = 0.209$ mWb, and $B$ drops to 0.21 T.

## Inductance from reluctance

Flux linkage is $\lambda = N\Phi = N^2 I/\mathcal R$, so

$$
L = \frac{N^2}{\mathcal R}
$$

Inductors for power converters often include a deliberate gap: it lowers $L$ but keeps the core out of saturation at high current, and makes $L$ predictable despite variation in $\mu_r$.

## Nonlinearity

Real iron saturates (silicon steel near 1.5–1.8 T) and exhibits **hysteresis**, a loop in the $B$–$H$ curve whose area is energy lost per cycle. Saturation limits how much torque a machine can produce for its size; hysteresis and eddy currents produce **core loss**.

## Key takeaways

- $\mathcal F = NI = \Phi\mathcal R$, $\mathcal R = \ell/(\mu A)$.
- Air gaps dominate total reluctance.
- $L = N^2/\mathcal R$.
