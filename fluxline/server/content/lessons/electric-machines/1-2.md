## Two windings on one core

A transformer couples two windings through a shared magnetic core (Lesson 1.1). The same changing flux links both, so by Faraday's law each turn has the same induced voltage. With $N_1$ and $N_2$ turns and turns ratio $a = N_1/N_2$:

$$
\frac{V_1}{V_2} = a, \qquad \frac{I_1}{I_2} = \frac1a, \qquad V_1I_1 = V_2I_2
$$

An **ideal** transformer (no losses, no leakage, infinitely permeable core) is a perfect power-preserving voltage converter.

## Referring impedances

An impedance $Z_2$ on the secondary looks, from the primary, like

$$
Z_2' = a^2Z_2
$$

since the voltage is scaled by $a$ and the current by $1/a$. This **impedance transformation** is how audio output transformers match a high-impedance tube amplifier to an 8 Ω speaker, and how analysis "moves" everything to one side of a transformer. (Per-unit analysis, Power Systems Lesson 1.2, does the same thing automatically.)

## The real transformer

Real transformers have winding resistances, **leakage** flux that links only one winding, a finite-permeability core that needs a **magnetizing** current, and **core losses** (hysteresis and eddy currents). The standard equivalent circuit, referred to the primary, has series $R_{eq} + jX_{eq}$ and a shunt magnetizing branch $R_c \parallel jX_m$ (Power Systems, Lesson 1.3, shows it).

## Finding the parameters: two tests

**Open-circuit test** (rated voltage on the low-voltage side, other side open): the current is just the magnetizing current, and the power measured is the **core loss**.

$$
R_c = \frac{V_{oc}^2}{P_{oc}}
$$

**Short-circuit test** (secondary shorted, reduced voltage raised until rated current flows, usually on the high-voltage side): the core is barely excited, so the power is the **copper loss**, and the series impedance follows.

$$
R_{eq} = \frac{P_{sc}}{I_{sc}^2}, \qquad |Z_{eq}| = \frac{V_{sc}}{I_{sc}}, \qquad X_{eq} = \sqrt{|Z_{eq}|^2 - R_{eq}^2}
$$

> **Example: a 20 kVA, 2400/240 V transformer.**
>
> Open-circuit test (LV side): 240 V, 110 W. So $R_c = 240^2/110 = 524\ \Omega$ (referred to the LV side), and the core loss is 110 W.
>
> Short-circuit test (HV side): 80 V at rated current $20\,000/2400 = 8.33$ A, 330 W. So $R_{eq} = 330/8.33^2 = 4.75\ \Omega$, $|Z_{eq}| = 80/8.33 = 9.6\ \Omega$, and $X_{eq} = 8.34\ \Omega$ (referred to the HV side). Only 80 V (3.3% of rated) drives full current: the per-unit impedance is 0.033.
>
> Full-load efficiency at 0.8 pf: $P_{out} = 16$ kW, losses $110 + 330 = 440$ W, so $\eta = 16/16.44 = 97.3\%$.

## Regulation

Under load, the series impedance drops voltage. **Voltage regulation** is the rise in secondary voltage when full load is removed, as a fraction of the full-load voltage. For lagging power factor it's approximately $R_{pu}\cos\theta + X_{pu}\sin\theta$; at a leading power factor (capacitive load) it can even be negative.

## Key takeaways

- Ideal transformer: $V_1/V_2 = a$, $I_1/I_2 = 1/a$; impedances refer as $a^2Z$.
- Open-circuit test → core loss and magnetizing branch; short-circuit test → copper loss and series impedance.
- Real transformers lose a few percent at most; regulation $\approx R\cos\theta + X\sin\theta$.
