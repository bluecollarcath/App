## Storing charge and energy

A **capacitor** is two conductors separated by an insulator. Connect it to a voltage $V$ and charges $+Q$ and $-Q$ collect on the two conductors, in proportion to the voltage:

$$
Q = CV
$$

The **capacitance** $C$, in farads, depends only on geometry and the insulating material. One farad is enormous; practical values run from picofarads to millifarads (and supercapacitors reach thousands of farads).

## The parallel-plate capacitor

Plates of area $A$ separated by $d$: the field between them is uniform, $E = \sigma/\varepsilon_0 = Q/(\varepsilon_0A)$ (Lesson 1.3), and $V = Ed$. So

$$
C = \frac{\kappa\varepsilon_0A}{d}
$$

where $\kappa$ (the **dielectric constant**, or relative permittivity $\varepsilon_r$) accounts for the insulator. Bigger plates and thinner gaps give more capacitance.

> $A = 1\ \text{cm}^2$, $d = 0.1$ mm, $\kappa = 4$: $C = 4(8.854\times10^{-12})(10^{-4})/10^{-4} = 35$ pF.

## Dielectrics

Inside an insulator, the field slightly displaces each molecule's charges (or aligns polar molecules), creating tiny dipoles: **polarization**. Their charges partly cancel the applied field, so for the same charge the voltage is lower, and the capacitance rises by $\kappa$.

| Material | $\kappa$ | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Vacuum / air | 1 | air-gap tuning capacitors |
| Polypropylene | 2.2 | low-loss film capacitors |
| SiO₂ | 3.9 | transistor gates |
| FR-4 | ~4.3 | circuit boards |
| Al₂O₃ | ~9 | aluminum electrolytics |
| HfO₂ | ~25 | modern transistor gates |
| Barium titanate ceramics | 1000s | multilayer ceramic capacitors |

Every dielectric also has a **breakdown field** above which it fails (about 3 MV/m for air, hundreds of MV/m for thin oxides), which sets the capacitor's voltage rating.

## Energy storage

Charging a capacitor takes work, since each additional charge must be pushed against the voltage already there. The stored energy is

$$
U = \frac12CV^2 = \frac{Q^2}{2C}
$$

> 10 µF charged to 100 V stores $\tfrac12(10^{-5})(10^4) = 0.05$ J. A camera flash dumps that energy in a millisecond: 50 W.

The energy lives in the **field** itself, with density $u = \tfrac12\varepsilon E^2$ (J/m³).

## Combining capacitors

- **Parallel:** voltages equal, charges add: $C_{eq} = C_1 + C_2 + \cdots$.
- **Series:** charges equal, voltages add: $\dfrac{1}{C_{eq}} = \dfrac{1}{C_1} + \dfrac{1}{C_2} + \cdots$.

(The opposite of resistors.)

```

```

## Key takeaways

- $Q = CV$; parallel plates $C = \kappa\varepsilon_0A/d$.
- Dielectrics raise $C$ by $\kappa$ through polarization; breakdown field sets the voltage rating.
- $U = \tfrac12CV^2$, stored in the field with density $\tfrac12\varepsilon E^2$.
- Parallel capacitances add; series combine as reciprocals.
