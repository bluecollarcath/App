## Electric charge

Charge comes in two kinds, positive and negative. Like charges repel; unlike charges attract. Charge is

- **quantized** — every free charge is an integer multiple of $e = 1.602\times10^{-19}$ C, and
- **conserved** — the net charge of an isolated system never changes.

Conductors (metals) have mobile electrons; insulators do not. Semiconductors sit in between and are the subject of an entire later course.

## Coulomb's law

The force between two point charges separated by distance $r$:

$$
\vec F_{12} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}\,\hat r_{12}
\qquad
k = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \approx 8.99\times10^{9}\ \text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2
$$

where $\varepsilon_0 = 8.854\times10^{-12}$ F/m is the permittivity of free space and $\hat r_{12}$ points from $q_1$ to $q_2$. The force is along the line joining the charges; a positive product means repulsion.

> **Worked example.** Two +1 µC charges are 10 cm apart.
> $F = (8.99\times10^9)\frac{(10^{-6})^2}{(0.1)^2} = 0.899$ N — repulsive, roughly the weight of a 90 g mass. A microcoulomb is a *lot* of charge.

## Superposition

Electrostatic forces add as **vectors**. The net force on a charge is the vector sum of the forces from every other charge, each computed as if the others were absent:

$$
\vec F_{\text{net}} = \sum_i \vec F_i
$$

> **Worked example.** $q_1 = +2$ µC at the origin, $q_2 = -2$ µC at $x = 2$ m. Find the force on $q_3 = +1$ µC at $x = 1$ m.
>
> ```circuit
> LINE -0.6,0 4.6,0 dash
> Q 0,0 + q_1
> Q 2,0 + q_3
> Q 4,0 - q_2
> ARROW 2.3,-0.55 3.3,-0.55 F_{1→3} color=anno
> ARROW 2.3,-1.25 3.3,-1.25 F_{2→3} color=anno
> TEXT 1,0.6 "1 m" color=muted size=13
> TEXT 3,0.6 "1 m" color=muted size=13
> caption: $q_1$ pushes $q_3$ away and $q_2$ pulls it closer, so both forces point in $+x$.
> ```
>
> From $q_1$: repelled toward $+x$, $F = 8.99\times10^9\cdot\frac{2\times10^{-12}}{1} = 0.018$ N.
> From $q_2$: attracted toward $+x$, same magnitude.
> Net: $0.036$ N in the $+x$ direction.

## Why this matters for circuits

Coulomb's law is the microscopic origin of capacitance, voltage, and the electric field — the next lessons. It also explains why electrostatic discharge (ESD) destroys MOSFET gates: a few nanocoulombs on a 1 pF gate produce thousands of volts.

## Key takeaways

- Charge is quantized and conserved.
- $F = k\,q_1q_2/r^2$ along the joining line.
- Forces superpose as vectors.
