## When the table runs out

Most integrals that matter don't appear directly in a table. Three techniques convert them into ones that do. (Computer algebra systems do this automatically, but recognizing the structure tells you what the answer will look like, and these exact steps appear throughout Laplace-transform work.)

## Substitution

The chain rule in reverse. If the integrand contains a function $u(t)$ and its derivative, substitute $u$:

$$
\int f\big(u(t)\big)\,u'(t)\,dt = \int f(u)\,du
$$

> $$\int \frac{2x}{x^2 + 1}\,dx \quad (u = x^2 + 1,\ du = 2x\,dx) \quad = \int\frac{du}{u} = \ln(x^2 + 1) + C$$

> $$\int_0^1 xe^{x^2}\,dx \quad (u = x^2) \quad = \frac12\int_0^1e^u\,du = \frac{e - 1}{2} = 0.859$$

For definite integrals, change the limits to the new variable (here they happened to stay 0 and 1).

## Integration by parts

The product rule in reverse:

$$
\int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du
$$

Choose $u$ to be the factor that gets simpler when differentiated (a power of $t$, a logarithm) and $dv$ the factor you can integrate (an exponential, a sinusoid).

> $$\int te^{-t}\,dt \quad (u = t,\ dv = e^{-t}dt) \quad = -te^{-t} + \int e^{-t}\,dt = -(t + 1)e^{-t} + C$$
> So $\int_0^\infty te^{-t}\,dt = 1$ (the limit at infinity is zero; Lesson 2.4).

Integrals like $\int e^{-at}\cos\omega t\,dt$ need parts twice, after which the original integral reappears and can be solved for algebraically. That's exactly the Laplace transform of a cosine.

## Partial fractions

A ratio of polynomials can be split into simple fractions, each easy to integrate:

$$
\frac{1}{s(s + 1)} = \frac As + \frac{B}{s + 1}
$$

**Cover-up method:** to find $A$, cover the $s$ factor and set $s = 0$ in what remains: $A = 1/(0 + 1) = 1$. For $B$, cover $(s + 1)$ and set $s = -1$: $B = 1/(-1) = -1$. So

$$
\frac{1}{s(s + 1)} = \frac1s - \frac{1}{s + 1}, \qquad \int\frac{dx}{x(x + 1)} = \ln\left|\frac{x}{x + 1}\right| + C
$$

This is the key step in inverting Laplace transforms (Signals & Systems, Lesson 3.2): each simple fraction corresponds to one exponential mode of the circuit's response.

## Key takeaways

- Substitution reverses the chain rule; by parts ($\int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du$) reverses the product rule.
- Partial fractions split rational functions; the cover-up method finds the coefficients quickly.
- These steps are the machinery of Laplace-transform analysis.
