## Adding up over areas and volumes

To find the total charge in a region with charge density $\rho(x, y, z)$, the mass of a part, the current through a conductor's cross-section, or the power over a surface, integrate over two or three dimensions.

## Double integrals

Over a rectangle, a double integral is just two single integrals in turn (**iterated integrals**):

$$
\iint_R f(x, y)\,dA = \int_a^b\left(\int_c^d f(x, y)\,dy\right)dx
$$

> $\displaystyle\int_0^1\int_0^2 xy\,dy\,dx = \int_0^1 x\left[\frac{y^2}{2}\right]_0^2 dx = \int_0^1 2x\,dx = 1$

For non-rectangular regions, the inner limits depend on the outer variable: for the triangle under $y = x$ from 0 to 1, $\int_0^1\int_0^x f\,dy\,dx$.

## Polar, cylindrical and spherical coordinates

Choose coordinates that match the shape. The **area or volume element** picks up a scale factor:

| Coordinates | Element |
|---|---|
| Polar | $dA = r\,dr\,d\theta$ |
| Cylindrical | $dV = r\,dr\,d\phi\,dz$ |
| Spherical | $dV = r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi$ |

The extra $r$ (or $r^2\sin\theta$) is because an outer ring of a given thickness has more area than an inner one.

> **Area of a disk** of radius $a$: $\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^a r\,dr\,d\theta = 2\pi\cdot\frac{a^2}{2} = \pi a^2$.

> **Volume of a sphere:** $\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^\pi\int_0^a r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi = 2\pi \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{a^3}{3} = \frac43\pi a^3$.

## Engineering examples

> **Charge in a sphere** with density $\rho = \rho_0\dfrac ra$ (growing outward), radius $a$:
> $$Q = \int_0^a\rho_0\frac ra\,4\pi r^2\,dr = \frac{4\pi\rho_0}{a}\cdot\frac{a^4}{4} = \pi\rho_0a^3$$
> (The angular integrals give $4\pi$ because $\rho$ depends only on $r$.)

> **Current through a wire** with current density $J(r) = J_0(1 - r^2/a^2)$ (peaked at the center): $I = \int_0^a J\,2\pi r\,dr = \tfrac12\pi a^2J_0$: half what a uniform $J_0$ would carry.

These integrals appear constantly in Electromagnetics: total charge, flux and stored energy.

## Key takeaways

- Double and triple integrals are iterated single integrals; limits can depend on outer variables.
- Use polar ($r\,dr\,d\theta$), cylindrical ($r\,dr\,d\phi\,dz$) or spherical ($r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi$) coordinates to match symmetry.
- Total charge, current and energy are integrals of densities over regions.
