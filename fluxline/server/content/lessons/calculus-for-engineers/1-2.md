## Building derivatives from pieces

The basic derivatives of Lesson 1.1 ($t^n$, $e^t$, $\sin t$, $\cos t$, $\ln t$) combine through a handful of rules to differentiate almost any formula an engineer writes down.

| Rule | Formula |
|---|---|
| Constant multiple and sum | $(af + bg)' = af' + bg'$ |
| **Product** | $(fg)' = f'g + fg'$ |
| **Quotient** | $\left(\dfrac fg\right)' = \dfrac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}$ |
| **Chain** | $\dfrac{d}{dt}f\big(g(t)\big) = f'\big(g(t)\big)\,g'(t)$ |

## The product rule

> **Example.** A damped signal $v(t) = t^2e^{-t}$:
> $$v'(t) = 2te^{-t} + t^2(-e^{-t}) = (2t - t^2)e^{-t}$$
> At $t = 1$: $v'(1) = e^{-1} = 0.368$. The derivative is zero at $t = 2$, where the signal peaks.

Power in a component is a product, $p = vi$, so its rate of change is $\dfrac{dp}{dt} = \dfrac{dv}{dt}i + v\dfrac{di}{dt}$.

## The quotient rule

> $$\frac{d}{dx}\frac{x}{x^2 + 1} = \frac{(1)(x^2 + 1) - x(2x)}{(x^2 + 1)^2} = \frac{1 - x^2}{(x^2 + 1)^2}$$
> At $x = 2$: $-3/25 = -0.12$.

## The chain rule

The chain rule handles functions of functions, the most common situation of all:

> $$\frac{d}{dt}\sin(\omega t + \phi) = \omega\cos(\omega t + \phi), \qquad \frac{d}{dt}e^{-t/\tau} = -\frac1\tau e^{-t/\tau}, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\sqrt{1 + x^2} = \frac{x}{\sqrt{1 + x^2}}$$

The factor $\omega$ in the first one is why an inductor's voltage $L\,di/dt$ grows with frequency: differentiating a sinusoid multiplies its amplitude by $\omega$ and shifts it by 90°. That fact becomes inductive reactance $X_L = \omega L$ in Circuit Analysis II.

## Implicit differentiation

When $x$ and $y$ are related by an equation rather than a formula, differentiate both sides with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as a function of $x$:

> $x^2 + y^2 = 25$: $\;2x + 2y\,\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac xy$. At $(3, 4)$ the slope of the circle is $-3/4$.

**Related rates** use the same idea with time: if two quantities are linked, their rates of change are linked. For a resistor with $p = v^2/R$, $\dfrac{dp}{dt} = \dfrac{2v}{R}\dfrac{dv}{dt}$.

## Practice

```quiz
? $f(x) = x^3\ln x$. What is $f'(e)$? ($e \approx 2.718$)
= 29.56 | 0.005 |
> $f' = 3x^2\ln x + x^2$; at $x = e$: $3e^2 + e^2 = 4e^2$.
```

```quiz
? $g(x) = (3x + 1)^5$. What is $g'(0)$?
= 15 | 0.001 |
> Chain rule: $5(3x + 1)^4 \cdot 3$.
```

```quiz
? $h(t) = \sqrt{1 + t^2}$. What is $h'(\sqrt3)$?
= 0.866 | 0.002 |
> $t/\sqrt{1 + t^2} = \sqrt3/2$.
```

```quiz
? On the curve $xy = 6$, what is $dy/dx$ at $(2, 3)$?
= -1.5 | 0.001 |
> $y + x\,y' = 0$, so $y' = -y/x$.
```

```quiz
? $v(t) = 10\sin(100t)$ V across a 50 Ω resistor. What is $dp/dt$ at $t = 0$, in W/s? ($p = v^2/R$)
= 0 | 0 |
> $dp/dt = (2v/R)\,dv/dt$, and $v(0) = 0$.
```

```quiz
? What is $\dfrac{d}{dt}\cos(5t)$?
+ $-5\sin(5t)$
- $5\sin(5t)$
- $-\sin(5t)$
- $-5\cos(5t)$
> Chain rule: the inner derivative is 5.
```

## Key takeaways

- Product $(fg)' = f'g + fg'$; quotient $(f/g)' = (f'g - fg')/g^2$; chain $f(g)' = f'(g)\,g'$.
- Differentiating $\sin\omega t$ multiplies by $\omega$ and shifts 90°: the origin of reactance.
- Implicit differentiation and related rates handle quantities linked by equations.
