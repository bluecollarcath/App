## Finding the best

Engineering is full of "best" questions: the load that draws the most power, the dimensions that use the least material, the operating point with the highest efficiency. Calculus answers them: at a smooth maximum or minimum, the slope is zero.

## Critical points

To optimize $f(x)$:

1. Solve $f'(x) = 0$ for the **critical points**.
2. Classify them with the **second derivative**: $f'' < 0$ means a local maximum (curving down), $f'' > 0$ a local minimum.
3. Check the **endpoints** of the allowed range too: the best value can be at a boundary.

## Maximum power transfer

A source with internal resistance $R_s$ drives a load $R_L$. The load power is

$$
P(R_L) = \frac{V^2R_L}{(R_s + R_L)^2}
$$

Setting $dP/dR_L = 0$ (quotient rule):

$$
\frac{dP}{dR_L} = V^2\,\frac{(R_s + R_L)^2 - 2R_L(R_s + R_L)}{(R_s + R_L)^4} = 0 \ \Rightarrow\ R_L = R_s
$$

and the maximum power is $P_{max} = \dfrac{V^2}{4R_s}$.

> A 12 V source with 6 Ω internal resistance delivers at most $144/24 = 6$ W, into a 6 Ω load. (Circuit Analysis I, Lesson 3.3, uses this result; note that efficiency is then only 50%.)


## Peak of a pulse

> The response $f(t) = te^{-t/\tau}$ (a critically damped system, or a "bump" signal): $f'(t) = e^{-t/\tau}(1 - t/\tau) = 0$ at $t = \tau$, where $f = \tau/e$.

## A design example

> **Least material.** A cylindrical can must hold 1 liter (1000 cm³). Minimize its surface area $A = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh$.
>
> The volume constraint gives $h = 1000/(\pi r^2)$, so $A(r) = 2\pi r^2 + 2000/r$.
>
> $A'(r) = 4\pi r - 2000/r^2 = 0 \Rightarrow r = (500/\pi)^{1/3} = 5.42$ cm, and $h = 10.8$ cm $= 2r$. The optimal can is as tall as it is wide.

## Constraints and Lagrange multipliers

With several variables and a constraint $g(x, y) = 0$, the optimum occurs where the gradients (Lesson 4.1) are parallel: $\nabla f = \lambda\nabla g$. This **Lagrange multiplier** method underlies economic dispatch in power systems (sharing load among generators at minimum cost) and many design optimizations.

## Key takeaways

- Optimize by solving $f' = 0$, classify with $f''$, and check endpoints.
- Maximum power transfer: $R_L = R_s$, $P_{max} = V^2/4R_s$ (at 50% efficiency).
- Use constraints to eliminate variables, or Lagrange multipliers ($\nabla f = \lambda\nabla g$) for several variables.
