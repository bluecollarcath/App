## Adding up continuous change

Differentiation finds rates; **integration** accumulates them. If current is the rate of flow of charge, the total charge delivered is the accumulated current. If power is the rate of energy use, energy is accumulated power.

## Riemann sums

Divide the interval $[a, b]$ into $n$ slices of width $\Delta t$, multiply the function's value in each slice by $\Delta t$, and add. As the slices get thinner, the sum approaches the **definite integral**:

$$
\int_a^b f(t)\,dt = \lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^n f(t_k)\,\Delta t
$$

Geometrically it's the **signed area** under the curve (area below the axis counts as negative). Numerically, that's exactly how computers integrate measured data, with the trapezoid or Simpson's rule.

## The fundamental theorem of calculus

If $F'(t) = f(t)$ ($F$ is an **antiderivative** of $f$), then

$$
\int_a^b f(t)\,dt = F(b) - F(a)
$$

Integration and differentiation are inverse operations. Common antiderivatives:

| $f(t)$ | $\int f\,dt$ |
|---|---|
| $t^n$ ($n \ne -1$) | $\dfrac{t^{n+1}}{n + 1}$ |
| $1/t$ | $\ln\lvert t\rvert$ |
| $e^{at}$ | $\dfrac1ae^{at}$ |
| $\sin\omega t$ | $-\dfrac1\omega\cos\omega t$ |
| $\cos\omega t$ | $\dfrac1\omega\sin\omega t$ |

(each plus a constant of integration $C$ for indefinite integrals).

## Integrals in circuits

$$
q = \int i\,dt, \qquad v_C(t) = v_C(0) + \frac1C\int_0^ti\,d\tau, \qquad i_L(t) = i_L(0) + \frac1L\int_0^tv\,d\tau, \qquad W = \int p\,dt
$$

> **Charge.** A current $i(t) = 3t^2$ A flows for 2 s: $q = \int_0^2 3t^2\,dt = t^3\big|_0^2 = 8$ C.

> **Capacitor voltage.** A constant 1 mA charges a 2 µF capacitor (initially uncharged) for 4 ms: $v = (1/C)(It) = (10^{-3})(4\times10^{-3})/(2\times10^{-6}) = 2$ V. Constant current gives a linear ramp, which is how oscillators make triangle waves and how integrating ADCs measure voltage.

> **Energy.** A device's power ramps as $p = 4t$ W for 3 s: $W = \int_0^3 4t\,dt = 2t^2\big|_0^3 = 18$ J.

## Key takeaways

- The definite integral is the limit of Riemann sums: the signed area under the curve.
- Fundamental theorem: $\int_a^bf\,dt = F(b) - F(a)$ for any antiderivative $F$.
- Charge, capacitor voltage, inductor current and energy are integrals of current, voltage and power.
