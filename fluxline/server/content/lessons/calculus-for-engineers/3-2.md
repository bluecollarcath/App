## Approximating functions with polynomials

Near a point $a$, a smooth function can be approximated by a polynomial that matches its value and its derivatives there. The **Taylor series**:

$$
f(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x - a) + \frac{f''(a)}{2!}(x - a)^2 + \frac{f'''(a)}{3!}(x - a)^3 + \cdots
$$

Around $a = 0$ it's called a **Maclaurin series**. The ones to know:

| Function | Series | Valid for |
|---|---|---|
| $e^x$ | $1 + x + \dfrac{x^2}{2!} + \dfrac{x^3}{3!} + \cdots$ | all $x$ |
| $\sin x$ | $x - \dfrac{x^3}{3!} + \dfrac{x^5}{5!} - \cdots$ | all $x$ |
| $\cos x$ | $1 - \dfrac{x^2}{2!} + \dfrac{x^4}{4!} - \cdots$ | all $x$ |
| $\ln(1 + x)$ | $x - \dfrac{x^2}{2} + \dfrac{x^3}{3} - \cdots$ | $-1 < x \le 1$ |
| $(1 + x)^k$ | $1 + kx + \dfrac{k(k - 1)}{2}x^2 + \cdots$ | $\lvert x\rvert < 1$ |

## Linearization: the engineer's favorite approximation

Keeping just the first two terms gives the **tangent-line approximation**:

$$
f(x) \approx f(a) + f'(a)(x - a)
$$

This is the mathematical basis of **small-signal analysis** (Microelectronics I, Lesson 2.3). A diode's current $i = I_Se^{v/V_T}$ is wildly nonlinear, but for small variations $\Delta v$ around a bias point, $\Delta i \approx \dfrac{di}{dv}\Delta v = \dfrac{I}{V_T}\Delta v$. The derivative becomes a conductance, $g = I/V_T$, and a nonlinear circuit becomes a linear one we can analyze with all of circuit theory. The same idea linearizes nonlinear control systems around an operating point and power systems around their steady state.

Standard small-value approximations: $\sin\theta \approx \theta$, $\cos\theta \approx 1 - \theta^2/2$, $e^x \approx 1 + x$, $(1 + x)^k \approx 1 + kx$, $\sqrt{1 + x} \approx 1 + x/2$.

> $\sqrt{4.1} = 2\sqrt{1.025} \approx 2(1 + 0.0125) = 2.025$ (true value 2.02485).

## How good is it?

The error after truncating at the degree-$n$ term is bounded by the next term (**Taylor's remainder**):

$$
|R_n(x)| \le \frac{M\,|x - a|^{n+1}}{(n + 1)!}, \qquad M = \max|f^{(n+1)}|
$$

> $\sin(0.1) \approx 0.1$ has error at most $0.1^3/6 = 1.7\times10^{-4}$; adding the cubic term, $0.1 - 0.000167 = 0.099833$, the error drops below $10^{-7}$. Small angles make the approximation excellent; the pendulum and small-angle optics rely on it.

## Key takeaways

- Taylor series match a function's derivatives at a point; know $e^x$, $\sin$, $\cos$, $\ln(1 + x)$, $(1 + x)^k$.
- Linearization $f(a) + f'(a)(x - a)$ is the basis of small-signal analysis.
- The truncation error is bounded by the next term.
