## The function that describes every transient

Charge a capacitor, energize an inductor, cool a heatsink, discharge a battery, fill a buffer: the quantity approaches its final value **exponentially**. The exponential function $e^x$ (with $e = 2.71828\ldots$) is special because it is its own derivative:

$$
\frac{d}{dx}e^x = e^x, \qquad \frac{d}{dt}e^{at} = ae^{at}
$$

So any quantity whose rate of change is proportional to the quantity itself, $\dfrac{dy}{dt} = ay$, is exponential: $y = y_0e^{at}$. That's the defining equation of a first-order system (Differential Equations, Lesson 1.2).

## Logarithms undo exponentials

The natural logarithm is the inverse: $\ln(e^x) = x$. Its rules turn products into sums, which is why engineers measure gain in decibels:

$$
\ln(ab) = \ln a + \ln b, \qquad \ln(a^n) = n\ln a, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\ln x = \frac1x, \qquad \log_{10}x = \frac{\ln x}{\ln10}
$$

## Time constants

A first-order transient has the form

$$
\text{decay: } x(t) = X_0e^{-t/\tau}, \qquad \text{rise: } x(t) = X_f\big(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\big)
$$

The **time constant** $\tau$ ($RC$ for a capacitor, $L/R$ for an inductor) sets the speed.

| Time | Decay remaining | Rise completed |
|---|---|---|
| $\tau$ | 36.8% | 63.2% |
| $2\tau$ | 13.5% | 86.5% |
| $3\tau$ | 5.0% | 95.0% |
| $5\tau$ | 0.7% | 99.3% |

"Five time constants" is the engineering definition of "finished."

```plot

caption: The two shapes of every first-order transient. After one time constant a rise is 63% complete; after five it's essentially finished.
```

## Solving for time with logarithms

To find **when** a transient reaches a value, isolate the exponential and take the logarithm.

> **Example.** An RC circuit with $\tau = 2$ ms charges toward 5 V. When does it reach 99% (4.95 V)?
> $$1 - e^{-t/\tau} = 0.99 \ \Rightarrow\ e^{-t/\tau} = 0.01 \ \Rightarrow\ t = \tau\ln100 = 4.61\tau = 9.21\ \text{ms}$$

Useful special cases: reaching 50% takes $\tau\ln2 = 0.693\tau$ (the **half-life**), and 90% takes $\tau\ln10 = 2.30\tau$. The 10%–90% **rise time** is $\tau(\ln10 - \ln(10/9)) = 2.2\tau$.

## Key takeaways

- $\dfrac{d}{dt}e^{at} = ae^{at}$: anything changing in proportion to itself is exponential.
- Transients: $X_0e^{-t/\tau}$ and $X_f(1 - e^{-t/\tau})$; 63% at $\tau$, about done at $5\tau$.
- Solve for time with logs: 50% at $0.693\tau$, 90% at $2.30\tau$, 99% at $4.61\tau$; rise time $2.2\tau$.
