## Adding infinitely many terms

A **sequence** is an ordered list $a_0, a_1, a_2, \ldots$; a **series** is the sum of its terms. Infinite series appear whenever a process repeats: echoes in a room, reflections bouncing on a transmission line (Electromagnetics, Lesson 1.4), the samples of a digital filter's impulse response, the harmonics of a Fourier series.

A series $\sum a_n$ **converges** if its partial sums $S_N = \sum_{n=0}^N a_n$ approach a finite limit. A necessary (but not sufficient) condition is that the terms go to zero.

## The geometric series

The most important series by far:

$$
\sum_{n=0}^\infty ar^n = \frac{a}{1 - r} \quad (|r| < 1)
$$

It diverges for $|r| \ge 1$. The proof is one line: $S_N - rS_N = a - ar^{N+1}$, and $r^{N+1} \to 0$.

> **Bouncing reflections.** On a mismatched line, each round trip multiplies a wave by $\Gamma_L\Gamma_s$. With $\Gamma_L\Gamma_s = -1/6$, the total voltage at the load after all the bounces is the first-arrival value times $1/(1 + 1/6) = 6/7$: the DC steady state, exactly as circuit analysis predicts.

> **A digital filter's DC gain.** $y[n] = 0.9y[n-1] + 0.1x[n]$ has impulse response $h[n] = 0.1(0.9)^n$. Its DC gain is $\sum h[n] = 0.1/(1 - 0.9) = 1$ (DSP, Lesson 1.2).

## When the terms shrink but the sum still diverges

The **harmonic series** $1 + \tfrac12 + \tfrac13 + \tfrac14 + \cdots$ diverges, even though its terms go to zero: group the terms as $\tfrac12 + (\tfrac13 + \tfrac14) + (\tfrac15 + \cdots + \tfrac18) + \cdots$, and each group exceeds $\tfrac12$. By contrast $\sum1/n^2 = \pi^2/6$ converges (the same $p > 1$ rule as for integrals, Lesson 2.4).

## Convergence tests

- **Ratio test:** if $|a_{n+1}/a_n| \to L$, the series converges for $L < 1$ and diverges for $L > 1$ (inconclusive at 1).
- **Comparison:** a series smaller than a convergent one converges.
- **Alternating series:** terms of alternating sign that shrink to zero converge, and the error after stopping is less than the first omitted term.

## Power series

A **power series** $\sum c_nx^n$ is an "infinite polynomial." It converges for $|x| < R$, where the **radius of convergence** comes from the ratio test:

$$
R = \lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{c_n}{c_{n+1}}\right|
$$

> $\sum x^n/2^n$ is geometric with $r = x/2$: it converges for $|x| < 2$, to $\dfrac{1}{1 - x/2}$.

Power series let us compute functions like $e^x$ and $\sin x$ with only multiplication and addition (next lesson), which is exactly what calculators and DSP chips do.

## Key takeaways

- Geometric series: $\sum ar^n = a/(1 - r)$ for $|r| < 1$; it models echoes, reflections and IIR filters.
- Terms → 0 is necessary, not sufficient: the harmonic series diverges.
- Ratio test and comparison decide convergence; power series converge within a radius $R$.
