## Functions of several variables

Most physical quantities depend on more than one variable: the potential $V(x, y, z)$ in space, a resistor's power $P(V, R)$, a transistor's current $I_D(V_{GS}, V_{DS})$. A **partial derivative** measures the rate of change with respect to one variable while holding the others fixed:

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = \lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x + h, y) - f(x, y)}{h}
$$

To compute it, treat the other variables as constants.

> $f(x, y) = x^2y + \sin y$: $\;\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 2xy$, $\;\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y} = x^2 + \cos y$.

In electronics, partial derivatives are small-signal parameters: $g_m = \partial I_D/\partial V_{GS}$ (at constant $V_{DS}$) and $1/r_o = \partial I_D/\partial V_{DS}$ (at constant $V_{GS}$).

## The gradient

Collect the partial derivatives into a vector, the **gradient**:

$$
\nabla f = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\hat{\mathbf x} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\hat{\mathbf y} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\hat{\mathbf z}
$$

It points in the direction of **steepest increase**, its magnitude is that steepest rate, and it's perpendicular to the level curves (contours) of $f$. The electric field is the downhill gradient of the potential, $\mathbf E = -\nabla V$, which is why field lines cross equipotentials at right angles.

**Directional derivative:** the rate of change in the direction of a unit vector $\hat{\mathbf u}$ is $\nabla f \cdot \hat{\mathbf u}$.

> $V = x^2y$ volts. At $(1, 2)$: $\nabla V = (2xy, x^2) = (4, 1)$ V/m. In the direction $(3, 4)/5$: $(4 \times 3 + 1 \times 4)/5 = 3.2$ V/m. The steepest rate is $|\nabla V| = \sqrt{17} = 4.12$ V/m.

## The multivariable chain rule and sensitivity

If $f$ depends on $x$ and $y$, a small change in each gives

$$
df = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}dx + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}dy
$$

This is the basis of **tolerance and sensitivity analysis**. For a product or quotient of powers, the relative errors combine simply:

> $P = V^2/R$: $\dfrac{dP}{P} = 2\dfrac{dV}{V} - \dfrac{dR}{R}$. If $V$ is uncertain by 1% and $R$ by 2%, the worst case is $2(1\%) + 2\% = 4\%$. For independent random errors, add in quadrature: $\sqrt{2^2 + 2^2} = 2.8\%$.

## Key takeaways

- Partial derivatives hold the other variables fixed; they're small-signal parameters like $g_m$ and $1/r_o$.
- The gradient points uphill, perpendicular to contours; $\mathbf E = -\nabla V$; directional derivative $= \nabla f\cdot\hat{\mathbf u}$.
- $df = \sum(\partial f/\partial x_i)dx_i$ gives sensitivity and tolerance analysis.
