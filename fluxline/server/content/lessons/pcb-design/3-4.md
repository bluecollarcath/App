## Switching regulators live or die by layout

A switching regulator (Power Electronics, Unit 1) chops current at hundreds of kHz to several MHz with edges of a few nanoseconds. Its schematic can be perfect and the board still fail: unstable, noisy, or radiating far beyond EMC limits. The fix is almost entirely in placement and copper.

```circuit
caption: In a buck converter the current in the loop formed by $C_{IN}$, the switch Q and the diode D (or low-side switch) changes abruptly every cycle. This **hot loop** must be as physically small as possible. The inductor and output side carry smooth current and are far less critical.
```

The rules, which most regulator datasheets repeat in their layout section:

1. **Minimize the hot loop.** Place $C_{IN}$ (a small ceramic, right at the IC's VIN and GND pins), the switch and the diode or low-side switch tightly together on the same layer, with a solid ground plane directly underneath. The loop's inductance, multiplied by $di/dt$, is what produces the voltage spikes and ringing.
2. **Keep the switch node small.** The node between Q, D and L swings the full input voltage in nanoseconds. Make its copper just big enough for the current; a large switch-node area is a large $dv/dt$ antenna that couples capacitively into neighbors.
3. **Route feedback carefully.** The feedback divider sits close to the IC's FB pin, the trace from the output runs away from the inductor and switch node, and it senses the voltage at the point of load if accuracy matters.
4. **Separate power and signal grounds at the IC** where the datasheet calls for it (PGND and AGND), joined at one point, usually the exposed pad.
5. **Keep sensitive circuits away:** ADCs, crystals, RF and audio stay well clear of the inductor and switch node. Shielded inductors help.

## Thermal design on the board

On small boards, the PCB **is** the heat sink. Surface-mount power parts (regulators, MOSFETs, LED drivers) dump heat into copper through their leads or exposed pad.

- Connect **exposed thermal pads** with a grid of **thermal vias** to large copper areas on inner and bottom layers.
- A single via's thermal resistance is set by its plated wall: for a 0.3 mm drill with 25 µm plating through 1.6 mm of board,

$$
\theta_{via} = \frac{\ell}{k\,A} \approx \frac{1.6\times10^{-3}}{390 \times (\pi \times 0.3\times10^{-3} \times 25\times10^{-6})} \approx 174\ \text{°C/W}
$$

- Vias act in parallel, so **nine** of them give about 19 °C/W. Filled or plugged vias conduct better and keep solder from wicking away from the pad.
- Copper area matters: a regulator's $\theta_{JA}$ can drop from 150 °C/W on minimal copper to 40 °C/W on a few square centimeters of 2 oz pour.
- Use **thermal relief** spokes on through-hole pads connected to planes, so they can be soldered; but use **solid connections** (no relief) where heat transfer is the goal.

## EMC: designing to pass

Every product sold must meet **electromagnetic compatibility** regulations: limits on **emissions** (FCC Part 15, CISPR 32) and minimum **immunity** to ESD, radiated fields and fast transients (IEC 61000-4-x). Failing a test late is expensive, so design for it:

- **Small loops everywhere:** solid planes, decoupling at every pin, compact hot loops. Loop area times $di/dt$ is what radiates.
- **Slow the edges** you don't need fast: series resistors (22–100 Ω) on clocks and data lines, and the MCU's slew-rate settings on GPIOs.
- **Cables are antennas.** Most radiated emission comes from common-mode current on cables. Filter every signal that leaves the board at the connector (RC filters, ferrite beads, common-mode chokes), and keep fast circuits away from connectors.
- **Stitch the edges:** a row of ground vias around the board perimeter, and avoid routing fast signals along the edge.
- **Protect connectors from ESD:** TVS diodes placed right at the connector, with a short, wide path to ground, before the signal reaches anything sensitive.
- Keep **clock traces short**, on inner layers between planes where possible, and use spread-spectrum clocking if the chip supports it.

## Key takeaways

- Switching regulators: tiny hot loop with $C_{IN}$ at the pins, small switch node, quiet feedback routing, solid ground beneath.
- The PCB is the heat sink: thermal vias in parallel (≈ 174 °C/W each), large copper pours, exposed pads soldered down.
- Pass EMC by design: small loops, slowed edges, filtered connectors, stitched edges, ESD protection at the connector.
