## Placement decides the outcome

Experienced designers say that a board's performance is set by **placement** more than by routing: good placement makes routing short and obvious, while poor placement forces long, tangled traces that no amount of routing skill can fix. Spend the time here.

## The order of placement

1. **Mechanical constraints first:** board outline, mounting holes, connectors, switches, LEDs, displays and anything that must line up with the enclosure. These positions usually aren't negotiable.
2. **Critical components:**
   - **power stages** (switching regulators with their inductor and capacitors, Lesson 3.4);
   - **crystals and oscillators**, right next to the pins they drive;
   - **decoupling capacitors**, right at each power pin;
   - **high-speed** interfaces (USB, Ethernet, memory) close to their connectors or partners;
   - **sensitive analog** circuits (ADC inputs, references, sensor front ends).
3. **Everything else**, grouped by function.

## Grouping and flow

- **Group each functional block** together: the regulator and its parts, the MCU and its crystal and decoupling, the motor driver and its sense resistor. A schematic drawn by block makes this easy.
- **Follow the signal flow** in a line from input to output, so traces don't double back.
- **Separate noisy from sensitive.** Keep switching regulators, motor drivers and clocks physically away from analog inputs and RF sections. This is **partitioning by placement** on a common ground plane, which works far better than splitting the ground (Lesson 3.2).
- **Keep critical loops small:** a decoupling capacitor's loop (power pin → capacitor → ground pin) and a regulator's switching loop must enclose as little area as possible.

> **Crystal placement.** A microcontroller's 8 MHz crystal and its two load capacitors go within a few millimeters of the oscillator pins, with short, direct traces and solid ground beneath, and no other signals routed between them. Long crystal traces pick up noise, add capacitance and can stop the oscillator from starting.

## Thermal considerations

- Spread hot parts (regulators, power transistors, high-current resistors) apart, and give them copper area and thermal vias (Lesson 3.4).
- Keep temperature-sensitive parts (references, crystals, electrolytic capacitors, sensors) away from heat sources.
- Consider airflow in the enclosure.

## Design for manufacture and assembly

- **One-sided SMT** assembly if at all possible: every extra side or through-hole part adds a process step.
- Keep consistent **orientation** for polarized parts (all diodes the same way, all ICs with pin 1 in the same direction): it speeds assembly and inspection.
- Respect component **courtyards** (keep-out spacing around each part) so they can be placed and reworked.
- Keep parts several millimeters from the **board edge** and from V-score lines.
- Add **fiducials** (copper reference marks) for pick-and-place machine alignment, and **test points** on power rails and key signals for bring-up and production test.
- Check **height** limits and connector clearance against the enclosure, ideally in 3-D (KiCad and most tools export STEP models).

## Practice

```quiz
? Which components should be placed first?
+ connectors, mounting holes and parts fixed by the enclosure
- resistors
- decoupling capacitors far from ICs
- test points
> Mechanical constraints aren't negotiable.
```

```quiz
? Where should a microcontroller's crystal be placed?
+ within a few millimeters of the oscillator pins, with short traces and ground beneath
- near the board edge for cooling
- next to the switching regulator
- anywhere, since crystals are slow
> Long crystal traces pick up noise and add capacitance.
```

```quiz
? The best way to keep switching noise out of an ADC input is usually:
+ place the switching circuit far from the analog section on a common, unbroken ground plane
- split the ground plane into two islands joined by a long trace
- route the ADC trace under the inductor
- remove the decoupling capacitors
> Partition by placement; keep the ground plane solid.
```

```quiz
? Why place all polarized components in the same orientation?
+ It speeds assembly and makes visual inspection easier
- It improves electrical performance
- It's required by the Gerber format
- It reduces the number of layers
> Consistency reduces assembly errors.
```

```quiz
? What are fiducials for?
+ alignment references for pick-and-place machines and inspection cameras
- mounting screws
- test probes
- heat sinking
> Machines locate the board precisely from them.
```

## Key takeaways

- Placement sets performance: mechanical parts first, then critical parts, then the rest.
- Group by function, follow signal flow, separate noisy from sensitive, keep critical loops small.
- Place crystals and decoupling capacitors right at their pins.
- Design for assembly: one-sided SMT, consistent orientation, courtyards, edge clearance, fiducials, test points.
