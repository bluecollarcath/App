## The design rule check

Before a board goes to fabrication, the **design rule check** (DRC) compares every piece of copper against the rules you loaded (Lesson 3.1):

- trace width and spacing below the minimum;
- **unrouted nets** (ratsnest lines still present): the most embarrassing error of all;
- **annular ring** too small: the copper ring left around a drilled hole, $(\text{pad} - \text{drill})/2$;
- copper too close to the board edge, holes, or other copper;
- overlapping courtyards, silkscreen on pads, missing solder-mask openings;
- **zone fill** out of date: always refill pours before the final DRC.

A clean DRC means the board is **manufacturable as drawn**; it doesn't mean the circuit is right. ERC (Lesson 2.2) and design review catch those.

## Design review checklist

Before ordering, walk through the design with fresh eyes, ideally someone else's:

- **Footprints vs. datasheets:** pin 1, pinouts of every SOT-23 and connector, polarity of diodes, LEDs and electrolytics.
- **Power:** every rail's regulator, capacitors, current capacity and thermal path; the power-on sequence.
- **Connectors:** pinout matches the mating cable or board (draw it from the mating side!).
- **Mechanical:** outline, hole positions and part heights against the enclosure (3-D check).
- **Bring-up aids:** test points, a debug/programming header, LEDs on power rails, jumpers or 0 Ω resistors to isolate sections.
- **Silkscreen:** reference designators readable, polarity marks, board name, revision and date.

## Manufacturing files

The fabricator receives a package of files, not your EDA project.

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Gerber** (RS-274X or X2) | one file per layer: each copper layer, solder mask top/bottom, silkscreen top/bottom, paste top/bottom, board outline |
| **Excellon drill** | hole positions and sizes; plated and non-plated holes often separate |
| **Fab drawing** | stackup, material, thickness, finish, color, impedance requirements, tolerances |
| **IPC-2581** or **ODB++** | (alternative) a single intelligent file containing all layers, drills, netlist and stackup |
| **IPC-356 netlist** | lets the fab's electrical test verify the copper matches your connectivity |

A four-layer board needs 4 copper + 2 mask + 2 silkscreen + 2 paste + 1 outline = **11** Gerber files, plus drill files. The paste layers aren't used by the fabricator itself; they make the **solder stencil**.

**Always open your Gerbers in a viewer** (KiCad's GerbView, or the fabricator's online viewer) before ordering. It's the last chance to see exactly what will be made: a missing outline, a mirrored layer, or a plane that didn't fill all show up here.

## Assembly files

If a contract manufacturer will assemble the board, they need:

- **BOM** (bill of materials): reference designators, quantity, value, MPN, and DNP flags (Lesson 2.2);
- **Pick-and-place / centroid file:** X, Y, rotation and side for each part;
- **Assembly drawing:** where each part goes, polarity, special instructions;
- **Paste Gerbers** for the stencil.

The most common assembly problem is a **rotation mismatch** between your library's zero orientation and the assembler's. Reputable assemblers review it and ask; check their preview images carefully.

## Panelization and ordering

Small boards are built in **panels**: several copies joined by **V-score** grooves or **tab routing** with breakaway "mouse bites," with rails for the assembly machine and fiducials. Your fab or assembler can panelize for you.

For a prototype order, specify: quantity, layers, thickness (1.6 mm), copper weight, finish (HASL or ENIG), solder-mask color, and whether you need controlled impedance or electrical test. Low-cost prototype fabs turn 2- and 4-layer boards around in days.

## Practice

```quiz
? A via has a 0.6 mm pad and a 0.3 mm drill. What is its annular ring, in mm?
= 0.15 | 0.001 | mm
> $(0.6 - 0.3)/2$.
```

```quiz
? How many Gerber files does a two-layer board with paste, mask, silkscreen on both sides and an outline need?
= 9 | 0.001 |
> 2 copper + 2 mask + 2 silk + 2 paste + 1 outline.
```

```quiz
? A board passes DRC with no errors. What does that guarantee?
+ the layout meets the manufacturing rules as drawn
- the circuit works correctly
- every footprint matches its datasheet
- the board will pass EMC testing
> DRC checks geometry, not design intent.
```

```quiz
? Which file tells the assembler the position and rotation of every part?
+ the pick-and-place (centroid) file
- the Excellon drill file
- the Gerber silkscreen
- the IPC-356 netlist
> X, Y, rotation and side for each reference designator.
```

```quiz
? What are the solder paste layers used for?
+ making the stencil that prints solder paste onto the pads
- etching the copper
- printing the reference designators
- defining the board outline
> The fab doesn't use them; the stencil maker does.
```

```quiz
? Why should you view your Gerbers before ordering?
+ They are exactly what will be built, so errors like missing outlines or unfilled planes show up there
- The EDA tool cannot display the PCB
- Gerbers contain the schematic
- The fab requires a screenshot
> Gerbers are the real deliverable.
```

## Key takeaways

- Run DRC with fab rules and refilled zones; then do a human design review (footprints, power, connectors, mechanical, test access).
- Fabrication needs Gerbers (one per layer), drill files and a fab drawing, or a single IPC-2581/ODB++ file.
- Assembly needs a BOM with MPNs, a centroid file, an assembly drawing and paste layers; watch rotations.
- Always inspect the Gerbers in a viewer before you order.
