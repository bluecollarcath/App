## What a board is made of

A printed circuit board is a sandwich of **copper** layers separated by **insulating** layers, almost always **FR-4**: woven fiberglass cloth bonded with epoxy resin. FR-4 is cheap, strong and flame-retardant, with a relative permittivity of about 4.2–4.6. Its key limits are moderate dielectric loss at GHz frequencies (RF boards use specialized laminates such as Rogers materials) and its glass-transition temperature $T_g$ (130–180 °C), above which it softens.

- **Copper weight** is quoted in ounces per square foot: **1 oz = 35 µm** thick (the usual default), 2 oz = 70 µm for high-current boards. Inner layers are often 0.5 or 1 oz.
- **Standard thickness** is 1.6 mm (0.062 in), with 0.8, 1.0 and 2.0 mm common alternatives.

## Layer stackups

| Layers | Typical use |
|---|---|
| 1 (single-sided) | cheapest consumer products, power supplies |
| 2 | simple digital and analog boards, hobby projects |
| 4 | most microcontroller boards: two signal layers plus solid ground and power planes |
| 6–12 | dense digital, DDR memory, high-speed interfaces |
| 12–30+ | servers, networking, smartphones (with HDI microvias) |

A multilayer board is built from **cores** (rigid laminate with copper on both sides) and **prepreg** (uncured glass-epoxy sheets) pressed together under heat.

**Signal / ground / power / signal** is the classic 4-layer stackup. A common improvement is **signal / ground / ground / signal** (power routed as wide traces or pours on the signal layers), which gives both signal layers an adjacent ground.

## How a board is manufactured

1. **Inner layers:** photoresist is applied to copper-clad cores, exposed through the layer artwork, developed, and the unwanted copper **etched** away.
2. **Lamination:** cores and prepreg are stacked and pressed.
3. **Drilling:** holes for vias and through-hole parts.
4. **Plating:** the hole walls are plated with copper, connecting the layers.
5. **Outer layers:** imaged and etched like the inner ones.
6. **Solder mask:** the colored coating (green by tradition) that covers copper everywhere except the pads, preventing solder bridges and corrosion.
7. **Surface finish** on exposed pads: **HASL** (solder-coated, cheap, uneven), **ENIG** (nickel and gold, flat and durable, good for fine-pitch parts) or **OSP** (organic coating, cheap, short shelf life).
8. **Silkscreen:** reference designators, polarity marks, logos.
9. **Electrical test** and **routing** the board outline.

## Vias

| Via type | Connects | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Through-hole via** | all layers | cheapest; typical 0.3 mm drill, 0.6 mm pad |
| **Blind via** | an outer layer to an inner one | extra cost |
| **Buried via** | inner layers only | extra cost |
| **Microvia** | adjacent layers, laser-drilled (~0.1 mm) | HDI boards: phones, BGAs |

The **aspect ratio** (board thickness ÷ drill diameter) is limited to about 8–10:1, because plating can't reach the middle of deep, narrow holes: a 1.6 mm board allows drills down to about 0.2 mm. **Via-in-pad** (a via in an SMD pad, filled and capped) saves space under BGAs but costs more.

## Fab capabilities and cost

Typical low-cost limits: **0.15 mm (6 mil) trace width and spacing**, 0.3 mm minimum drill, 2 or 4 layers. Pushing below those, adding layers, special materials, controlled impedance, ENIG or blind vias all raise the price. Design within your fabricator's standard capabilities whenever possible: download their design rules and load them into your EDA tool before you start routing.

## Key takeaways

- Boards are copper and FR-4 layers; 1 oz copper = 35 µm; 1.6 mm is standard.
- 4-layer signal/ground/power/signal (or signal/ground/ground/signal) is the workhorse stackup.
- Manufacturing: image, etch, laminate, drill, plate, mask, finish, silkscreen, test.
- Vias: through, blind, buried, micro; aspect ratio limits drill size.
- Stay within the fabricator's standard rules to keep cost down.
