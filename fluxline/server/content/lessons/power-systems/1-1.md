## Why three phases?

Nearly all bulk electric power is generated, transmitted, and consumed as **balanced three-phase AC**. Compared with single-phase, three-phase:

- delivers **constant instantaneous power** (no 120 Hz pulsation, so motors run smoothly),
- uses about **25% less conductor** for the same power and losses,
- naturally produces a **rotating magnetic field**, which is what makes induction motors self-starting.

## Balanced sources

Three voltages of equal magnitude, displaced by 120°. For **positive (abc) sequence**:

$$
\mathbf{V}_{an} = V_p\angle0^\circ,\quad \mathbf{V}_{bn} = V_p\angle{-120^\circ},\quad \mathbf{V}_{cn} = V_p\angle{+120^\circ}
$$

They sum to zero, so in a balanced system the neutral carries no current.

## Wye (Y) connection

Line-to-line voltages are larger than phase (line-to-neutral) voltages and lead them by 30°:

$$
\mathbf{V}_{ab} = \sqrt3\,V_p\angle{30^\circ}, \qquad V_L = \sqrt3\,V_p, \qquad I_L = I_p
$$

## Delta (Δ) connection

Phase voltages equal line voltages; line currents are $\sqrt3$ times phase currents and lag by 30°:

$$
V_L = V_p, \qquad I_L = \sqrt3\,I_p
$$

A balanced Δ load $\mathbf{Z}_\Delta$ is equivalent to a Y load $\mathbf{Z}_Y = \mathbf{Z}_\Delta/3$.

## Per-phase analysis

Because the system is balanced, solve **one phase** of an equivalent Y–Y circuit (phase *a*, line-to-neutral), then get the other phases by ±120° shifts. This is how power engineers analyze systems with thousands of buses.

## Three-phase power

$$
\mathbf{S}_{3\phi} = 3\,\mathbf{V}_p\mathbf{I}_p^* 
\qquad
P_{3\phi} = \sqrt3\,V_L I_L\cos\theta
\qquad
Q_{3\phi} = \sqrt3\,V_L I_L\sin\theta
$$

where $\theta$ is the angle of the per-phase impedance (the power-factor angle), **not** the angle between line voltage and line current.

> **Worked example.** A 480 V (line-to-line) motor draws 100 A per line at 0.85 pf lagging.
>
> $|\mathbf S| = \sqrt3(480)(100) = 83.1$ kVA
> $P = 83.1 \times 0.85 = 70.7$ kW
> $Q = 83.1 \times \sin(\cos^{-1}0.85) = 83.1 \times 0.527 = 43.8$ kVAR

```quiz
= 7.967 | 0.01 | kV
> $13.8/\sqrt3 = 7.97$ kV.
```

```quiz
? A balanced Δ load has 30 Ω per phase. What is the equivalent Y impedance per phase, in Ω?
= 10 | 0.001 | Ω
> $\mathbf{Z}_Y = \mathbf{Z}_\Delta / 3$.
```

```quiz
? A 208 V three-phase heater (unity pf) draws 25 A per line. What is its power, in kW?
= 9.007 | 0.01 | kW
> $\sqrt3 \times 208 \times 25 = 9.01$ kW.
```

## Key takeaways

- Y: $V_L = \sqrt3V_p$; Δ: $I_L = \sqrt3I_p$; $Z_Y = Z_\Delta/3$.
- Solve balanced systems per-phase.
- $P = \sqrt3V_LI_L\cos\theta$, with $\theta$ the load impedance angle.
