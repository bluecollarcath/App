## Transforming randomness

Signals pass through circuits, detectors square voltages, and receivers add up samples. Each operation turns input random variables into new ones. The question: what is the distribution of the output?

## Linear functions

For $Y = aX + b$:

$$
E[Y] = aE[X] + b, \qquad \text{Var}(Y) = a^2\text{Var}(X), \qquad f_Y(y) = \frac{1}{|a|}f_X\!\left(\frac{y - b}{a}\right)
$$

A linear function of a Gaussian is Gaussian. An amplifier with gain 10 multiplies the noise standard deviation by 10 and its power by 100.

## General functions: the CDF method

For a monotonic $Y = g(X)$, work with the CDF and differentiate, or use the direct formula

$$
f_Y(y) = \frac{f_X(x)}{|g'(x)|}\bigg|_{x = g^{-1}(y)}
$$

(summing over all roots $x$ if $g$ isn't one-to-one). Even without the full distribution, expectations of functions are easy: $E[g(X)] = \int g(x)f_X(x)\,dx$.

> **Example: power of a uniform signal.** $X$ uniform on $[0, 1]$, $Y = X^2$. Then $E[Y] = \int_0^1x^2\,dx = 1/3$. By the CDF method, $P(Y \le y) = P(X \le \sqrt y) = \sqrt y$, so $f_Y(y) = 1/(2\sqrt y)$ on $(0, 1]$: small values are much more likely.

## Sums of independent variables: convolution

If $Z = X + Y$ with $X$ and $Y$ independent, the PDF of the sum is the **convolution** of the PDFs:

$$
f_Z(z) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_X(x)\,f_Y(z - x)\,dx
$$

That's the same convolution as in Signals & Systems.

> **Example.** The sum of two independent uniform $[0, 1]$ variables has a triangular PDF on $[0, 2]$, peaked at 1. Adding a third makes it look like a bell already (next lesson).

```plot
x: -0.2, 2.2, z
y: 0, 1.2, f_Z(z)
f: u(x) - u(x - 1) | f_X (uniform) | dash
f: x*(u(x) - u(x - 1)) + (2 - x)*(u(x - 1) - u(x - 2)) | f_Z = f_X * f_X
caption: Convolving the uniform PDF on $[0, 1]$ (dashed) with itself gives a triangle on $[0, 2]$.
```

Two important special cases:

- **Sum of independent Gaussians** is Gaussian, with means adding and **variances** adding: $\mathcal N(\mu_1, \sigma_1^2) + \mathcal N(\mu_2, \sigma_2^2) = \mathcal N(\mu_1 + \mu_2, \sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2)$.
- **Sum of independent Poissons** is Poisson, with the rates adding.

## Moment-generating functions

Convolution is awkward; transforms make it multiplication. The **moment-generating function** is

$$
M_X(t) = E\big[e^{tX}\big]
$$

For independent $X$ and $Y$, $M_{X+Y}(t) = M_X(t)M_Y(t)$, just as Laplace transforms turn convolution into products. Its derivatives at $t = 0$ give the **moments**: $E[X^n] = M_X^{(n)}(0)$.

| Distribution | $M_X(t)$ |
|---|---|
| Bernoulli($p$) | $1 - p + pe^t$ |
| Poisson($\lambda$) | $e^{\lambda(e^t - 1)}$ |
| Exponential($\lambda$) | $\dfrac{\lambda}{\lambda - t}$ ($t < \lambda$) |
| Gaussian($\mu, \sigma^2$) | $e^{\mu t + \sigma^2t^2/2}$ |

> Multiply two Gaussian MGFs: $e^{(\mu_1 + \mu_2)t + (\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2)t^2/2}$, again a Gaussian MGF. That proves the sum rule above in one line.

```quiz

= 5 | 0.001 |
> $3(1) + 2$.
```

```quiz

= 18 | 0.001 |

```


```quiz
? $X$ is uniform on $[0, 2]$. What is $E[X^2]$?
= 1.333 | 0.002 |
> $\int_0^2 x^2\,\tfrac12\,dx = 8/6 = 4/3$.
```

```quiz
> The merged stream is Poisson with $\lambda = 8$: $e^{-8} = 3.35\times10^{-4}$.
```

## Key takeaways

- $Y = aX + b$: mean scales and shifts, variance scales by $a^2$; Gaussian stays Gaussian.
- General $g(X)$: CDF method or $f_Y = f_X/|g'|$; $E[g(X)] = \int gf_X$.
- Sums of independent variables convolve PDFs and multiply MGFs; Gaussian + Gaussian is Gaussian with variances adding.
