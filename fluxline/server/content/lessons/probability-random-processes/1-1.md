## Why engineers need probability

Thermal noise in a receiver, bit errors on a link, component tolerances, failures in a power grid, and every machine-learning model are probabilistic. This course gives you the tools to design systems that work *reliably* in an uncertain world.

## The axioms

A **probability space** has a sample space $\Omega$ (all outcomes), events (subsets of $\Omega$), and a probability measure satisfying:

1. $P(A) \ge 0$
2. $P(\Omega) = 1$
3. For mutually exclusive events, $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B)$

Everything else follows, e.g. $P(A^c) = 1 - P(A)$ and $P(A\cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A\cap B)$.

## Counting

For equally likely outcomes, $P(A) = |A|/|\Omega|$, so counting matters:

- Ordered selections of $k$ from $n$ without replacement: $\dfrac{n!}{(n-k)!}$
- Unordered selections: $\dbinom{n}{k} = \dfrac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}$

## Conditional probability and independence

$$
P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}
$$

Events are **independent** if $P(A\cap B) = P(A)P(B)$. The **law of total probability** splits a problem by cases $B_i$ that partition $\Omega$:

$$
P(A) = \sum_i P(A\mid B_i)P(B_i)
$$

## Bayes' rule

$$
P(B \mid A) = \frac{P(A\mid B)\,P(B)}{P(A)}
$$

Bayes' rule is the basis of optimal detection in communications: given a received voltage, which bit was most likely sent?

> **Worked example: manufacturing test.** 1% of chips are defective. A test flags 95% of defective chips, but also falsely flags 2% of good ones. A chip is flagged — what's the probability it's actually defective?
>
> $P(\text{flag}) = 0.95(0.01) + 0.02(0.99) = 0.0095 + 0.0198 = 0.0293$
>
> $P(\text{defective}\mid\text{flag}) = 0.0095/0.0293 \approx 0.324$
>
> Only about one in three flagged chips is bad. When the base rate is low, false positives dominate — a lesson that applies to fault detection, intrusion detection, and medical testing alike.

## Key takeaways

- Three axioms define probability; everything else is derived.
- $P(A\mid B) = P(A\cap B)/P(B)$; Bayes reverses the conditioning.
- Low base rates make false positives dominate.
