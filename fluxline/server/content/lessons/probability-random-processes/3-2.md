## Where does a random signal's power live?

A deterministic signal has a Fourier transform. A random process doesn't (each sample function is different, and none has finite energy), but its **power** still has a well-defined distribution over frequency, the **power spectral density** (PSD). It's what a spectrum analyzer displays when you connect it to noise.

## The Wiener–Khinchin theorem

For a WSS process, the PSD is the Fourier transform of the autocorrelation:

$$
S_X(f) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} R_X(\tau)\,e^{-j2\pi f\tau}\,d\tau, \qquad R_X(\tau) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} S_X(f)\,e^{j2\pi f\tau}\,df
$$

Setting $\tau = 0$ gives the total power as the area under the PSD:

$$
P_X = R_X(0) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} S_X(f)\,df
$$

$S_X(f)$ is real, non-negative and even, in watts per hertz (for a 1 Ω reference). The power in a band is the area over that band, counting **both** the positive and negative frequencies in this two-sided convention.

The time–frequency trade-off of Fourier transforms carries over: a quickly decaying $R_X$ (erratic waveform) means a **wide** PSD, and a slowly decaying one means a narrow PSD.

> **Example.** $R_X(\tau) = e^{-a|\tau|}$ has
> $$
> S_X(f) = \frac{2a}{a^2 + (2\pi f)^2}
> $$
> a lowpass shape, flat up to about $a/2\pi$ Hz and falling at 20 dB/decade beyond. The random-phase sinusoid's $R_X = \frac{A^2}{2}\cos2\pi f_0\tau$ gives two impulses of area $A^2/4$ at $\pm f_0$: all its power at one frequency.

## White noise

The idealized limit is **white noise**: a flat PSD at all frequencies,

$$
S_N(f) = \frac{N_0}{2}, \qquad R_N(\tau) = \frac{N_0}{2}\delta(\tau)
$$

Its values at any two distinct instants are uncorrelated. White noise has infinite total power, so it never exists exactly, but it's an excellent model whenever the noise is flat over a much wider band than the system that receives it. Thermal noise, for example, is flat to about 6 THz at room temperature. Passed through any real filter, white noise becomes finite-power **colored** noise (next lesson).

In a band of width $B$ (from $-B$ to $B$ in two-sided terms), white noise delivers power

$$
P = \frac{N_0}{2} \cdot 2B = N_0B
$$

## Key takeaways

- PSD = Fourier transform of the autocorrelation (Wiener–Khinchin); total power = area under $S_X(f)$.
- Fast-decaying $R_X$ means a wide PSD.
- White noise: $S = N_0/2$, $R = (N_0/2)\delta(\tau)$; power in bandwidth $B$ is $N_0B$.
